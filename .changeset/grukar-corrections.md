**Corrections to the Aelwyth Grukar, and to how the Vardain arrangement actually holds.**

Two things in the previous change were wrong.

**Grukar do not gain knowledge by eating other species.** The inheritance-by-eating that defines them
passes from Grukar to Grukar; eating a Khazár or a human yields sustenance and nothing else. The reason
given for the Khazári hatred rested on that mistake and is removed. The hatred itself stands and is
recorded as what it is — the Deep Folk kill Grukar immediately, wherever found, without negotiation and
without the enormous patience they extend to everything else on the island, and it is the only subject
on which they have ever been observed to act quickly. Competition for underground ground is plainly part
of it and plainly not all of it. The reason has never been given, and the Vardain, who know the Khazári
better than any humans alive, are certain it is older and more particular than territory and have long
since stopped asking.

**The Grukar have been on Aelwyth for nearly seven hundred years**, not within living memory. That
removes the Stormveld theory entirely — they were established four centuries before there was a Jarldom
— and changes what they are. They are not an invasion or a crisis. They are a permanent condition, like
rats in a granary, and cannot be stamped out. Every generation believes the problem is worse than it
used to be and every generation is wrong; it is exactly what it has always been.

The tribal peoples have accordingly been fighting them **without pause for more than five hundred
years** — not a war admitting of campaigns or an end, but a condition that has shaped twenty generations
completely, which is most of what the lowlands are actually looking at when they call these people
barbarians. The civilised realms handle it as routine frontier expenditure: a few steadings a year, a
patrol that does not return. Aldorath has mounted formal campaigns perhaps a dozen times, each declared
a success, and the frontier is precisely where it was. It also means the mainland's ancient talk of fell
creatures in the Aelwythan woods may always have been about them.

**And the Vardain arrangement is symbiotic, not precarious.** The previous draft implied a failed conduit
might go unrepaired. It would not: the Khazári have no more margin than the Vardain, since a failed
conduit means a failed harvest and an empty granary means nobody eats, Deep Folk included. A request for
repair is not a petition to a lord who may feel obliged — it is a report that the hold's own supply is at
risk, and is answered as one. Refusals happen only for a reason and are rare enough that the valleys
remember the occasions.
