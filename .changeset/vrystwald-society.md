**Vrystwald: 500,000, no towns, and a society that explains its own ferocity.**

The population drops from the 1,400,000 of the previous change to **500,000** — and the previous figure
deserves calling out, because it was reverse-engineered to fill a world-model bucket rather than derived
from the ground, which is exactly the error this reference set has been correcting elsewhere. Half a
million across a continental forest is Baltic-tribal density, and it puts a proper gap between a
stateless people and the Nordlands' five crowns.

**No cities and no towns. Villages only** — thousands of them, forest clearings and riverbank landings, a
few score people and a palisade. Some grow larger where the trade is better: a good landing, a portage, a
confluence. They are still villages, because nothing about how they are held together changes with size.
Which is why "nothing to capture" was never a tactic they adopted — they simply never built anything
worth capturing.

**They deal in slaves**, and it would be strange if they did not: they raid, raiding produces captives, a
thousand miles of their southern frontier runs against the Velanthian grain-belt, the rivers reach markets
in every direction, and the best customer in the world is next door — the Nordmen run a thrall economy,
and Stormveld alone works 120,000. Having no cities, the Varokh are a **conduit** rather than a
destination: a village keeps a few and everything else goes down the river, through enough hands that the
eventual buyer rarely knows where the goods entered the system. The Aurèldían kingdoms deplore this and
buy the furs, which travel the same boats.

**And the division of labour is the whole explanation of their reputation.** Slaves do the labour. Men
train for war — not seasonally, not as a levy, but as an occupation, spending on weapons the working life
an Aurèldían peasant gives to the plough, _because there are slaves to do the ploughing_. That is why the
Varokh are fiercer than the Nordmen, and it is not temperament: a Nordman is a farmer who raids in season,
a Varokh warrior is a warrior. The difference is **hours**.

Women do everything else — the traplines and the river trade (so the region's most valuable commodity is
taken, carried and sold by women end to end), and the household, meaning the stores, the stock, the year's
provisioning, and **the slaves**, who answer to the mistress of the house every day about everything.

Which produces a country whose halves know different worlds. A foreigner **trading** in Vrystwald deals
with women exclusively, who have been down the rivers and know what furs fetch in three markets and can
tell when they are being lied to in two languages. A foreigner **fighting** in Vrystwald fights men, who
are the most dangerous people in the north and have mostly never met a foreigner they were not killing.
The people who understand the outside never fight it; the people who fight it understand nothing about it.

The men hold the one thing that decides everything, and the women hold everything else. Neither half has
ever managed without the other, and neither has been foolish enough to test it.
