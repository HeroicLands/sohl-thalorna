**The Kemetían arcane tradition gets its philosophy note: the Khemenu Hekau, the Eight Powers.**

Ta'Kheperu had a stated magical position and nothing implementing it — the empire note commits to
priestly magic, funerary-first, temple libraries closed to outsiders, and there were zero Kemetían
`mysticalability` notes and no arcane philosophy to hang them on. This is the philosophy.

**It is not a variant of the [[lore-hexhodai|Héx Hodäi]] and not mapped onto it.** Both traditions
agree there is one undifferentiated Dýnaris and that all practice consists of reaching into it. They
disagree about where its divisions lie. The Ordo partitions the ocean by **substance** — fire, water,
air, earth, metal, spirit — and holds as doctrine that the Dýnaris is _always already_ one of the Six.
The Kheperi partition the same ocean by **function in the cosmic order**: not what a portion of
reality is made of, but what it is for.

The consequence is the good part. **The two partitions cut across one another**, so no table of
equivalences is possible. A Duat working draws on what a Magister must call Spirit and Earth at once;
a River working spans his Water and his Earth. So an Ordo observer does not see foreign magic — he
sees **impure** magic, several Eídmata braided into one coherent act, which his own doctrine says
cannot be cast. And it works in front of him. That is why the priesthood files Ordo agents under
_arrogant amateurs_, and it gives the Ordo's standing claim a three-thousand-year-old counterexample
with an empire on top of it.

**The eight are not eight equal parts.** Order and Chaos are the frame — the pantheon's own central
tension, order against chaos, within which _"every other deity occupies a position"_ — and the other
six work inside it as **three both-required pairs**, not oppositions:

| Pair                      | Halves                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Aten'ré / Imnetáu**     | The two halves of one circuit; Rā crosses sky by day and the west by night |
| **Ankh'kemet / Deshr'ut** | The Black Land and the Red — the country itself                            |
| **Ren'sesh / Khat'nu**    | The two halves of a being, preserved by inscription and by embalming       |

**Sefút (Chaos) is taught and proscribed**, which is the tradition's hardest feature. It has to be taught —
_you cannot ward what you cannot describe_, and Ta'Kheperu's most valuable magic is protective: the
tomb seals, the threshold curses, the wards in the glyphs, the binding on the cursed road. Rā's and
Thōth's temples already keep the treatises on the chaos god for exactly this. Practising it is
capital, and the line is use rather than knowledge. The consequence is deliberate and uncomfortable:
**the people best equipped to work Chaos are the ones the temples trained to recognise it**, and a
scholar of the treatises never quite loses the shadow.

**The training runs opposite to the Ordo's.** The Ordo channels students into one Eídma early and
accepts lopsidedness because it is fast and easy to examine. A hekau who holds Sun without Duat, or
Form without Name, is **defective rather than specialised** — half a working — so the temple
education is long and general and finishes before ordination. That is also why the empire has no mage
class: _the distinction between priest and mage barely exists_ because the Eight are not a curriculum
a temple offers but the shape it believes reality has.

**Naming.** The practitioners are the **hekau**, and the corpus supplied that: **Wer'Hekau**, the
title of every high priest in the empire, is already glossed _"Great of Sacred Power"_ — the greatest
of them. (Note `Heka'hut`, the estate-lord title, is a separate and unrelated sense already in use.)
The tradition is seated at **Khemenu**, Thōth's archive-city, whose name was unglossed anywhere in
the tree and is now the number: the place of the Eight. That a philosophy of magic is seated in an
archive rather than a sanctum is the point — where power is correct address, the library is the
armoury.

Three practical marks of Kheperi working, all drawn from existing text: it is **installed rather than
cast** (seals and wards that stand for centuries), **transferable through labour** (a correct
inscription works for whoever set it correctly — hence the bribed craftsman as the tomb-robber's
master key), and **error executes rather than fails** (a malformed working does what was actually
written).

Still open: Kheperi-language names for the eight domains, and whether each domain takes an affiliation
plus an `Incantus`-equivalent skill the way the Eídmata do. No incantations are written yet.
