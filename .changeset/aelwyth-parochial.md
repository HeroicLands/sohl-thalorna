**Tarvenne's Vylarian pretension is called what it is, and the parochialism behind it is generalised to
the whole island.**

The earlier draft was too kind — it granted the aspiration as "sincere and in its own terms not absurd."
It is neither. The claim to comparison with Vylaria is **unadulterated vanity and rubbish**, and nobody
with real knowledge of the mainland could sustain the mistake for an afternoon. The forms are copied
without being understood: magistracies bearing Vylarian names attached to offices no Vylarian would
recognise, a processional calendar assembled from hearsay, mantles of the wrong cut and colour, and
Senate oratory in a Vylari that is archaic where it is not simply wrong, delivered in an accent nobody
has corrected because nobody present could. The portico at Thornbury is a portico the way a child's
drawing of a horse is a horse.

What it resembles is not a republic but **children playacting at the behaviour of adults** — with total
seriousness, immense ceremony, and no way of knowing how they look. Vylaria has never sent an embassy
and has no reason to; it is not aware of Tarvenne in any sense an imperial clerk would record. Were one
ever to arrive, its members would die of laughing and take considerable trouble to do it somewhere else.

**And this is the island's condition, not one republic's failing.** Almost nobody leaves Aelwyth: the
mainland is a long dangerous sail, what news arrives is second-hand and years old, and there is
essentially nobody on the island placed to say _that is not how it is done elsewhere_. So every
institution is conducted with total seriousness and no external measure — Aldorath's lords holding court
as though precedence mattered beyond the next valley, Dúnavarre's Synod deliberating as a great power,
Stormveld's jarls reckoning themselves conquerors of consequence. Tarvenne is only the most florid case,
because it built a whole imitation empire out of hearsay and cannot tell how it looks.
