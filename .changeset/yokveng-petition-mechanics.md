**Petitioning the Yokveng: the celestial bureaus fail safe, and these fail dangerous.**

The Yokveng dislike being petitioned, and most petitions sent to them are ignored — which is the
good outcome. What a petition must do is _interest_ them: a request any bureau would have granted
anyway is not worth their notice, while one that opens a gap, embarrasses a bureau, or requires a
classification nobody has dared write is worth a great deal. The sacrifice must be substantial in
proportion, and an underpaid petition that is taken up anyway is answered in the cheapest manner
that satisfies its words.

The difference from the orthodox administration is the direction of failure:

- **A celestial bureau fails safe.** A misaddressed, underpaid, badly drafted or outranked petition
  is simply lost. Nothing happens.
- **The Yokveng fail dangerous.** A petition they take up is granted **exactly as written** — not
  as intended, by beings who take open pleasure in the gap between the two.

This is not caprice but the cosmology working correctly. A thing that receives a classification
becomes that thing, which is how Vëkthōr's forgeries made real what the Great Sorting had
dissolved. A petition asking that a dearly departed daughter be **returned** is therefore granted:
she is returned. It did not ask that she be returned _living_, in her own body, in her right mind,
or that she be permitted to stay. Classified as returned and not as living, she comes back undead —
and the filing is correct.

Hence the trade: an Unlicensed Clerk's fee is high because the drafting _is_ the service, and a
ring's most valuable people are those who know which words a particular Demon Lord will exploit. A
petition to the Yokveng is written the way a contract is written against a hostile counterparty who
will read every clause and honour none of the intent.
