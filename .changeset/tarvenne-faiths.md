**Tarvenne's two great faiths, both of which follow from its corruption rather than sitting beside it.**

The **Black Flame** — suppressed across most of the Aurèldían world and reduced elsewhere to hidden
shrines — carries enormous influence in the republic and barely troubles to hide it. It does not need
to: a faith is suppressed by magistrates, and Tarvenne's magistracies are bought. The Flame buys them
as every other interest does, funds candidates who are known to be funded, and is prosecuted about as
often as the shipping houses are. Its doctrine of purification by fire and the strong over the weak sits
with unpleasant ease in a polity where influence is openly for sale, and the Sacred Forge has been
saying so for fifty years to no effect. Its other western stronghold is across the water at Hárivren,
whose earl holds it openly; whether the two are coordinated is a question the Forge asks loudly and
cannot answer.

**Thánatos** is prominent for exactly the opposite reason. In a republic whose courts work for those who
can pay them to work, the Silent Judge — who judges everyone without exception and cannot be reached,
bought or flattered — is the only judge most Tarvennese will ever face on equal terms with their
betters. His clergy are correspondingly the one institution in the republic with unpurchased moral
authority: they take no part in elections, accept no civic office, and arbitrate the disputes both
parties genuinely want settled rather than won.

So the two strongest religious bodies in Tarvenne are a suppressed cult of domination and a death-cult
of impartial judgement. They both deal in death and mean opposite things by it, they are not allies, and
the republic's politics runs in the space between them — with its magistrates careful to be seen at both
temples.
