**Grukar reach Aelwyth — and the infestation makes sense of three things that were already there.**

They came the way Grukar always come: **fission**. A tribe that outgrows its ground sheds a faction; a
tribe that is starving does something worse, when a Sha sees the end of the line coming and conspires
against its own Ahk. The desperate kind is what crossed. Grukar cannot build a ship, so they were
carried — in holds, on the northern crossing — and the first tribes established themselves within a
decade or two of the conquest of Stormveld. The Jarldom denies bringing them and is believed by nobody,
which is one of very few things Aldorath, Tarvenne and Dúnavarre agree on.

There are perhaps a dozen tribes now, in the empty third of the island, with no single head and no
territory a realm would recognise — growing, splitting and sending fragments outward, which is the part
that ought to frighten the lowlands and mostly does not, because the lowlands have not seen one.

**The tribal peoples are fighting them almost alone.** The Grukar are in the empty country and the empty
country is where the island's tribes live, so the forest communities have been at war for two
generations in a conflict no lowland chronicle records. That reframes them: the lowlands call them
barbarians and treat their fierceness as a defect of character, when it is closer to a symptom. A people
fighting something that breeds has little patience for a border dispute.

It also vindicates part of Aelwyth's lurid reputation. The mainland's talk of fell creatures in the
Aelwythan woods predates the Grukar by centuries and was nonsense when it started — and is not nonsense
now.

**The Khazári enmity is of a different order.** Grukar dig, nest and raise young underground, which puts
them in direct competition for the only ground the Deep Folk value, and bands have reached Vorgald's
outer workings more than once. But the real horror is what Grukar are: **a tribe inherits by eating**, so
one that took a Khazár alive would come away holding some part of what that Khazár knew — the workings,
the water-engineering, the stone that no human has ever been taught. The Khazári kill them on sight,
without negotiation and without the patience they show every other living thing on the island. It is the
only subject on which the Deep Folk have been observed to act quickly.

Also recorded: Aldorath's two campaigns in forty years described as clearing bandits, which produced no
bandits and casualties out of proportion to the reported enemy; and Stormveld's habit of booking its lost
upland steadings as raids by post-conquest holdouts, which is convenient, occasionally accurate, and
increasingly hard to maintain.
