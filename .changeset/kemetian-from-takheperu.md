**Second Kemetían pass: the ladders rebuilt from the Ta'Kheperu material, not just the pantheon
note.**

The first pass used only `Affiliations/Divine/Kemetian/`. That was too narrow — the empire note, the
capital, and the necropolis cities carry most of what actually defines this religion, and two of
those details contradicted what I had written.

**The Sêdjet Hâti.** My pantheon summary said the cults are separate and rivalrous, full stop. They
are, but there is one standing body where their high priests sit together: the **Council of the High
Priests**, the Wer'Hekau of the eight greatest temples, meeting four times a year in the capital.
It governs the **Pér-Háti**, the temple-treasury network — attesting the weight-pieces that circulate
as the empire's currency, holding the granaries that anchor the grain-credit economy, issuing the
temple chits that serve as the region's paper script. Its chief officer, the **Imy'Râ Hâti**
("Overseer of the Account"), is appointed from among the high priests for life. This is a religious
institution in form: _"every senior officer is a priest of one of the great gods."_ So the cults now
carry `economy: [perhati]`.

**Ápōphis has a real cult after all.** I wrote that the secret cults are of disputed existence. The
capital's own note is far more specific: the forbidden cults are hunted without rest by the Medjay
and the **temple-inquisitors**, most of what they find is harmless desperation — _"but the one the
priesthood of Rā fears in its bones is the small, hidden sect that does not merely defy the order of
the dead but means to break it"_: the cult that would **wake the False Rā**. That the sect exists at
all is a secret the temple keeps, and it is why the kill-on-sight order on the cursed road has never
been relaxed. The office is now **The Sect of the Waking**, and it is the sharpest hook in the
pantheon.

**The shared ladder gains what the empire note supplies:**

- **Wab** — the purification the rank is named for: strict dietary, behavioural and hygiene codes
  marking them apart from ordinary life.
- **Hem'Netjer** — a wide seniority band, veterans commanding temple districts and advising nomarchs,
  and five recognised specialisations (ritual, administration, medicine, scholarship, legal
  arbitration).
- **Wer'Hekau** — not hereditary, elevated from the senior Hem'Netjer by temple politics, capability
  and _"the priesthood insists"_ divine revelation; as **High Ritualist** conducts the seasonal rites
  **and the divinations that guide state policy**; alone with the Per-Aá may enter the inner
  sanctuary.
- **Per-Aá** — the polite fiction nobody believes and everyone maintains, and the two-way threat: a
  Per-Aá has doctrinal grounds to replace any Wer'Hekau (_"Few have tried. Fewer have succeeded."_),
  while the Wer'Hekau of Rā _"have deposed or elevated more Per-Aás than any army."_

The mortuary offices are the ones with teeth. A tomb's real defence is its **endowment** — where a
cult dwindles or a line is forgotten, decay creeps in, and those are exactly the reaches where the
robbers work. Below ground the **Binder** sets the shabti-warriors and revenant-wardens, and the
robbers' surest master-key is a corrupt priest who knows which glyph is live and which is bluff.

Hâpi's **Keeper of the Granaries** gains the dole: given to temple-dependents, the flood-season
construction gangs and the registered poor, and _"less charity than statecraft — a hungry Wasetkara
is a dangerous one."_

Seats are now set where the notes name them: Rā and the pantheon at **Iat-Tepy**, the Sacred Island;
Thōth at **Khemenu**; Ánubís and Sēker at **Khensuret**, the great pilgrimage necropolis.
