**Aelwyth gets its internal geography: twelve new regions and features, and an island map that explains
its politics.**

**Stormveld** divides into three. The **Stormplain** is the great central plain holding four-fifths of
the Jarldom's people and nearly all its farmland; the **Nordfells** wall it to the north and are half
Stormveld's ground with almost none of its population, their inlets giving Nordmen a way in and thralls
a way out; and the **Sunderfells** are its southern march.

**The Ironfells** are the island's spine — rising on Stormveld's southern border, running east to the sea
and then south down the coast into the foothills above Dúnavarre. **Only two passes cross them**, both
through deep wilderness and holdable at the top by a fraction of the force attempting them, which is why
the Jarldom has never pushed south, Aldorath has never retaken the north-east, and sixty years of enmity
have produced skirmishes at the pass-heads and nothing else. In their eastern heights is **Vorgald**, a
hold of the Khazári: known to exist, effectively unreachable, and holding no relationship with anyone.
Aldorath has sent three expeditions; one found a doorway that would not open and could not be marked in
any way still there the next season.

**Aldorath** divides into five. The **Aldorvale** is the breadbasket that feeds the island — Dúnavarre
buys grain there every year, Tarvenne in bad ones, and even Stormveld has bought over the passes through
intermediaries at prices nobody enjoyed. **Brynwold** runs the flocks whose clip is one of only two
Aelwythan goods with a mainland reputation. The **Wyndmarch** watches the passes and shades into forest
nobody governs. The **Greylevels** carry the whole kingdom's trade out through Greywater. And
**Sunnerslade** grows vines, which on a cool wet island should be impossible — it works because the
Ironfells take the rain out of the westerlies and break the wind, leaving a band a few miles deep that
behaves like somewhere hundreds of miles south. A cold summer costs the vintage outright about one year
in five, and that precariousness is exactly why the wine is worth carrying to the mainland.

**The Central Highlands** are Tarvenne's inland half and the pleasantest ground on Aelwyth, because
coastal fog is a low thing and the upland rises out of it — a traveller climbing from the coast road
breaks into sunlight within the hour and looks back on a white sea. All strictly relative: a Provenzian
told this was the island's finest weather would put his cloak back on and say something polite.

**The Hallowwood** is the fae forest of the south where Aelwyth's Sinalë live, and it closes the loop on
the Synod. Dúnavarre is _tolerated_ there; Élavendre is welcome; the Áelendan most of all. So the island
that is the Ordo's unconstrained research ground precisely because its fae country has no Wardens turns
out to have its greatest concentration of fae presence in the one place that does have a keeper — one
that does not answer to Dúnavarre and receives the Synod's envoys the way one tolerates weather.

Climate is recorded island-wide: cool, wet and windy, with the western coast fog-bound and chilly as its
ordinary condition. Every settlement is reparented into its new subregion; all 25 Aelwyth place notes now
have bodies and parents.
