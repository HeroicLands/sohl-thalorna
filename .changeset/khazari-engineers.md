**Correcting who maintains the Khazári works, and what the Vardain actually know.**

The earlier draft had the Vardain maintaining the water-works and granaries, some of it by rote. That
was wrong on both counts.

**The Khazári maintain their own works.** Their engineers come down to do it — dredging channels,
clearing ventilation shafts, re-facing terrace walls, adjusting gates, opening and closing the flues by
season — arriving when the work requires it rather than when anyone asks, and going back up when they
are done. This suits both sides exactly: engineering is the Khazári trade in the way farming is not,
they take evident satisfaction in it, and they are deeply unwilling to teach any of it to a human.
Maintaining a valley's water system costs them nothing they mind spending and keeps the knowledge where
they want it. What they will not do is the sowing, herding and slaughtering the arrangement exists for.
They would far rather cut a mile of conduit than keep a single cow.

**The Vardain use the works, expertly, and understand only their operation.** They know the water
schedule to the hour and which gate serves which terrace, how to load a granary and read whether it is
keeping, when a flue should be open and when shut. They cannot repair any of it, and they have no real
idea why any of it works — they were given the operation, never the principles. A Vardain can tell you
exactly what to do with a conduit and not why it runs where it does, why it is that depth, or what would
follow if it did not.

When something breaks they do not attempt it. They report it up the mountain through Barakth and wait,
and an engineer comes down in a day or a week. That dependence is total, and the arithmetic behind it —
ten thousand people, six thousand feet, four months of season, one failed conduit in a year nobody came
— is not one the valleys like to do aloud.

It also makes the Vardain the only humans anywhere for whom a Khazár is an **ordinary sight**. Children
in these valleys grow up seeing Deep Folk at work in the channels, and are raised knowing not to
interrupt, not to follow, and not to ask what anything is for.
