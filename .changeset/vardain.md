**The Vardain: a nation reduced to ten thousand, and the reason the Khazári of Vorgald deal with humans
at all.**

Aelwyth was once ruled by both elder races together — a single kingdom under a **Sinalë king**, whose
works are still in the ground. It ended in a war against barbarian hordes: the king was killed, the
surviving Sinalë **withdrew into the forest** that is now the Hallowwood, and left the Khazári to finish
the fighting alone. The Khazári have never forgiven it, and the two elder races of Aelwyth have had
nothing to do with one another since. Whether this is the great sundering or only its Aelwythan chapter,
nobody will say.

What the Khazári did instead was turn to the humans of the plain below. **Vardanreach** — the Vardain
nation — held the whole north-east, something like a fifth of the island, and kept the Deep Folk's
friendship for thousands of years. It was the only relationship of its kind in the world.

The Nordmen conquest sixty years ago destroyed it. The lowlands were devastated and the people enslaved;
**a hundred and twenty thousand Vardain are thralls in Stormveld today**, working ground their families
held free within living memory, and the conquerors renamed the country. What reached the high valleys was
a remnant of a remnant: **ten thousand people**, all that is left free of a nation that held a fifth of
Aelwyth.

They are, in plain terms, **asylum seekers in the Khazári kingdom** — the Deep Folk admitted them, which
was neither obliged nor explained and is the largest thing they have ever done for humans. But asylum is
not right. The Vardain hold their freedom at the pleasure of a lord who has never stated the terms, and
that is why they have never taken their kin's part against Stormveld: they cannot spend a protection that
is not theirs to spend.

The valleys are a **broad upland basin** — wide grassy floor, scattered oak, ringed by higher peaks that
take the western weather — which is why ten thousand can live there. Nine in ten farm: oats, barley and
rye, sheep and hardy black cattle. New notes for the region and for **Vargate** (5,000) plus eight
villages: **Barakth** the tally-keepers, **Vellick** the smiths taught the shallow end of Khazári
technique and forbidden to teach it onward, **Stonyhaugh** the masons, **Kalm** the grain valley that
took the refugees, **Highfold** whose cheese the Khazári ask for by name, **Coldbeck** the mills,
**Dorrin** the watch, and **Threndale**, the burial valley whose _new ground_ is still being added to
because people still come over the mountains.

Vargate is the end of the caravan road. Caravans come up two or three times a year and stop; **none has
ever been allowed into the hold**, there is no fee that buys it, and no outsider has been above the
Weighing Yard.
