**It was never concealment. It is the Khazári aesthetic: the wonder is seen from within, not from
without.**

The previous change read the disguised towers as hiding, and that was wrong — there was nobody to hide
from in 3800 BF, and the Deep Folk have never behaved as though they thought otherwise.

Their works are routinely indistinguishable from the natural environment because a Khazári work is
finished to **belong to the ground it stands in**. The towers were not screened or camouflaged; they were
cut and finished as rock — bedded, weathered, faulted, jointed, stained, carrying the cliff's own strata —
because that is simply what a well-made thing looks like from outside. The exterior is not a surface for
display. It is the mountain, and it should look like the mountain.

Everything they have to say is said inside.

Which makes Khazártúrn one principle stated twice. A people who finish the outside of their greatest work
so it cannot be told from a cliff, and then spend a thousand years raising vaults hundreds of feet high
and painting the ceilings as open sky, are not being secretive and are not being modest — they are being
consistent. There is no version of a Khazári building whose point is visible from the valley floor.

Humans build to be seen, read this as hiding, and cannot easily be talked out of it. It has made the Deep
Folk seem far more furtive than they are, and they have never thought the misunderstanding worth
correcting. The principle is now stated in the Khazári folk note as well, since it governs everything
they make.

One distinction the note keeps: **their silence about Khazártúrn's location is deliberate defence** — no
marker, no monument, no name on any map they have let out of their hands. The invisibility of the place
itself is not. The valley looked like a valley from the day the first tower was finished. They have simply
understood the value of that since 2800 BF, and guard the knowledge accordingly.
