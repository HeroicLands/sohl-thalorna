**The Synod is the one thing on Aelwyth that is not parochial — and the island's isolation turns out to
have a beneficiary.**

Exactly one body on Aelwyth knows how the island compares with the mainland: the **Synod** of Ordo
Arcanis mages who govern Dúnavarre. They are members of a continental institution, they read its
correspondence, some trained in the Empire, and they know to the nearest order of magnitude how small
Aelwyth is and what a Vylarian would make of Tarvenne's Senate. They have never said so to anybody.

That reframes why the Ordo governs a kingdom here at all — the only place in the world where it does. The
usual explanations are Dúnavarren and explain only the form. The substance is the rest of the island:
**Aelwyth is where the Ordo can study the fae without constraint.** In Élavendre, far richer fae country,
it is permitted and limited, fenced out of the sacred sites by Wardens the crown will not overrule.
Aelwyth has real fae sites and no equivalent institution — the country they lie in belongs to scattered
tribes with no common council, no wardens and no means of compelling the lowland realms to care. Dúnavarre
is therefore not an anomaly in the Ordo's history but its **laboratory**, and the Synod governs a kingdom
chiefly because that is the price of unrestricted access to an island.

The Synod does also **protect** Aelwyth, sincerely, and the other realms know it and are grateful in a
resentful sort of way. But protection and possession are hard to distinguish from outside, and the
Synod's interests run all one way: an isolated, disregarded, slightly frightening island is one nobody
else visits. The parochialism keeps its people incurious, the dreadful reputation keeps the mainland
away, and the tribes' disunity keeps the fae country open — and the Synod has never lifted a finger
against any of the three.

Whether it has done more than not lift a finger, and quietly encouraged the reputation rather than merely
finding it convenient, is a question nobody on Aelwyth is equipped to ask. The Synod has never denied a
story about the island in its life.
