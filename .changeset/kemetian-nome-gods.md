**The nome gods, the Set-Maat brotherhood, and Per'Nuw's two hunt-patrons — the last of the
Ta'Kheperu material.**

**Six gods stand outside the Twelve, and none of them had a note.** The empire note says each nome has
its own patron deity and that local cults give the religion _"extraordinary regional variety beneath
the surface of theological orthodoxy"_ — and the nome-class tables bear that out: across the
thirty-eight rostered nomes, six patrons are gods the pantheon's `## The Twelve` never lists. The
tell was in the markup. The Twelve are written as wikilinks to the pantheon; these six are plain
text, deliberately marked as having nowhere to point.

| God        | Domain                         | Standing                                                          |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mentu**  | War                            | Patron of four of the ten border nomes; the war-temple at Behen   |
| **Sobek**  | River-beasts                   | Three nomes; the great crocodile cult of the eastern marsh        |
| **Wadjit** | The marsh                      | The delta papyrus nome, whose reed-beds are the empire's paper    |
| **Min**    | Fertility and the desert roads | Caravan-head of the eastern routes; harvest and virility cults    |
| **Anhur**  | The hunt                       | Religious foundation of Per'Nuw, paired with Shesmu               |
| **Shesmu** | The hunt                       | Paired with Anhur; neither temple's rite counts without the other |

Each gets an affiliation on the shared Kheperi temple ladder, a `Ritual:` skill, and a draft deity
lore note — nineteen new notes in all — plus a new `## The Nome Gods` section in the pantheon that
ties them in.

**Mentu** is the clearest case of a cult whose strength is geographic rather than doctrinal: strongest
exactly where the empire's writ is thinnest, and _"rare in the gentler valley."_ **Sobek** carries the
sharpest tension in the pantheon — the crocodile is sacred to the god and is also the beast that takes
farmers and children along the river, so the office is **Sanctioner of the Cull**: killing it is a
religious act before it is a commercial one.

**Per'Nuw's hunt-patrons were four of the eighty-nine link problems.** The guild note reaches for
`[[Faith_of_Anhur]]` and `[[Faith_of_Shesmu]]` in two places each — malformed addresses pointing at
notes that did not exist. Creating the two cults and repairing the links takes link problems from
**89 to 85** and compile errors from **97 to 95**. The pairing is the interesting part: the guild's
senior elevations are _"rites at both temples, performed in tandem"_, so neither counts alone, and
their patronage is understood to cover not just the hunt's success but the hunter's conduct — which is
the religious weight behind Per'Nuw's principle of the Hunter's Respect.

**The Brotherhood of Set-Maat** now exists as an affiliation (`setmaatbr`) rather than only as a
village. It is a closed hereditary brotherhood of stonecutters, draughtsmen, sculptors and painters
on a six-rank ladder from Village-Born to Headman, governing itself through its own headmen, courts
and shrines. Its defining office is **Keeper of the Locations**: the brotherhood's real secret is not
how the tombs are cut but where they are, which makes the village _"at once the most trusted and the
most watched community in the nome"_ — and makes a bribed craftsman the tomb-robbers' surest
master-key.

**Patron deities are wired** on the eight nomes that have their own notes: Rā to the Capital Nome,
Mentu to Ta-Resu-Heka, Ptā'h to Ta-Set-Iabtet, Hápi to Per-Ankhet, Íšét to Sau-Neferu, Má'át to
Ankh-Setûn, Sēker and Ánubís to Khensuret, Thōth to Per-Thōth.

**One fix to my own earlier work:** I wrote `economy: [perhati]` on ten Kemetían notes. The corpus
addresses that target as `affiliation-perhati` in all sixteen pre-existing uses; my ten bare ones were
the anomaly, and are now normalised.
