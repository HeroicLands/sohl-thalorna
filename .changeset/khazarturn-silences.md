**Who says what about Khazártúrn — and the treasure question is reopened, deliberately.**

The previous change closed off what is in the sealed city ("nothing to recover, no hoard, no relic").
That is removed. **Nobody knows, and nobody is going to find out.**

**The Khazári never speak of it.** Not to humans, not to the Vardain who have lived beneath a hold for
millennia, not much among themselves outside the reading of the account. But silence is not denial, and
the distinction is theirs: to disown the city would be to disown the people in it, an affront to their own
ancestors that no Khazár will commit merely to end a conversation. Pressed, one declines. None has ever
said it did not happen.

Everything else is open, and stays open. **Where it stands:** no comment, and no name on any map they have
let out of their hands. **What is still in it:** no comment — whether the wealth of a people lies in those
halls, whether everything that mattered was carried out before the last block went in, or whether there is
something in there worth more than either. **Whether anyone goes back:** no comment — whether the tomb has
been tended once a century since the day it closed, or never entered again at all.

**So every description in the human world comes from the Sinalë**, and comes as story at three and four
removes. They saw the city while it lived, and what they let out is elegy: the halls, the light, the
fountains, the birds — in snippets, in asides, in songs whose subject is not announced.

They say exactly two things. That the city was wondrous. And that it was destroyed by a horde of Grukar
compelled by a renegade.

**They do not say the renegade was one of their own.** The word stands alone with no people attached to
it, in every telling, without exception — which is not carelessness in a race with memory like theirs.
Pressed, a Sinalë does not deny it; they stop, and the stopping is unmistakable, and everyone who has
pushed that far came away certain and without having been told. So the fact is not known but **inferred**
— corroborated by the other silence beside it, which is that the Deep Folk have not spoken to the Sinalë
about anything in four and a half thousand years and will not say why.

The rest is closed on both sides: the renegade's name, never given; what became of him, never described;
what drove him, never offered; where the Second Voice came from, nothing whatever.

Which leaves a strange shape to what the world knows — vivid, lyrical, minutely detailed descriptions of a
city nobody alive has seen, and almost nothing about the atrocity that ended it. The beauty survived the
telling. The crime was edited out by the only people who could have told it.
