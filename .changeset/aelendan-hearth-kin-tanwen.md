**Tanwen, the Hearth of Eshálosha — the Áelendan hearth-kin exemplar, and the last of the five
kinds to be named.**

- **[[lore-tanwenspr|Tanwen]]** is the hearth-kin of the great ceremonial hall at Eshálosha and the
  oldest continuously tended fire in Áelendan country, with a Spirit Power of her own. She is the
  only named Kindred met **indoors**, and the only one every Áelendan meets, since every tribe
  comes to the Lodge to gather.
- Her domain is **guest-right**: no weapon carried past her fire, no oath sworn at it and broken,
  no guest received and then harmed. This explains something already written — Eshálosha's
  hospitality protocols say a violation "even inadvertent" ends a visit and is _not forgiven_,
  which read as Áelendan severity. It is not their call. They are not the offended party and have
  no standing to forgive on her behalf.

Hearth-kin also get the doctrine that distinguishes them from every other Kindred: **they depend on
people.** A river-mother holds her water whether or not anyone comes; a hearth-kin comes into being
where people keep returning and thins where they stop. So Áelendan settlement and Áelendan spirits
are the same subject —

- a permanent hall holds a hearth-kin of long standing;
- its **satellites** — seasonal hunting camps, fishing stands, summer pasture, the small shrines
  raised for a purpose and let go — hold a thin one that must be **woken on arrival**, or none;
- a night's camp holds none, and where none can be raised the Áelendan **carry fire**, an ember
  from a permanent hearth, so the camp burns under a hearth-kin that is elsewhere;
- a new village's fire is kindled from a carried ember rather than struck fresh, and where the
  ember came from Eshálosha the new hall's hearth-kin is held to be Tanwen's own.

Where two friendly villages share a satellite, whoever arrives first wakes the camp and the second
party is sitting at a hearth not theirs — guest-right in miniature, a long way from any hall.
