**The petition is a craft, and the priesthood's monopoly on it is explained.**

Three things stand between an ordinary Tānvüri and a filed petition, and the Celestial Order now
says what they are.

- **The script.** Petitions are written in [Tëng Thōk](skill-tngthkscrpt), the Celestial Record —
  logographic, three to four thousand characters for educated reading, ten to fifteen years for a
  full education. A villager cannot write the request, before any question of protocol arises.
- **The forms.** Opening formulae, addressing conventions, order of citation and the seals each
  bureau requires are held by the clergy and not taught outside the temple — the Tānthëi
  guardianship of sacred knowledge. An outsider who obtains them otherwise is refused whether or
  not the petition was correct.
- **Beauty**, which is not decoration. A correct petition is processed; a beautiful one is read
  with attention, and the difference shows in the outcome. At the low end that is a clean hand and
  a well-proportioned column; at the high end the document is an artwork — illuminated borders,
  landscape scenes of the country concerned, creatures appropriate to the bureau addressed, and the
  divine symbolism of the office petitioned. The greatest petitions are paintings that happen to
  carry text, and copies of them are studied by clerks the way jurists study rulings.

Priests charge for all of it at a price of their own setting, and the Tānvüri do not call this
corrupt: the years are real, the skill is real, a badly made petition is genuinely lost, and it is
also — as everyone understands — a monopoly.

`commonSkills` now name what the work actually requires: **Tëng Thōk Script**, **Drawing** and
**Law**. Two offices are added — the **Master of Forms**, who does not teach outside the temple,
and the **Illuminator**, who paints what a weighty petition carries; Rëkgüng's illuminator paints
the parched field and the swollen river, which the bureau is held to read as evidence.
