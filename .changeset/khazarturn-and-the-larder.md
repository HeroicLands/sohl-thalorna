**The fall of Khazártúrn, the appearance of the Grukar, and why human agriculture exists at all.**

**The Grukar appear around 3000 BF.** They had no origin date before this; now they have one, and it is
recent enough to matter. The Khazári archive at Khazártúrn — the finest in the world at that date — does
not mention them in its first seven centuries, and then within a generation mentions a great deal. Where
they came from the account does not speculate. Human scholarship on the point is worth nothing, human
records of the period being two and a half thousand years in the future.

**Khazártúrn** was raised around 3800 BF and stood a thousand years: not a hold among holds but the work
all their other work was measured against. The Khazári had two centuries to take the measure of the
Grukar and got one thing wrong — the only thing that counted. A tribe does not tire, lose heart, need
paying or die; it _replaces_, and its diggers hatch knowing how to dig, so **time, which defeats every
siege, costs a Grukar tribe nothing**. A Sha spent the better part of a Khazári generation beneath the
most closely kept city in the world, mapping its ventilation and its water by sound.

Then two acts on one day: the outer galleries, where the young were schooled and the craft-records
copied — and the cisterns, fouled from below, which was not discovered for eleven days.

They did not kill the ones they took. They kept them alive on the gallery floor before the great inner
doors, where the city could see, and made it last, because the Sha had understood exactly what a Khazár
will not do. **The doors stayed shut.** It was correct. It bought nothing: the water had been poisoned
eleven days earlier, and the survivors opened the doors anyway, onto a gallery holding only what the
Grukar had left. Khazártúrn was not sacked but **emptied**, and no Khazár has entered it since.

The remnant wrote it down — every name, the exact sequence of the eleven days — and **every hold keeps
that record**, in the working archive beside the craft-records, and reads it. So a Khazár of Vorgald who
has never seen a Grukar kills one on sight without deliberation. It is not grievance and not passion. It
is a conclusion, reached by people who wrote down their reasons and never revisited them.

**And the larder.** Two contradictions surfaced while writing this and are now fixed. The Khazári note
claimed no Élavendren-style exceptions and no sustained human relationship — but the Vardain are a
closer and older one than Élavendre's with the Sinalë. The continent note claimed both Elder Races
observed strict non-interference — true of the Sinalë, and not of the Khazári.

Both resolve the same way. **A hold grows nothing and will not be supplied from outside**, so every hold
has always needed farmers at its gate — and when the Deep Folk arrived around 4280 BF there were none
anywhere, humanity being hunter-gatherers who cannot provision anybody. So in the uplands around their
first holds they taught the nearest peoples to farm: water-works, drainage, terracing, the granary.

It was not a gift and not an uplift. They were building a larder, and would have done the same for
anything that could be taught to hold a plough. The knowledge ran downhill on its own into the river
valleys, where it exploded into Ta'Kheperu and Tānvür — civilizations raised on a foundation nobody
remembers being handed them, by people who never went near the valleys and took no further interest.

Every such arrangement has since lapsed but one. The **Vardain** are the last people on Thalorna still
doing what humans were originally taught to do, and asked about it they will say their people have
always farmed for the lords of the mountain — which is true, and rather more literally true than they
know.
