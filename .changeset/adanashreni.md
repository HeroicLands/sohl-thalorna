**Vedyara's thieves' guild stops being "the Thieves' Guild of Vedyara" and becomes the Ādānaśreṇī.**

The old name was the one thing the setting's own _Crime Syndicates of Thalorna_ note explicitly says
Thalorna does not have — a monolithic, generically-named thieves' guild — and Vedyara was missing from
that note's roster entirely.

The replacement takes the region at its word as a pre-colonial, medieval Indic subcontinent, so the
organisation is built in a period-correct form: a **śreṇī**, a chartered craft guild with its own
court, treasury and hereditary craft-lines, holding lawful charters in some forty kingdoms. That makes
it categorically unlike the five syndicates — it is a caste rather than an enterprise, so it cannot be
broken by taking a city or killing a boss, and it has outlived every dynasty that tried.

Its distinguishing features: a doctrine of _ādāna_ ("receiving" and "seizing" at once) that frames
theft as relieving an owner of attachment; more income from **underwriting** theft than committing it,
which entangles it with Vedyaran banking and the Merchant Collective; taboos that read as piety and
are really charter-protection; and the unchartered thief as its most implacable enemy, since handing
those over is the service that makes a charter worth granting.

Also adds the schism it is at war with — the **Chinnasūtra**, who reject the prohibition on killing —
which honours rather than contradicts Chandrakīrtisundarī Jayavallī's existing assassination-commission
hook, and gives the guild a reason to hire outsiders it cannot be seen hiring.

Every reference to the old name is repointed (Twilight House, Merchant Collective, Chandrakīrtisundarī,
Chrysantos), and Vedyara now appears in the syndicate overview. The **Umbral Court** is deliberately
untouched — it is a Dunavarran body inside the Embassy adventure and has nothing to do with Vedyara, so
only its misleading "(Thieves' Guild)" gloss was decoupled.
