**The Āsháian faiths take the fire-priesthood, which is one priesthood for all twelve gods.**

All thirteen notes carried the generic ladder, while the pantheon note documents the real one in a
`## Temple and Priesthood Structure` section and Ātáröš's states the doctrinal reason: _"The
priesthood dedicated to Ātáröš forms the core structure of all Āsháian religious organization. Every
fire-temple, regardless of which specific deity is its primary focus, maintains an altar dedicated
to Ātáröš and priests trained in the sacred rituals of fire-kindling."_

So unlike the Aurèldían Twelve — twelve separate priesthoods — the Āsháians have **one**, and every
faith takes the same ladder: **Āthravān** (Fire-Keeper) → **Hērbad** (Teacher-Priest) → **Mōbad**
(Master of Fire). Ordination from Āthravān requires having maintained a portion of the temple fire
for a full year without its failing.

Level 0 is **Flame-Denied**, and it bites harder here than excommunication does elsewhere: in a faith
where every prayer to every god begins with kindling a fire, being barred from the altar ends
religious life outright. Level 1 is the lay faithful, who keep the household fire-ritual at sunrise
and sunset without a priest.

Offices are the practice the notes describe — the **Flame-Reader**, who takes colour for the quality
of an offering, flicker for the god's mood and the ash-patterns on the altar stones as messages; the
**Keeper of the Fire-Lineage**, holding the record of which fire a flame was kindled from, on which a
temple's standing rests; and the **Caravan Priest**, a Hērbad who travels with the caravans and
ministers beyond any temple's reach.

The unity is doctrinally motivated rather than administrative: it holds _"not by hierarchical
authority but by the objective reality of the flames themselves"_ — a Hērbad in Dunhara uses
identical procedures to one in Amradad because fire answers to correct technique, not to sectarian
innovation.

Affiliations still on the generic ladder fall from 43 to 30.
