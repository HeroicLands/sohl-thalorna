**The Élavendren thin places are named Arálwen, and the concept gets a note.**

They had been carrying a name imported from outside this setting, which did not belong in it. It
existed as thirteen prose mentions in one adventure, with no note, no shortcode and nothing
addressing it, so retiring it cost nothing.

**Arálwen** is built to sit in the Áelendan register rather than beside it. The root is _arallwyn_,
"the other place"; the mutation into Áelendan follows features the language already shows — the
doubled _l_ simplifies, since no Áelendan name in the tree carries `-ll-`; `-wyn` fronts to `-wen`,
as in Tanwen and Gwalchen; and the acute marks the stress, as in Béravel and Élavendre. The plural is
**arálwain**.

**The concept now has an address** (`lore-aralwen`, subType `arcana`) instead of living as prose in a
single adventure. An arálwen is a place where the material and spirit worlds lie thin enough to
cross: arcane power runs strongest there, the fae are met there, and what people report on returning
is as often about time, distance and memory as about anything a sword would help with.

**Arálwen-water** covers the rivers that run partly in each world, whose two courses are not the same
river — the banks do not match, and neither does the distance between two points. These are reckoned
the strongest of all, on the reasoning that a place is a doorway that stays where it is put, while a
river is a road with one end in each world.

It also gives the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] something to point at. Their note
described the job — guarding the sacred sites, answering to the Kindred Pact, killing Ordo surveyors
who ignore warnings — without the thing they guard having a name.

Úqua-Arálwen keeps its qualifier. The At'aan Wyrd is related but distinct: not an arálwen itself, but
a Kindred that holds one.
