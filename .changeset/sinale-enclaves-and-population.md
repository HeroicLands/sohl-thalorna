**Three Sinalë enclaves, two seated sites written out, every parent audited, and the population model
reconciled from the top down.**

The **[[Sinalë]] enclaves** of Élavendre, each doing a different job. **Aelirossë** in the Silver
Forests is the closest Sinalë and humans live anywhere in Thalorna — daily neighbourhood with the
Áelendan, and where most of the songs the Bardic Colleges treasure were first heard. **Serenthalë** in
Glanmor's deep western forest is the oldest and most withdrawn; the Wardens who patrol its approaches
do not know where it is, and have never asked. **Ethalossë** sits at the tribal lands' eastern margin
and is the only enclave positioned for access rather than seclusion, because it is where the Sinalë
conduct business with the world.

That last carries the arrangement you described: the Sinalë will not treat with human powers, they will
deal with the **Áelendan**, and the Áelendan carry. Two properties matter — the Áelendan _carry, they
do not represent_, and no elder would embellish; and it makes them indispensable, since the crown's
celebrated centuries-long relationship with the Sinalë is in practice a relationship with intermediaries.
The crown does not advertise this, because it means the relationship is not the monarchy's to possess
but the tribes' to grant.

**The Owl Hollow** and **The Long Stair** are written out from their spirits. Nyfain listens at night
and remembers, so things are brought to the Hollow _to be said_ — an oath sworn there after dark is
witnessed by something that will not forget — and Death-Speakers come from several tribes to ask whether
the recently dead have gone, which the nearest tribe permits and refuses to charge for. Aranwy is
_appeased_ rather than venerated: the offering is made at the lowest pool at first light, in silence,
and must be something the giver made and would rather keep, which supports a small craft-economy of
things made slowly by people who intend to give them away. And her first prohibition forbids climbing
**by daylight** — wording the Áelendan have had a long time to notice. Nobody has asked her about it,
because asking would require climbing.

**Parents audited.** All 59 place notes across Provènzia and Élavendre now point at the most specific
region containing them; two were corrected (the Blood-field of Tiravlen to Lake Tiravel, Silvain to the
Silver Forests). The Drenavar Spine also gained the description and tags it had never carried.

**Population reconciled.** The world model carried the Aurèldían kingdoms as one ~35,000,000 line, which
could not survive worked regional figures: reaching it would have needed Tarvénia alone to hold ~21
million, as many as the whole Vylarian Empire. The bloc is now itemized — Provènzia 5M, Tarvénia 5M
(the two are about equal), Élavendre 2M, Aelwyth 500,000, Calypsa 45,000 — totalling ~12.5M.

Aelwyth's realms were the worst offenders, summing to 7.6 million on an island of half a million;
Dúnavarre alone was recorded at 3.5M. They are rescaled to fit — Aldorath 120,000, Tarvenne 105,000,
Stormveld 100,000, Dúnavarre 75,000 — with 100,000 (a fifth) as the scattered barbarian tribes, and the
island note now explains the emptiness: a third of Aelwyth is dense forest, and much of the rest is
upland and bog, which is why its polities have never absorbed one another.

Western Ankaris falls from ~97M to ~74.5M and the world from ~392M to **~370M**.

Dúnavarre is worked out further: **75,000**, making it the _smallest_ of Aelwyth's settled realms and
still the most powerful, since its weight comes from harbours, trade, the Synod and the Just Blade
rather than numbers — Aldorath has half again as many subjects and rather less influence. It holds two
cities, Ravenmoor at ~20,000 and a new note for **Wraymouth**, the chief harbour, at ~15,000, with the
remaining forty thousand on manorial estates administered for the Synod by appointed stewards rather
than held by a landed aristocracy. Aelwyth's settlement is banded — western coast, north-eastern coast,
eastern interior, plus Dúnavarre in the south-east — with a third of the island under dense forest and
much of the rest upland and bog, which is why its polities have never absorbed one another.
