**What Khazártúrn was like, and the light that still falls into it.**

The greatest structure ever built on Thalorna, and — the part that is hardest to picture — not a warren.
Its entire design was an argument against being underground: every choice in it made to produce the
feeling of standing **outside**, in open air, under weather. Bright, airy and magnificent, in the middle
of a mountain, on purpose.

Great halls hundreds of feet from floor to vault. **Skylights** cut through the mountain dropping real
daylight into the interior in shafts that moved across the floors as the sun went over. Stone finished
until it read as something else — _worked like spun glass_, which is not decoration but a description of
what the masons were attempting. Colonnades the length of the great ways, galleries above galleries, open
courts. **Fountains in the main thoroughfares** — running water inside a mountain, because they could. And
**market circles roofed with painted ceilings done as open sky**, so that a Khazár doing the day's
business stood under weather someone had put there deliberately.

The Deep Folk are not a people given to display. Khazártúrn was the exception and was always understood
to be: the thing they made when they were doing their best.

Which is what makes the sealing land. It is all still there, exactly as it was, cleaned and closed and
full of its dead. The colonnades, the galleries, the painted skies. The fountains, fed by works nobody has
turned off. And the skylights — which were cut through a mountain and cannot be sealed without unmaking
the halls beneath them, so **the daylight still comes down, every day, in shafts that move across the
floors as the sun goes over, into courts where nobody is standing.**

No human has ever seen any of it. Khazártúrn fell five hundred years before the earliest human dynasty,
and every description comes from Khazári records, quoted rarely and to very few.
