**The Second Voice: what destroyed Khazártúrn, why the elder races stopped speaking, and the most
dangerous object on Thalorna, currently missing.**

The previous account had a Grukar tribe destroying a city of thirty thousand on its own initiative. That
could not stand, because it contradicted the constraint the species is built on: **Ahks do not
cooperate.** They cannot combine, which is exactly why Grukar are a permanent frontier nuisance and have
never in their history been a conquest.

So the constraint was removed by something else. The **Second Voice** does not ally Ahks — it speaks over
them, taking the hatched directly and displacing the spawner's claim, so that Grukar of any number of
tribes answer one will. Not an alliance: a single mind operating tens of thousands of bodies. What it
does to the tribes is worse than what it does to their victims, since an Ahk cannot follow its own spawn
and does not stop existing afterward.

In **2800 BF** it was taken up by a **Sinalë outlaw**, who used it against Khazártúrn.

**How the city fell.** Its doors could not be forced and were not. A city of thirty thousand must
breathe and drink, so it had ventilation shafts and water galleries reaching miles out into the mountain
— individually trivial, and useless to any tribe, which has too few bodies for more than one and no Ahk
to coordinate the rest. Under a single will, **every shaft in the massif was opened at the same hour.**
The city's whole defensive logic assumed a threat arrives at a door.

**Why he did it.** Feeding a hold that size meant manufacturing an agricultural hinterland and expanding
it for a thousand years — forests cleared, bogs drained, rivers diverted, uplands terraced and settled.
To a Sinalë that is a stone thing eating the living world to keep itself alive. He was, narrowly, _not
wrong about what was happening_, which is what his own people found hardest: many shared the diagnosis
while being appalled by the remedy, and that is not something one says to the bereaved.

**The sundering, which both peoples guard for different reasons.** The Sinalë knew the relic existed and
never mentioned it — not from malice but indifference, a weapon against cities being no concern to a
people who build none. Then one of theirs used it, and they hunted him down themselves, slowly, and
afterward handed over nothing and explained nothing. _Your people knew, your people did it, your people
settled it in private_ against _one outlaw, whom we destroyed; we owed you no reckoning._ Both
defensible; neither survivable.

**And the Khazári doctrine is now something worse than a grudge.** They know the Grukar were compelled —
it is written down — and they kill them on sight anyway, because the conclusion they reached was not that
Grukar are wicked but that **Grukar are ammunition**, and the relic was never destroyed. A policy of
prevention, reasoned out, executed for four and a half thousand years against a species they know to be
innocent, and never revisited.

**Where it went.** The Sinalë recovered it, and the two races made their only agreement since: it would
be held in trust **on Aelwyth, by both together** — the one country they still shared, and so the only
ground neither would hold alone. Six ships left Élavendre carrying it, crewed by people chosen for being
beyond reproach.

They never arrived. No storm of note, no wreckage, no spar, no body, on any coast in any year since. The
crews' unimpeachability is the detail that has troubled everyone ever told of it: it forecloses the
comfortable explanation and offers nothing in its place. Vorgald has been waiting four thousand years for
a delivery — which is worth remembering alongside the fact that the Sinalë of the Hallowwood later
withdrew from a war and left those same Khazári to finish it alone.
