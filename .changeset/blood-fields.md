**Provènzia's blood-fields become a category rather than two stubs.**

The kingdom has been fought over for as long as anyone remembers, and it carries the record in its
ground. A new note, _The Blood-fields of Provènzia_, defines what one is by contrast with something
the setting already had: an **arálwen** is a place where the material and spirit worlds lie _naturally_
thin, so a **blood-field** is one where the boundary was **torn** — by a great many deaths in one place
at one time — and did not close afterwards. An arálwen is a feature of the world and has keepers; a
blood-field is an injury to it and has none.

That yields a typology a GM can use, since most blood-fields are frightening and harmless: a
**recurrence** replays the battle, a **quickening** makes the ground grow too well, a **silence** holds
nothing that sings, a **dread** returns what was felt on it — commanders have manoeuvred enemies onto
such ground for centuries — and a **withering** kills the district, which is what Yhârvalen is doing.

It also records how Provènzia remembers. The kingdom names the **ground**, not the battle: a Provenzian
will tell you a field is a blood-field long before they can say which war made it, and for many of them
nobody can. The clergy of Thánatos hold the competent authority and are candid that their rite of
settlement works about half the time. The Black Flame's interest in Provènzia is best understood as an
interest in its blood-fields, since a field can be _worked on_.

The two existing stubs are written out — **Yhârvalen** as the withering, worsened because the raids
threw down the henge as well as the men, and **Tiravlen** as a recurrence that replays out over the
lake, which the lakeside villages have turned into an observance rather than a crisis. Three more give
the category reach: **Serravel** on the eastern frontier, still being added to and impossible to settle
because neither crown will grant the other's priests safe conduct; **Kývarel** in the west, a quickening
whose produce feeds Zûravlen's preserving trades; and **Calvènza**, a silence sitting in the middle of
the richest country in the kingdom, repeatedly sold and never built on.
