**The Yokveng and the Lesser Demons keep their affiliations, because somebody has to know the
forms.**

Both notes read as a realm and a class of being, which is why they looked like candidates for `lore`
rather than affiliations. They are not: they are minor deities that get petitioned, and a petition
cannot be improvised. The people who know the forms of address, the appeasements and the things that
must not appear in the same document are the Yokveng's priests, and priests make an affiliation.

- **Yokveng** gains _Those Who Know the Forms_. A ring attached to a named Demon Lord is an
  organised enterprise with a hidden censer and a Watcher to enforce loyalty, but the wider trade in
  Yokveng petitions is thinner and older: a village scribe with three prayers his grandfather knew,
  a temple copyist quietly selling a form he was never taught. They hold no office and would be
  astonished to be called clergy. What makes them priests is that the knowledge is specific — and a
  petition filed in the wrong Demon Lord's forms is not refused as a temple would refuse it. It is
  read by whoever it reached.
- **The Lesser Demons** gain _Those Who Petition Them_. They are petitioned far more often than the
  Lords, being cheaper to reach and smaller in their answers, and their practitioners are
  individuals rather than rings — scattered through exactly the clerical positions the note already
  says such mortals end up in: scribes, monks, minor officials. Their craft is a small inheritance
  taught one practitioner to one other, rarely written and constantly lost, which the Tānthëi blame
  for lesser-demon petitions going wrong far more often than the Lords', which are drafted by
  professionals.

They go wrong in the established direction. A ledger-gnawer asked to remove an inconvenient entry
removes the entry — and, if the request was loosely drawn, the ledger, the archive it sat in, and
the clerk who filed it.

Their `subType` stays `faithtradition` rather than following the six named Demon Lords to
`criminal`: a diffuse hedge-priesthood of petitioned minor deities is a different shape from an
organised ring, and the notes now say so explicitly.
