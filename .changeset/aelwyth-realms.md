**Aelwyth's four realms get real populations and real societies, and the island gets the thing that
actually defines it.**

The numbers first, because they forced a revision. Aldorath at 200,000, Stormveld at 150,000 and
Dúnavarre at 75,000 already exceed the 400,000 that a 500,000 island with a fifth in tribes would allow.
With Tarvenne at 75,000 the settled realms come to **500,000**, and a fifth of the whole outside every
realm puts the island at **625,000**. The earlier round half-million now reads as the _civilized_
population, which is how the newer figures make sense of it.

- **Aldorath** — 200,000, much the largest and the most conventionally feudal: four in five are serfs or
  peasants on manorial estates, under an aristocracy with real hereditary claims. Dunmere, the seat, is
  the only place in the kingdom that is a city rather than a market town.
- **Stormveld** — 150,000, and **not an Aelwythan realm at all**. Nordmen took the north-east some sixty
  years ago and enslaved the population rather than displacing it: around 30,000 Nordmen over around
  120,000 native thralls. Four in five people there are the property of the remaining fifth. That a
  Nordmen holding sits a hundred and twenty sea-miles off the Élavendren coast explains a great deal of
  Élavendren foreign policy, and why Dúnavarre keeps the Just Blade at strength. The thralls have risen
  twice; neither rising reached its second year.
- **Tarvenne** — 75,000 along the western coast, a republic and a **thoroughly corrupt** one. Offices
  are elective and are bought, the deliberation is genuine and for sale, and none of it is hidden — its
  political class regards Dúnavarre's incorruptible mage-administration as tyranny that has found a
  polite name.
- **Peshtar Wilderness** — the north-west and much of the northern coast, holding with the other
  unsettled country about 125,000 people in many distinct tribal cultures. Further communities live in
  isolated wooded settlements through the interior and the eastern and southern forest, and those are
  markedly **more warlike**. The lowland realms call all of them barbarians, which is convenient and
  tells you nothing.

**And the island's defining fact.** Aelwythans think of themselves as the world: six hundred thousand
people conducting their affairs with the entire seriousness of continental powers, in an order it has
never occurred to them is local — one Vylarian province holds ten times as many. Meanwhile the mainland
regards Aelwyth as a wilderness of barbarians, fell creatures and mystical powers, to be avoided; most
Aelwythans would be astonished to hear it, their lives being boring and normal in the way lives
generally are.

Both hold, because the reputation is **wrong about the people and right about the land**. There really
are places on the island where the fae are strong, in the deep interior forest and the wilder north
that no realm has ploughed or pretends to govern. The mainland has heard true things about a third of
Aelwyth and applied them to all of it; the farmer who has never seen anything stranger than a heron is
not wrong either, having never had cause to go where such things are.
