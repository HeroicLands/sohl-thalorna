**The Varkhad, the cause of the Pelwar Migration, and what the crossing to Aelwyth actually cost.**

The Migration was a **displacement**, and it now has a cause. The Pelwar of the eastern grasslands were
comparatively peaceful — herders and cultivators, tribal but not warlike. Into that country came the
**Varkhad**: a fierce people out of the further east, kin to the ancestors of the Khazryn desert peoples.
They did not exterminate anybody; over generations they **took the country and merged into it**, until the
two stocks could not be told apart. What came out of that mixture holds the north and north-east today —
the **Varokh of Vrystwald** and the **Nordmen of the Nordlands**, sharing the double ancestry and, notably,
the temperament that came with the eastern half of it.

The Pelwar who did not stay went west and south, generations of them, until the movement **ran out of land
against the western coast**. Which is why Pelwar-descended peoples are packed along that shore as densely
as they are, and why a few communities did the only thing left and went across the water. **Some Varkhad
followed them.**

**And there were Pelwar on Aelwyth long before any of that — because the Khazári brought them.** A hold
grows nothing and will not depend on an outside larder, so wherever the Deep Folk settled they needed
farmers at the gate, and Pelwar communities were carried to hold-country across Ankaris and over open
water to Aelwyth to work land for lords who did not farm. It left no story: it happened to particular
villages over centuries, and the Vardain are the last of them. So when the flood arrived, **the people
already on the island were the newcomers' own kin**, settled generations deep — which is much of why
Aelwyth could not simply refuse them, and much of why refusing them anyway went as it did.

**The crossing was very nearly a death sentence.** A hundred and twenty sea-miles is not a distance
barbarian peoples cross — not in that craft, with families and stock aboard, without pilots or charts. The
early Pelwar had come as passengers in Khazári bottoms; the ones in 2200 BF came in whatever would float,
and **losses are reckoned at two in three or worse**. Tens of thousands arriving means something like a
hundred thousand set out. They were not a host. They were the surviving third of a people who looked at
open sea with their families in tow and judged it the better option — which tells you what was behind
them.

**And what killed the Sinalë king is left vague, deliberately.** A Sinalë king of that age was not
something mortals killed, and the tradition is consistent that some other force was present on that field,
spiritual in nature, for reasons nobody has explained. The Sinalë do not discuss it and the Khazári were
never told.

What is agreed is the effect: not defeat but **heart-failure**. The Sinalë did not withdraw from the war —
they **abandoned everything** outside the deep wood, the crown and the kingdom and the works of six
centuries, and left it to the other races. What they gave up by walking away is, straightforwardly, why
Aelwyth has human kingdoms on it at all.
