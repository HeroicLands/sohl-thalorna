**Zûravlen becomes a coastal region, and Provènzia's geography is settled.**

Provènzia's compass is now fixed and consistent: Élavendre north, Tarvénia east, the warm Vylarian Sea
south, and the open ocean west — a vastly larger and far colder water. The region has **two coasts that
are nothing alike**: a warm, sheltered, sandy southern shore that carries nearly all its trade, and a
craggy western seaboard of cliffs above cold grey swell. Inland the north and east rise into mountains
that are genuinely cold and very nearly impassable in winter, which gives the constant skirmishing
along the Tarvénian frontier a **season** — raiding begins when the passes open and ends when they
shut, and a winter crossing is either a disaster or a deliberate outrage. Relations with Élavendre are
by contrast very cordial between the crowns, which is not the same as ease in the borderlands, where
Batáren's quarrels are real and both courts work to keep them domestic.

Zûravlen sits on that western shore, and the coast turns out to explain the barony rather than
complicate it. It is now **three bands**: a cliff coast where the salt wind burns anything tender and
nothing worth exporting grows; a sheltered valley behind the ridge, hot in summer, terraced for
**peaches and vines**; and uplands above for the flocks.

The wind is the reason the orchards are inland, not a problem for them. The frost works the same way
round — a maritime coast scarcely frosts, while cold air drains off the uplands and pools in the
sheltered valley — so the frost vigil is the valley's business and not the shore's, and Portvent is
neither called to it nor comes. Cold water against a hot land also makes **fog**, which drowns the
harbour most summer mornings, climbs the cliff, and stops at the ridge.

**Vines** answer the barony's worst problem: vintage falls after the peach harvest so the two never
compete for hands, wine keeps and travels where fruit does not, a vine bears for a lifetime against the
peach's twenty years, and in a frost year the vines usually come through because they break bud later.

New settlement: **Portvent**, the harbour at the foot of the cliff — fish (the cold water being far
richer than the Vylarian), salt pans, and the fast boats that now carry fresh fruit to markets no rider
could reach. Every basket that leaves fresh pays no share to the baron's drying yards, which are his
largest income, so the harbour and the yards are quietly in competition and everyone has noticed.
