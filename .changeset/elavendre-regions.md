**Élavendre is normalised into five regions, every place is located, and the Ordo question is stated as
the three-way standoff it actually is.**

The kingdom's 2,000,000 divide by geography before administration:

| Region       | People  | What it is                                                                                  |
| ------------ | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Tirwen**   | 900,000 | the heartland basin of the **Bérav** — capital, University, and where value is added        |
| **Glanmor**  | 420,000 | the western seaboard: cliffs, Sinalë forest, the thickest fae country outside the mountains |
| **Cilfor**   | 260,000 | the northern coast on **Cilfor Bay**, thirty miles from Vrystwald across the water          |
| **Brynavar** | 240,000 | the eastern hills — wool, iron, and the land march that takes the raiding                   |
| **Ardwyn**   | 180,000 | the eastern and southern mountains — Áelendan country, and wealth the crown may not take    |

The economy runs inward and out again: raw material from the four margins, worked in Tirwen, exported
through Béravel. Each region's trade goods are given — and **Ardwyn's are the interesting case**. Its
mountains hold silver, gold and gemstone in real abundance, and on any ordinary reading Élavendre ought
to be the richest kingdom in western Ankaris. It is not, because the ore lies under Áelendan ground and
the tribes do not permit mining. What little is extracted is marginal, dangerous and ruinous, which is
exactly why Élavendren gold and gemwork command what they do: **the scarcity is political, not
geological**, and everyone in the trade knows it.

Two features are named because the geography needed them: **Cilfor Bay**, the backward-C gulf fifty
miles across and thirty at the narrows — not a barrier but a night's rowing, which is why the north is
raided — and **the Bérav**, the river that carries every regional economy down past the capital.

Six stub settlements are written with locations and livelihoods: Béravel, Liranel, Silvain, Elanmere,
Valdûn and Ceridwen's Hollow.

**Contradictions resolved.** Dúnavarre's note claimed Élavendre "refuses to permit the Ordo any presence
within its borders" while Élavendre's own note gives the Ordo the largest Panepistemium campus outside
the Empire. The correct statement is that Élavendre **permits the Ordo and limits its access** — houses
and teaching in the cities, nothing past the line into tribal lands, sacred sites or deep forest.

That line is now stated as a standoff between three parties: the **Ordo**, which wants a great deal
from the one place in the world where the veil is this thin and steals artifacts from the sacred sites
to get it; the **crown**, which sees real political gain in cordiality and will not expel it; and the
**Áelendan**, who regard the Ordo as meddlesome intruders and thieves. The crown's cordiality has a hard
ceiling — Élavendren legitimacy runs through the old blood, so a monarch believed to have taken the
Ordo's side would face not rebellion but the quiet judgement that he was no longer quite of it.

**Calypsa** is corrected too: population 1,000,000 on a fifteen-mile island becomes 45,000; it sits
forty miles NNW outside the bay's mouth; its agriculture is essentially nil (it eats what it buys); and
it keeps a navy out of all proportion to its size, which is why it is nobody's vassal — and why northern
Élavendre enjoys protection it neither pays for nor acknowledges.

The standoff's operative machinery is recorded too. Ordo missions are genuinely welcomed and embassies
permitted in the cities; movement inward is never refused, only deferred and _discouraged on grounds of
safety_ — the interior is dangerous, the Áelendan proud, the crown could not answer for a distinguished
visitor beyond the tree line. So the crown provides **guides**, who are courteous, skilled, and lead the
Ordo nowhere that matters. The Ordo has known this for generations and keeps the fiction, because it
keeps a foot in the door — and sends its own people into the countryside to look for the sites. None of
it fools the Áelendan, who tolerate the arrangement because the alternative is worse, and what they do
about the men with the sketchbooks is not the crown's business.
