**Tarvenne doubles to 150,000 and becomes a republic on purpose — a deliberate imitation of Vylaria,
run by a dozen men who are worse than corrupt.**

The republic now holds **150,000**, the second-largest polity on Aelwyth and twice the size of
Dúnavarre. The island rises to **700,000**, of which 575,000 are in the four settled realms and 125,000
outside every realm.

Its constitution is not accidental. Tarvenne copies the **Vylarian forms** on purpose: a Senate whose
members are styled senators, magistracies with Vylarian names and terms, a civic calendar of processions,
and public building in a self-consciously Vylarian manner executed in local stone by masons working from
descriptions. Senators wear the bordered mantle; the Senate house at Thornbury has a portico. The
aspiration is sincere and, in its own terms, not absurd.

What undoes it is arithmetic. Vylaria holds some **twenty-one million** people; Tarvenne holds a hundred
and fifty thousand, on the western shore of an island most Vylarians could not place on a chart. Even
diminished, the empire remains larger than the whole Aurèldían west together, and Tarvenne beside it is a
sorry shadow of a thing it has never seen. The Senate does not entertain the thought; Vylarian factors
are received with immense ceremony and go home with anecdotes.

And corruption flatters them. Senatorial place is bought, the deliberation is for sale, and the courts
work for those who can pay them to work — but a good number of Tarvenne's senators are plainly **evil
men**: wholly indifferent to the governed, whose condition enters their calculations only as a supply of
labour, rents and votes; driven by self-interest without restraint or embarrassment; and vain past
caricature — jealous of precedence, incapable of an unrewarded act, contemptuous of anyone who cannot
injure them, entirely persuaded of their own greatness.

There are a dozen of the first rank and the republic's politics is the friction between them. They ruin
each other's clients and combine only against whatever threatens the arrangement that lets all of them
prosper. Whether the Senate does anything for Tarvenne's hundred and fifty thousand depends on whether
it happens to lie along one of these men's ambitions — often it does not, nothing is done, and everyone
understands this as the weather.
