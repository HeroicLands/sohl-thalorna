**Aelwyth's settlements are written out and its parents audited — the island now matches the treatment
Provènzia and Élavendre got.**

Every one of Aelwyth's thirteen place notes now has a body and a parent pointing at the most specific
thing containing it. Eight were stubs; four parents were wrong, including two seats parented to the
island rather than to their own realms, and two notes that listed the continent redundantly alongside
their actual region.

**Aldorath** gets the six towns of a feudal kingdom, each with a job: **Dunmere**, the seat and only
real city, where a king who wants anything must obtain it from lords whose estates are their real
interest; **Caerwynd**, the western fortress watching the forest margin and the long unfought rivalry
with Dúnavarre over the interior; **Ashford**, the drove-market where the wool clip is graded and whose
merchant families have money and no voice in a kingdom where standing is land; **Greywater**, the tidal
river port that hates the Ravenmoor requirement and has complied with it for generations;
**Brynhallow**, an upland town held on older and lighter terms, keeping the Twelve and something else
besides at the stones above it; and **Kingshollow**, the dynasty's original seat, where kings are still
crowned and buried though the court left long ago.

**Stormveil** is Stormveld's seat and looks nothing like an Aelwythan town — a jarl's hall on the
height, sworn men's houses around it, the harbour facing north, and the thrall quarters down the slope
and set apart. Both of the Jarldom's risings began there, which the jarls have taken to be a lesson
about vigilance.

**Thornbury** is the republic in miniature: a colonnaded Senate house with a portico raised by masons
working from descriptions of buildings none of them had seen, on a plan copied from an account of
Magnápolis and adapted without acknowledgement to a much smaller and wetter site.
