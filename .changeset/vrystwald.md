**Vrystwald gets a population, and its "primeval homeland" becomes true twice over.**

**1,400,000**, spread very unevenly across an enormous territory — which completes the northern bucket at
~3,450,000 alongside the Nordlands' 2,000,000 and Grukarholm's 50,000. The northern taiga is nearly empty,
hunter-fisher-trapper clans working furs worth more than the country they come from. The central
mixed-forest belt holds most of the Varokh, though "most" means innumerable small clearings rather than
towns. Settlement actually concentrates on the **river-courses** — the rivers are the roads, the trade and
the sites, so a map of Vrystwaldi population is essentially a map of its water. The densest ground is the
southern frontier with Velanthia, where forest gives way to grain-belt through a thousand miles of mixed
villages and intermittent raiding.

A large population for a forest and a small one for the space, which is precisely why no army has ever
finished a war there. **There is nothing to capture.** The Varokh are not concentrated anywhere an invader
can reach them, and the country is big enough to lose an army in — which several have obliged it by doing.

**And the homeland line is fixed by being doubled rather than qualified.** The Varkhad reframe made
"primeval homeland" only half true; it turns out to be true _twice_, in two ways that ought to contradict
each other and don't.

The country was **Pelwar** first — peaceful people, herders and cultivators, who had the forest and the
rivers a very long time before anyone disturbed them. Then the **Varkhad** came out of the further east
and took it, and did not exterminate anybody but married into them over generations until the two stocks
could not be told apart. The result is the Varokh.

So a Varokh defending Vrystwald is descended from the people who were here first **and** from the people
who invaded them, and has no way of telling which ancestor is doing the defending. Nobody up there
distinguishes, and after three thousand years nobody could.

What is not in doubt is which half the ferocity came from. The Pelwar who stayed to be conquered were not
the ones who break armies in the forest — which is an observation an outsider makes once, in a Varokh
hall, and does not make twice.

It also resolves something that puzzles Aurèldían scholars: how the Varokh can be both the people who
**drove the Pelwar Migration** and themselves _be_ Pelwar. Both are correct. They drove out their own
cousins, did not think of them as cousins at the time, and by the time anyone could have said so the
question had stopped meaning anything.
