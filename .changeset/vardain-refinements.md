**The Vardain arrangement gets its scale, its altitude and its engineering — and the Vardain are counted
separately from the tribes.**

**Counted apart.** Aelwyth's population is now three groups, not two: the four settled realms
(~575,000), the tribal peoples (~125,000), and the **Vardain** (~10,000), for an island total of
**710,000**. Lumping the Vardain with the tribes is noted as the commonest error outsiders make — they
are a settled, literate farming people and the free remnant of a nation, not barbarians in any sense.

**Altitude.** The basin floor is about **six thousand feet**; the Stormplain below is a couple of
hundred. Nearly everything follows from that gap. The season is four months, wheat is out of the
question, the real living is livestock and hay, and the basin is snowbound for a third of the year —
which gives the trade a season, gives escape from Stormveld a season, and explains why Threndale's _new
ground_ keeps taking people who very nearly made it.

**Scale corrected.** The valley is one small basin — perhaps twenty miles by a few — above an immense
conquered country. The free Vardain hold a pocket in the mountains above land they used to own.

**And the engineering, which is the point.** Unaided, this community would starve inside a decade. It
survives because the Khazári have engineered the valley on a scale no human work on Aelwyth approaches:
snowmelt caught behind cut dams and released on schedule through **buried conduits** that run for miles
below the frost line; a gravity distribution reaching every terrace at an appointed hour; the bog floor
drained to gradients no Aelwythan surveyor could reproduce; terrace walls built as **thermal mass**,
sited and angled to give back the day's heat after dark and carry a crop through frosts that would
otherwise take it; the highest fields at Highfold and Dorrin **warmed from beneath** by air drawn off
the deep workings; and rock-cut **granaries**, sealed and ventilated, that have held a year's food sound
for an unrecorded number of centuries.

The Vardain maintain all of it with a discipline that is the central fact of their public life. They did
not build it and could not. Parts they understand; parts they maintain by rote from procedures handed
down without explanation; and some elements' purpose nobody in the valleys can now state — they keep
those in repair anyway, on the grounds that the Khazári built nothing without reason and the reason will
not be explained twice.

Which makes the arrangement need rather than kindness on both sides. A hold cannot import its food, and
the conquest had just destroyed the Khazári's supply — the people coming up the passes were the farmers
who had been feeding them. The Vardain could not farm here without the Khazári; the Khazári could not
hold Vorgald without the Vardain.
