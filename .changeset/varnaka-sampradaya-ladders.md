**The four Varnaka _sampradāya_ take their own three-tier ladders, because Varnaka organises by school
rather than by god.**

The split here is the interesting part. The eleven Varnaka deity notes name no clergy at all; the
four traditions name theirs precisely, in Sanskrit and with glosses. So a Varnaka priest is ordained
into a **school** — a _sampradāya_ — and serves whichever of the gods that school's doctrine
requires, which is the reverse of the Aurèldían arrangement where each of the Twelve has a priesthood
of its own.

Each tradition is a clean three tiers of its own naming, under a shared **Patita** ("Fallen") at
level 0 and **Upāsaka**, the lay follower, at level 1:

| Tradition           | Acolyte                           | Working priest          | Senior                              |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Agnī-panthā         | Bhasma ("Ash")                    | Jvālita ("Kindled One") | Dahana-Mūla ("Root of the Burning") |
| Svapnasāri Samāja   | Nidrāpāla ("Keeper of Sleep")     | Darśaka ("Seer")        | Svapna-Ácārya ("Master of Dreams")  |
| Trimūrti Sampradāya | Sevaka ("Servant")                | Ácārya                  | Triyācārya ("Master of the Three")  |
| Vyālendravāda       | Antevāsin ("One who dwells near") | Ácārya                  | Parama-Ácārya ("Supreme Master")    |

The governance models differ because the prose says they do. **Agnī-panthā is a `council`**, not a
theocracy: its own note calls Dahana-Mūla _"a rare rank, since most of the sect's authority rests
with individual priests on the road"_ — the authority is in the itinerant priest and the ember-vessel
they carry, and ordination in that sect is really the entrusting of a vessel. **Vyālendravāda is a
`meritocracy`**: its senior rank turns on a rigorous philosophical examination and the holder is
expected to have published, and its acolytes are recruited at mature age out of the building trades
and the academies. The other two are theocracies of the ordinary kind.

**Trimūrti Sampradāya's ladder encodes its doctrine.** Ordination is to _one_ of the three forms
while every priest must keep the rites of the other two, so a middling temple seats three Ácāryas —
one per form — and the Triyācārya, ordained to serve all three at once, is the rank that is hard to
reach. The office **Keeper of the Three Rites** is what the tradition exists to maintain.

`commonSkills` are wired from each tradition's own **Key Skills** line: 27 of the 33 listed skills
resolve. The six that do not — _Trance_, _Alchemy_, _Astrology_ (twice) and _Empathy_ — are mystical
abilities or have no skill note, and are left out rather than invented.

Affiliations still on the generic ladder fall from 30 to 26.
