**Songbirds flew about inside Khazártúrn — which settles what the city was, and complicates what it is.**

The halls were vast enough that birds came in at the skylights, stayed, and nested in the high galleries,
and were left alone. It is the detail the records keep returning to, and it proves the design's whole
intent better than any measurement could: **a hall a bird will live in is not a room.** Whatever the
Khazári were trying to build when they built to feel like open air, the birds are the evidence they
managed it.

It also has a consequence the sealing cannot prevent. Skylights cut through a mountain cannot be closed
without unmaking the halls beneath them, so the way the daylight takes is a way in for other things —
nothing large, nothing that could trouble a sealed door. But the shafts stand open as they always did, the
halls below are warm and dry and full of running water, and there is no reason to suppose the birds ever
left.

Four thousand years of them, in a city nobody enters. The note records that this is the one thing about
Khazártúrn the Khazári will not discuss even among themselves — not because it is terrible, but because
it is not, and they have never worked out what to do with that.
