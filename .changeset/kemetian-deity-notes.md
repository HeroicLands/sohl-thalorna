**The nineteen Kemetían deity notes are written. They were all `TBD`.**

The affiliation notes described the priesthoods in detail; the lore notes that should describe the
gods said nothing at all. So the corpus could tell you exactly what a Hem'Netjer of Sēker does on a
given day and nothing about who Sēker is. That gap is now closed for Ta'Kheperu — the first pantheon
in the tree whose deity notes are written (of ninety-one across all pantheons, one was).

Almost nothing here is invented. Each faith note carries an intro and a `## Worship` section I had
not read on the earlier passes, and between them they hold the theology; the deity note's job was to
separate **the god** from **the institution** and say the first part plainly.

**What surfaced in the writing:**

- **Má'át's judgment has names.** The **Weighing of the Heart** happens in the **Hall of Two
  Truths**; a heart heavier than her feather is devoured by **Ámit, the Devourer of the Dead**, and
  one that balances passes into the **Duat**. Its moral force rests on a single feature the empire
  note states outright — wealth cannot buy a favourable verdict — which is what makes it a real check
  on power in an otherwise steeply unequal civilization.
- **Sunrise is a victory, not a fact.** Ápōphis _"cannot be destroyed, only conquered"_, so Rā's
  nightly battle is refought every night forever. That reframes the whole solar cult: its vigilance
  is not metaphor.
- **Bēs is not a Kemetían god at all.** He is the Kheperi name for
  [[lore-goddreams|the God of Dreams]] — **Baldr** to the Asguardians, **Æthería** to the Aurèldíans,
  known to the Sinalë since time immemorial. One deity, three pantheons, and the note now says so.
- **Ptā'h may have made Rā.** One strand of temple theology holds the universe came into being
  through Ptā'h's creative will with Rā as the animating principle moving through it — a claim that
  would be a schism anywhere else and here _"has never caused conflict."_
- **Sēker does not judge.** By the time a soul reaches him it has already been weighed. He and Ánubís
  divide cleanly: the body's transformation is Ánubís's, the soul's passage is Sēker's, which is why
  their priesthoods share the mortuary temples.
- **Rā has no schism**, and the note says why that is notable: the Aurèldían Vúlcani split into a
  sacred forge and a black flame over exactly the duality Rā refuses.

**Every note ends with a specific `TBD`**, following the one written deity note already in the tree.
They are real open questions rather than placeholders — what the Eternal Library holds that Khemenu's
archives do not; what Sēker does for a soul the scale condemns; why Sét's priesthood and Má'át's are
on bad terms. Two are worth flagging as genuine gaps in the world rather than in the notes:

- **Mentu has no myth.** The record establishes where he is worshipped and by whom, and says nothing
  about who he is — no cosmic role, no relations with the Twelve.
- **Khet'Amun may be Sobek.** The two share the water, the river-beasts and the problem of the cull,
  and the corpus never sets them side by side. The question decides whether the Sekhet'Neru's
  dispensation is owed to one cult or two.

The `draft` tag stays on all nineteen, since each still carries an open question.
