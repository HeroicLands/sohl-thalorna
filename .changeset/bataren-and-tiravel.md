**The Gulf of Batáren, Batáren City and Lake Tiravel get places in Provènzia's geography, and stop
being one-line stubs.**

_The Silent War in Batáren_ already fixed the city: coastal, in the **Élavendren borderlands**. With
Élavendre north and the ocean west, that puts it in the **northwest**, and the rest follows.

The **Gulf of Batáren** is the one opening in an ocean coast otherwise made of cliffs — a broad, deep,
sheltered bay with holding ground and room for a fleet, where Portvent down in Zûravlen is a notch in a
rock face unusable forty days a year. It faces the cold open ocean rather than the warm Vylarian Sea,
so nothing of the Vylarian world arrives there; what comes comes down from Élavendre and Aelwyth and
out of the northern seas. It is also, inevitably, how the Nordheim raids arrived.

**Batáren City** is therefore the city that faces the wrong way. Provènzia's wealth runs along the
sunlit southern coast in and out of the Vylarian world; Batáren takes its money from fish, timber,
northern furs and freight the south does not want, and a Provenzian from Válaren finds it cold in every
sense. Its population is substantially **Élavendren** — settlers who came down centuries ago and have
been there longer than the present arrangement of crowns — and the quarrels over land, rights and
inheritance customs the Crown does not recognise are unresolved and mostly not violent. They grind.
Both crowns keep it a domestic matter because relations are otherwise very cordial, and everyone in the
city understands that two kingdoms have agreed not to look closely, which is a great deal of latitude
for anyone who would rather they did.

**Lake Tiravel** goes to the eastern march, in a basin below the Tarvénian passes — far enough behind
them that raiders, who want what can be carried back before the alarm spreads, leave it alone. So it is
settled, fished, terraced for vines and comparatively prosperous, and it provisions the frontier
garrisons: they eat because the lake rarely has to think about them. Its blood-field replays out over
the water, and nobody can say which battle made it, the lake having been worth taking for a very long
time.
