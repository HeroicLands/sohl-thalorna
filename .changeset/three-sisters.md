**The elder kingdom of Aelwyth was a reconciliation, and the Battle of Three Sisters is where it died.**

This reframes the island's deep history. The joint rule was not an ancient inheritance — it was an
**attempt at repair**. After Khazártúrn fell in 2800 BF the Sinalë and Khazári stopped dealing with one
another everywhere in the world _except here_, where instead they united Aelwyth under a **single crown**
held by a Sinalë king and governed it together for **six hundred years**. It is the only reconciliation
either race has ever attempted.

It was ended by people who had nowhere else to go. The **Pelwar** reached Aelwyth around **2200 BF**,
driven off the mainland by the **Varokh** — themselves a Pelwar branch, in the great displacement that
scattered that people across half of Ankaris. They did not come to conquer; they were pushed off the edge
of the continent. But an island holds a fixed amount of good land, and two centuries of arrival, pressure
and reprisal became the **Great War**.

New note: **The Three Sisters**, three peaks above the Central Highlands, where in **1984 BF** the Sinalë
king was killed. With him dead the surviving Sinalë withdrew — out of the war, out of the kingdom, into
the forest that is now the Hallowwood — and left the Khazári to finish it.

They finished it. And the withdrawal is worse than the first breach, because of what preceded it: not a
quarrel, but the Sinalë walking away from the Khazári in a crisis **for the second time in eight hundred
years**, after six centuries of persuading them there would not be another. Nothing has been attempted
since.

Two details the note holds onto. **Nobody in the war was a villain** — an elder kingdom that could not
absorb them, and refugees with nowhere to go — which is the least comfortable thing about it. And every
human on Aelwyth today, every realm and every tribe, descends from those refugees. None of them know it,
and neither elder race has ever seen a reason to tell them.

Nothing marks the site. Three peaks over open grazing in the pleasantest country on the island, and the
Tarvennese senators with summer houses in sight of them do not know the name means anything.
