**Khazártúrn is sealed dark and silent, and nothing is ever to disturb it.**

This corrects the previous change, which had the skylights unsealable and songbirds still living in the
city. That was sentiment where the Khazári would be thorough. Of course they could seal them: each
skylight was unmade, the old window-work lifted out entire, and cut granite dressed to the surrounding
rock and set flush in its place — no seam, nothing to see from outside, the mountain reading as mountain.
The concealment was the point.

The **fountains were shut off** at their source, and not out of any feeling about silence: water left
running in a closed city with nobody to tend it floods that city in time, and the last thing they did for
Khazártúrn was make certain it would keep. The **birds went out with the light**, put out before the last
of the work, which the account records in one line and does not enlarge upon.

So what is in there now is everything, unaltered, in the dark. Nothing ruined, nothing disordered — the
colonnades, the galleries, the painted market skies, the spun-glass stone, and thirty thousand Khazári
lying in the rooms where they lived, perfectly kept for four and a half thousand years, and **completely
dark and completely silent**.

Anyone who ever got in would find the splendour wholly intact and wholly unable to do what it was built
for. Halls raised to hold daylight, holding none; market circles painted as open sky, under torchlight;
vaults hundreds of feet up and out of any lamp's reach, so the greatest rooms ever made register as a cold
draught, an echo, and no ceiling at all. **Bright and airy, made dim and vast** — which the Khazári did
deliberately, in order to keep it, and would do again.

**And nothing is ever to disturb it.** Not a preference and not sentiment: the settled, permanent,
unnegotiable position of every hold on Thalorna, and the shortest way to understand what the Deep Folk
will and will not do. They tolerate human kingdoms, ignore human wars and let millennia pass without
troubling anyone. Two things move them — Grukar, and this.

They issue no warnings and post no notices, because a warning invites negotiation and there is nothing
here to negotiate. Anyone who searches for Khazártúrn, finds it, speaks credibly of having found it, or is
understood to be assembling the means to open it, is a problem they will solve quietly and completely,
without reference to who it is or what they intended. _We only wished to look_ is not a distinction they
recognise — and there is nothing in there to recover in any case: no hoard, no relic, no lost craft. The
archive died with everyone who kept it, and the Second Voice never went near the place.
