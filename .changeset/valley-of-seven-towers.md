**Khazártúrn was the Valley of Seven Towers — and the concealment was original, not something added at
the sealing.**

It was never a buried hold. The city was cut into the **cliff face of a mountain valley**, with the
valley open in front of it, which is why its halls could take daylight at all and why anyone thought to
try. Standing out from that cliff were **seven great towers**.

Seven because there were **seven clans** — the seven that came to Thalorna together around 4280 BF — one
tower to each, and the city held jointly beneath them. It is the only thing the Khazári are known to have
built as a single people, which means its loss took some part of every line on Thalorna at once. The
Khazári folk note now carries the clans as origin lore: patrilineal names, genealogies kept as carefully
as the craft-records, and any Khazár able to recite a line back to one of the seven without notes.

**Every tower was built to be indistinguishable from a natural feature of the valley.** Not camouflaged
or screened — _cut and finished as rock_: bedded, weathered, faulted, jointed, stained, carrying the same
strata as the cliff they stand out from. Somebody in that valley sees seven great stone spires and a wall
of mountain, and is correct in every particular except the one that matters.

That reframes the sealing. When the Khazári closed the city they dressed the granite to the rock and left
no seam — but they were not inventing the technique for the occasion. **They were finishing a job begun
four thousand years earlier.**

And it produces the fact that makes the location worth guarding, and worth playing. The valley today looks
exactly as it always looked. Seven stone spires and a cliff.

Somebody has probably camped there.
