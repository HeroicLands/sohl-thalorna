**The Yokveng priesthoods are flat: lay, acolyte, priest, and nothing above.**

They had been given the deep criminal ladder — eight rungs from Client up through Seal-Forger and
Watcher to a Fallen Functionary — which described an organised enterprise rather than a priesthood.
The greater Yokveng do have priesthoods, but very flat ones.

Ranks are now **Recorded** → **Lay** → **Acolyte** → **Priest**. A person has petitioned, or they
have been taught, or they can draft; there is nothing else to be. The Priest is the top: they hold
that particular Yokveng's forms of address, appeasements and prohibitions, draft, file, and keep
whatever censer they can keep.

**No temples.** The Yokveng are not a temple religion and mostly have no temples — a censer in a
back room is the whole of the establishment — so the Temple Priest rank is gone and the offices no
longer assume a shrine to defend. What survives is the functional handful: Keeper of the Forms,
Seal-Forger, Clerk of Transmission, Receiver of Tribute, Gap-Maker, and the Watcher who keeps the
cultus ahead of the Celestial Marshals.

The flatness reinforces what the petition mechanics already established. There is no hierarchy to
appeal to, no procedure, and no supervising officer to catch a badly drawn request before it is
burned — which is precisely why a Yokveng petition is dangerous in a way that a bureau's is not.
