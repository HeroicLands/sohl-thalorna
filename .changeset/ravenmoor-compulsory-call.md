**The Synod's compulsory call at Ravenmoor — the instrument that makes Aelwyth its backyard.**

Every ship crossing between the mainland and Aelwyth must put in at **Ravenmoor** first: inbound before
it may go on to any other port, outbound before it may leave. The Synod's officers inspect cargo and
manifests for prohibited goods, which is unremarkable and quietly welcome, since no other realm on the
island can police its own coast.

The second interest is the real one. **Anything magical is reported** — relics, worked items,
curiosities of uncertain nature — and is recorded, examined and frequently detained, with compensation
offered and usually fair. The stated reason is that unexamined magic crossing into an island this
fae-touched endangers everyone, which is true and not the whole truth: no relic has left Aelwyth in
living memory without the Ordo seeing it first.

**Why the island tolerates it.** Fear of Dúnavarre, and indifference. The other realms would rather not
discover what testing the Synod costs, and being insular to the bone they lose little by letting somebody
else manage the mainland — what an Aelwythan realm cares about is what happens on Aelwyth. Tarvenne
dislikes it most and most loudly, sending its shipping the long way round to be searched by mages it
despises, and has passed resolutions for sixty years without once refusing to comply.

**Stormveld is outside the arrangement, by the Synod's choice.** Its trade and its wars run north to the
Nordlands, not east to the mainland, and Nordmen hulls reach the north-east coast without troubling
Ravenmoor. The Synod does not stop them because it does not care: the compulsory call was never really
customs, it is control of the channel between Aelwyth and the Aurèldían world — what knowledge crosses,
what relics cross, and who arrives. Iron, timber and quarrels between jarls bear on none of that. So the
mages watch the eastern water and ignore the northern, deliberately, and have never explained the
distinction to anyone who resents it.

That also gives Stormveld its own section: the one realm on Aelwyth that looks outward, and not at the
mainland at all. The Nordlands are not one power, so its northern dealings are mixed — steady trade with
some kingdoms, raiding and open war with others — and it is the one realm whose outside dealings are
nobody's business but its own. The other realms are islands unto themselves; Stormveld has a mainland of
its own behind it.

Ravenmoor also stops being a stub and is reparented from the island to its kingdom.
