**How the Synod enforces the compulsory call — which is to say, how Aelwyth's fear of Dúnavarre became
specific.**

The Synod has never threatened anyone over the Ravenmoor requirement and does not need to, because the
pattern is well known.

A realm that makes an end-run around Dúnavarre — trading with the mainland directly, sending its own
envoy, bringing something in unexamined — first notices something small. **Its Ordo advisors are not
there.** Every court on the island keeps mages because every court needs them, and they are Ordo-trained
and Synod-approved; one morning the man is simply gone, no letter and no trace, and the replacement the
court writes for does not come. Sometimes he is found later, in a condition the household will not
discuss.

Then the ordinary misfortunes begin: a harvest that fails where it had no business failing, a sickness
through the stock or the children that no physician recognises and that stops as inexplicably as it
started, a well gone bad, a season of weather the oldest farmers say they have never seen.

None of it is ever attributed. Nothing is claimed, no demand is made, no connection is asserted, and any
suggestion of one is met at Ravenmoor with grave and genuine-seeming concern. Twice in two centuries a
realm has pressed the point in writing and received a courteous reply regretting the misfortune and
offering assistance.

Both realms stopped what they were doing. Neither said why.
