**Provènzia's four regions get proper names, and Ménavar Abbey gets a home and a history.**

The placeholder descriptors are replaced with names in the kingdom's own Romance register, each of
which says what the region is:

| Was            | Now            | Gloss                                                            |
| -------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Southern Coast | **Meridiàna**  | _the noon country_ — the south, and by implication the rich part |
| Eastern March  | **Sèrramarca** | _the mountain march_                                             |
| Northern March | **Tramontàna** | _beyond the mountains_, and the cold wind that comes over them   |
| —              | **Zûravlen**   | unchanged; the west keeps the barony's name                      |

The rename carries through every note: shortcodes, directories, `parents`, and the descriptive prose
that had been saying "the eastern march" now names Sèrramarca and links it.

**Ménavar Abbey** goes to Tramontàna, where its own adventure already put it — _"damaged centuries ago
during the Nordheim raids and subsequently abandoned."_ Those are the raids that came in off the Gulf of
Batáren and threw down Yhârvalen Henge a day's march away, so the abbey's ruin is part of the same
catastrophe rather than a separate misfortune.

The note takes up the question the adventure implies but does not ask: houses of that standing are
normally rebuilt, and this one was not. The contemporary account is practical — the north was unsafe and
the endowment was gone — but every later attempt to reoccupy the site was abandoned within a season, and
the records of those attempts are notably brief for an institution that documented everything else
exhaustively. The abbey is said to be haunted by the monks who died defending it; the Church does not
teach this and has never formally denied it.

Its relic's location is uncertain because the abbey's own plans survive in three versions that conflict
_precisely where they matter_ — which some read as deliberate, and others as the reason the brothers
thought it safe. Four parties want it and none will say so plainly; given who has been working the
ground at Yhârvalen, a fifth is worth considering.
