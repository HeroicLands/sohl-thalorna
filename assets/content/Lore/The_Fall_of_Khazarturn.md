# The Fall of Khazártúrn

**Khazártúrn** was the greatest city the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] ever built, and its destruction in
**2800 BF** is the central fact of their history, the origin of their standing war against the
[[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]], and the reason they have not spoken to the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] in four
and a half thousand years.

The Deep Folk reached Thalorna about 4280 BF. By roughly **3800 BF** Khazártúrn stood: not a hold among
holds but the work all their other work was measured against, home to some thirty thousand, and the seat
of an entire people's craft-records.

It stood a thousand years.

## The Judgement

Around **3000 BF** the Grukar appeared — new in the world, and the Khazári were the only people on
Thalorna keeping records when it happened. Nothing written at Khazártúrn in its first seven centuries
mentions them; within a generation, a great deal does.

They studied the new species with their customary thoroughness: observed it, described the castes,
documented the fission, and set down the conclusion. Grukar were **a vicious but manageable vermin of
the deep country** — dangerous at a frontier, incapable of concentration, permanently limited by the
fact that **Ahks do not cooperate** and therefore cannot combine into anything resembling an army.

Every word of that assessment was **correct**. It is still correct. It was in the archive, in their own
hand, when the city fell — and it did not matter in the slightest, which is the part the Khazári have
never been able to put down.

## What Came

In 2800 BF a **Sinalë outlaw** came to Khazártúrn carrying the
**[[miscgear-secondvoice|Second Voice]]**.

The relic does not ally Ahks; it speaks over them, taking the hatched directly and displacing the
spawner's claim, so that Grukar of any number of tribes answer one will. What arrived at the city was
therefore not a Grukar army — there is no such thing — but tens of thousands of Grukar **taken** from
tribes across a wide country, moving as a single mind, incapable of fear, disagreement or exhaustion.

## The Shafts

Khazártúrn could not be forced at its doors, and it was not.

A city of thirty thousand cannot be sealed. It must **breathe**, and it must **drink**, and so it was
built with ventilation shafts and water galleries reaching out to the mountain — dozens of them, small,
scattered over miles, and individually of no consequence. No tribe could have used them: a tribe has
too few bodies to open more than one, and no Ahk would coordinate with another to open the rest.

**They opened every one of them at the same hour.**

Uk dig. That is what they are for, and under a single will there were enough of them to work every shaft
in the massif simultaneously, without communication, without a signal, without anything the Khazári
could observe as a plan. The city's entire defensive logic assumed that a threat arrives at a door and
can be met there. Instead it arrived everywhere, from inside, in the dark, in numbers.

Khazártúrn was not besieged. It was taken in days.

## The Dead

Thirty thousand Khazári, or near enough that the difference is a matter of a few hundred names the
record could not confirm.

They fought — the account is emphatic about this, in the flat tone the Khazári use for everything, and
records the resistance chamber by chamber and level by level. It made no difference. What they were
fighting did not tire, did not break, and did not care what it lost, because the thing directing it did
not care either.

The city was not sacked for wealth. It was **emptied**, and then it was left, and it is still standing
wherever it stands, and no Khazár has entered it since.

## Afterward

The Khazári worked out what had been done to them. It took a long time and they were thorough, and what
they arrived at was worse than a defeat.

They had not been beaten by the Grukar. **The Grukar had been used**, taken from their spawners by a
relic the Sinalë had known about and not mentioned, in the hands of one of that people, for a reason
that was — narrowly, unbearably — a reason.

The Sinalë dealt with him themselves, eventually, and handed over nothing and explained nothing. That is
the sundering, and it has held ever since. See [[miscgear-secondvoice|The Second Voice]].

## The Account

A remnant got out, and being Khazári they wrote it down: precisely, without adornment, with every name
lost and the exact order of what happened.

**Every Khazári hold keeps that record.** Not as history, which is a human use for the past — it sits in
the working archive beside the craft-records and the genealogies, it is read at intervals, and a Khazár
is expected to know it the way they are expected to know their own line.

The passage most often quoted is not about the shafts or the dead. It is the assessment of 3000 BF,
copied out entire, correct in every particular, dated two centuries before it stopped mattering.

## The Conclusion

This is what the record exists to transmit, and it is not revenge.

The Khazári know the Grukar were compelled. It is written down. They know the tribes were broken to make
the thing that killed Khazártúrn, that an Ahk cannot follow its own spawn and does not stop existing
afterward, and that no Grukar chose any part of it.

They kill them on sight anyway — everywhere, immediately, without negotiation and without the enormous
patience they extend to every other living thing in the world. Because the conclusion they reached was
not that Grukar are wicked. It was that **Grukar are ammunition**, that the Second Voice was lost rather
than destroyed, and that the only reliable defence against a weapon that may still exist is to ensure
there is nothing near a hold for it to pick up.

It is a policy of prevention, reasoned out by people who wrote down their reasons, executed for four and
a half thousand years against a species they know to be innocent, and never once revisited.
