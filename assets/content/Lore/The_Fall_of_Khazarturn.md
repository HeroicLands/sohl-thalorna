# The Fall of Khazártúrn

**Khazártúrn** was the greatest city the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] ever built, and its loss in
**2800 BF** is why they will not suffer [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] to live.

The Deep Folk reached Thalorna about **4280 BF**. Within five centuries they had chosen their ground
and begun, and by roughly **3800 BF** Khazártúrn stood: not a hold among holds but the work all their
other work was measured against, and the seat of the craft-records of an entire people.

It stood a thousand years.

## A New Thing in the World

Around **3000 BF** the Grukar appeared.

Where they came from is not known and the Khazári account does not speculate. What it records — with a
precision that reads, across five thousand years, like a man setting down evidence — is that they had
not been there before. There is no mention of Grukar in anything written at Khazártúrn in its first
seven hundred years, and a great deal was written.

The Khazári had **two centuries** to take the measure of them before it mattered. They used the time
well by their own standards: they observed, described, classified the castes, recorded the fission, and
concluded that these were a vicious but manageable vermin of the deep country.

They were wrong about one thing, and it was the only thing that counted.

## The Undermining

A hold cannot be forced. Khazári doors are the finest work the Deep Folk do and nothing on Thalorna
opens one from outside.

So the tribe never tried. Under a **Sha** — which thinks forward, and sees what another creature wants
and how the wanting can be made to serve — it went **beneath** Khazártúrn, and worked there for the
better part of a Khazári generation.

This is what the Khazári had failed to understand. A tribe does not tire, lose heart, need paying, or
die; it _replaces_. Its diggers hatch knowing how to dig. **Time, which defeats every siege, costs a
Grukar tribe nothing at all** — and while it dug it listened, mapping by sound what Khazártúrn's own
engineers had cut: the ventilation, the galleries, the water.

Twenty years of that went unnoticed beneath the most closely kept city in the world.

## Two Acts, One Day

They broke into the **outer galleries**, where the young were schooled and where the craft-records were
kept and copied.

And they took the **water** — not by cutting it, which the city could have answered, but by fouling the
cisterns from below, in a way that was not discovered for **eleven days**.

## The Doors

They did not kill the ones they took in the galleries.

They kept them alive, in the open, on the gallery floor before the great inner doors, where the city
could see them. And they made it last a very long time, because a Sha had understood exactly what a
Khazár is and exactly what a Khazár will not do.

The doors could be opened. Opening them would lose Khazártúrn.

**The doors stayed shut.** It was the correct decision. It was taken by people who could hear what was
being done on the other side of it, and held to until there was nothing left on the gallery floor to
hold out against.

## What It Bought

Nothing.

The cisterns had been fouled eleven days before the galleries fell. By the time the noise stopped the
city was already dying of it, and the survivors opened the doors after all — onto a gallery containing
nothing but what the Grukar had left — and fought out through a mine-cut that had not been used in
three hundred years.

Khazártúrn was not sacked. It was **emptied**. It is still standing, wherever it stands, and no Khazár
has entered it since.

## The Account

A remnant got out, and being Khazári they wrote down what had happened: precisely, without adornment,
with every name lost and the exact sequence of the eleven days. They carried it to the nearest hold and
it was copied, and it has been copied ever since.

**Every Khazári hold keeps that record.** It is not kept as history, which is a human use for the past.
It sits in the working archive beside the craft-records and the genealogies, and it is read at
intervals, and a Khazár is expected to know it the way they are expected to know their own line.

The passage most often quoted is not about the galleries or the doors. It is the line about 3000 BF:
_this was not in the world when we laid the foundations, and now it is._

This is what the [[lore-vardain|Vardain]] mean when they say the hatred is older and more particular
than territory. A Khazár of [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]] who has never seen a Grukar will kill one on
sight without deliberation, and it is neither grievance nor passion. **It is a conclusion** — reached
by people who wrote down their reasons, and never revisited.

## Afterward

The Khazári found that tribe. It took them a long time and they did not stop.

What was done to the Ahk is the only part of the account that was never written down.
