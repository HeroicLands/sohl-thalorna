# The Nome of Khensuret

## Overview

Khensuret is the great mortuary nome of the [[affiliation-uperivrnms|upper river]] — the place where [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]] tends its dead. Built around the temple-city of [[place-khensuret|Khensuret]] on the river's east bank and the vast necropolis that faces it across the water on the west, the nome is the beating heart of the empire's funerary civilization: its embalmers, its tomb-builders, its funerary artists, and the priesthoods of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Sēker]] and [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Ánubís]], lords of the passage into death. The empire's largest pilgrimage flows here, for to be buried in the sacred ground of Khensuret — or merely to make the journey and lay an offering — is held to weigh favorably when the heart is set against the feather of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Má'át]]. The nome is not large or rich in the worldly sense, but in the economy of the afterlife it is the capital of the world.

## Character

Khensuret is solemn, ancient, and wholly given over to death — not morbidly, for the Kheperi see death as renewal, but completely. The whole life of the nome organizes around the dead: the embalmers in their workshops on the west bank, the tomb-builders and quarrymen cutting and raising the houses of eternity, the funerary artists painting the walls that _are_ the afterlife made real, the coffin-makers and amulet-carvers and the keepers of the great rolls of mortuary spells. Above them all stand the Wer'Hekau of the mortuary temples, who command estates and labor-gangs rivaling the [[affiliation-empirtkhpr#Titles and Forms of Address|Haty'a]]'s own — and the central political fact of the nome is the wary sharing of power between the hereditary nomarch and the priesthoods of the dead. Pilgrims throng the temple-city year-round; the festival-calendar is dense with rites for the departed; and the whole place lives, paradoxically, very well off of dying well.

## Economy

The mortuary industry is the economy. Embalming and mummification, tomb construction and decoration, the manufacture of coffins, grave-goods, amulets, and funerary papyri, and the temple-pilgrimage trade together employ the overwhelming majority of the nome's people and draw wealth in from every corner of the empire — for the dead of distant nomes are sent here for proper preparation, and the rich of the whole realm pay to be buried, or at least commemorated, in sacred ground. Around this core the nome farms enough flood-silt grain to feed itself, quarries the stone its tomb-builders need from the western cliffs, and runs the natron-and-resin trades the embalmers depend on. It is a nome that exports the one service no one can finally do without.

## Notable Features

- The temple-city of [[place-khensuret|Khensuret]] and its great temples of Sēker and Ánubís
- The western-bank necropolis — the empire's most sacred burial ground
- The embalmers' workshops and the tomb-builders' settlements of the west bank
- The natron flats and resin-stores that supply the mummification trade
- The pilgrim-road and the festival of the dead that draws the whole empire

## Settlements

- **[[place-khensuret|Khensuret]]** (~90,000) — the nome capital and the empire's great temple-city of the dead; the mortuary temples, the pilgrim-markets, the embalmers' and coffin-makers' quarters, and the seat of the Haty'a in uneasy proximity to the Wer'Hekau.
- **[[place-setmaat|Set-Maat]]** (~6,000) — "the Place of Truth," the walled tomb-workers' settlement on the west bank; a closed company-town of the master-craftsmen, draughtsmen, and stonecutters who cut and paint the great tombs, hereditary in their trades and proud of their secrets.
- **The pilgrim and farm villages:** small communities of 500–2,500 line the east bank and the pilgrim-road — among them Per-Sekri, Hut-Anpu, Iat-Mut, and Sma-Djeser — lodging pilgrims, farming the river-margin, and serving the endless traffic of the dead and their mourners.

## For the Worldbuilder

Khensuret is the empire's uncanny heart, and it plays like nowhere else — a whole society organized around death, with all the wealth, secrecy, and dread that implies. Run it as a place of sacred industry and buried danger: the embalmers' guild-secrets, the tomb-workers' closed brotherhood at [[place-setmaat|Set-Maat]], the rivalry between the nomarch and the mortuary priesthoods, the endless war between the tomb-builders who hide the dead and the tomb-robbers who plunder them. The funerary magic here is real and the curses guarding the tombs are not always idle superstition. Adventure runs from the mundane — pilgrim-fleecing, grave-goods fraud, stolen mortuary papyri — to the genuinely eerie: what walks the necropolis after dark, what the deepest tombs were built to keep in as much as out, and what a desperate or greedy party finds when it goes down into the houses of eternity. What is abundant here is ritual, stone, and the dead; what is scarce is levity, and any tomb that has not already been robbed at least once.

## Commerce and Currency

Khensuret uses the [[lore-tkhprcrncy|Ta'Kheperu currency system]]. Much of its wealth moves as temple-account and pilgrim-offering rather than open trade, banked through the mortuary temples' own treasuries and the [[affiliation-perhati|Pér-Háti]] houses that serve them; the grave-goods and amulet trades, by contrast, deal briskly in struck copper and silver. See [[lore-tkhprcrncy|Ta'Kheperu Currency]] for the full system.
