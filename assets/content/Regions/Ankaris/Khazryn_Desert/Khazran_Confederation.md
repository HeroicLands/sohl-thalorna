The **Khazran Confederation** is what survives of the indigenous civilization of the [[doc-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]] — the people for whom the desert itself is named, the oasis-builders whose hereditary princes ruled the great walled cities for the better part of three thousand years, and the dispossessed nation that has been locked since the **Catastrophe** (four generations ago) in unresolved blood-feud with the [[doc-tribestrzd|Tribes of Ātárzád]] who took its choicest lands.

The Khazran are not a small people. There are perhaps eight hundred thousand of them in all — three hundred thousand still living on Khazran-held peripheral oases under three of the original seven princely houses; nearly five hundred thousand more living within the great Ātárzád-held cities (Zargandûr, Ashkarad, Kethramír, and the holy Oasis of Shirvan) as a subject class of merchants, craftsmen, water-engineers, scribes, physicians, and palm-grove tenders. The four other princely houses are in continuous exile, mostly in [[doc-sultntmrdd|Amradad]], where they have maintained their genealogies, their household priests, their court-ceremonial, and their explicit claim to the thrones from which their forebears were driven. The exile courts are not large — a few hundred souls each — but they are scrupulous about their continuity. A Khazran prince in Amradad today can recite the names of every ancestor back to the founding of his line.

To the Ātárzád, the Khazran are the people whose oases were promised to the Flame-born by Ātáröš and who must accept that the Promise has been fulfilled. To the Khazran, the Ātárzád are six generations of trespassers whose theology cannot create a fact that history did not establish. The land is named **Khazryn** because it is the land of the **Khazran**. No other claim outweighs that.

## Origins: The Ancient Settlement

Khazran tradition holds that the first Khazran came down from the [[doc-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]] — specifically from the **Northwestern Arc**, the great curving range that wraps the southwestern corner of the Khazryn between the Persianate oasis-belt and the Vedyari foothills further south — in the deep first age, settled the great springs of the Khazryn one by one as their lineages spread, and built the seven cities of which only the foundations of the original walls now remain — buried beneath the rebuilt walls of every successive generation. The seven cities, in order of their founding: **Shirvan** (the holiest, the first, founded at the great spring beneath the Mōrávar cliff), **Ashkarad**, **Zargandûr**, **Kethramír**, **Vahúrdash** (lost to the desert in a long-ago drought, ruins still visible in the deep interior), **Mehrnāgord**, and **Ushtra-bēr**.

Of these seven, three remain in Khazran hands today: **Mehrnāgord** in the western fringe, **Ushtra-bēr** in the deep north, and a small revived settlement at the ruins of **Vahúrdash** that has been kept barely alive by determined Khazran reclamation over the past generation. The other four — Shirvan, Ashkarad, Zargandûr, Kethramír — were taken by the Ātárzád in the War of the Twelve Years and have been Ātárzád-held since.

The Khazran civilization that flourished before the Catastrophe was urban, literate, polytheistic, and old enough to have absorbed and outlasted multiple waves of nomadic incursion. Khazran scribal records — kept on baked-clay tablets in the [[Khazri_Language|Khazri]] tongue, which uses a syllabic script descended from an ancient mountain-script — go back nearly two thousand years before the Catastrophe in continuous archive. The Mōbads of the Great Temple of Āhúrdáén at Shirvan maintained the calendar of the Khazran kingdoms, the records of dynastic succession, the treaties between the seven cities, and the great theological commentary tradition that gave Āsháian theology much of its shape outside the Sultanate. Most of this archive was burned in the fall of Shirvan; what survived was carried out by the fleeing princely households and is now scattered across the exile courts in Amradad.

## The Catastrophe

The events the Ātárzád call the Conquest and the War of the Twelve Years, the Khazran call the **Catastrophe** (in Khazri, **Vēštákán** — "the Breaking"). The narrative emphasis is sharply different. To the Khazran:

- The Ātárzád came as refugees, were treated by the Khazran cities with the hospitality the desert peoples extend to all wanderers in extremity, and were settled in marginal lands at the southwestern fringe under princely patronage.
- They paid this hospitality back by waiting until they had grown strong enough, then turning on their hosts in a religious war they had been planning from the beginning.
- The conquest was characterized by extraordinary brutality: the systematic demolition of Khazran temples (with the stones reused for Ātárzád fire-temples — a calculated insult), the proscription of public Āhúrdáén worship, the slaughter of the Mōbads who refused to flee, the desecration of the great spring at Shirvan with sacrificial fires built directly over the holy waters.
- The war was not won by Ātárzád military skill but by Khazran civilizational decline: the seven-city confederation had been weakening for generations, the princely houses were in factional conflict, and the Sultanate of Amradad — which should have intervened — was distracted by its own succession crisis at the critical moment.
- The "Promise" the Ātárzád cite is a legal fiction. No god has the authority to give one people the land of another. Even Āhúrdáén, the supreme deity of the entire pantheon, would not violate the principle of ancestral right. The Sacred Flame is a real god, the keeper of fire, and as a fire-god he is honored in his place — but he is not a sovereign who can grant kingdoms.

The Catastrophe is recited at every Khazran festival, every formal court ceremonial, and every funeral of a senior princely member. It is the foundational wound around which the entire surviving Khazran identity is organized.

## Government

The Khazran Confederation is governed by the **Council of the Princes**, which meets annually at Amradad — neutral ground, hosted by the Sultanate as a courtesy to the exile community. Each of the seven princely houses sends its head (or, in the case of the four in exile, the man or woman who would be ruling if the house were restored), the senior elders of the house, and the senior Mōbad attached to the house's court. The High Mōbad of Āhúrdáén at the Great Fire-Temple in Amradad presides as neutral convenor — a role institutionalized formally about sixty years ago when the Sultanate's then-Mōbad, Aravdán the Stargazer's predecessor, agreed to underwrite the Khazran council in perpetuity.

The seven princely houses are:

- **House of Shirvan** (in exile in Amradad). The senior house, traditionally first among the seven; by long custom the eldest Shirvan prince speaks first at every Council. The current heir-in-exile is **Mōrá-Shirván**, a woman of perhaps forty, scholar and theologian, who has spent her life building a comprehensive legal-historical case for the eventual recovery of Shirvan. The case fills a small library and is updated continuously.
- **House of Ashkarad** (in exile in Amradad). The traditional merchant-house; held the most cosmopolitan court before the Catastrophe. The current head, **Sāmīm of Ashkarad**, is a businessman who has grown wealthy on Vylarian Sea trade and quietly funds Khazran agitation in the Ātárzád cities.
- **House of Zargandûr** (in exile in Amradad). The traditional war-house; held the strongest military before the Catastrophe and accordingly suffered the most thorough destruction of its leadership. The current heir, **Khávar of Zargandûr**, is a young man of twenty-seven who has been training as a soldier in the Sultanate's forces and is widely (and quietly) understood to be preparing for an eventual reclamation campaign.
- **House of Kethramír** (in exile in Amradad). The traditional priest-house; closest to the Mōbadate before the Catastrophe and accordingly the most theologically serious of the exile houses. The current head, **Yázdín of Kethramír**, is herself a Mōbad of considerable reputation and has produced major commentaries on the Catastrophe and its theological implications.
- **House of Mehrnāgord** (still ruling). Holds the oasis of Mehrnāgord on the western fringe, perhaps thirty thousand people. The current ruling prince, **Pārván of Mehrnāgord**, is in his sixties, cautious, and inclined to accommodation with the Sultanate and (warily) with the Ātárzád. He is considered insufficiently militant by the exile houses.
- **House of Ushtra-bēr** (still ruling). Holds Ushtra-bēr in the deep northern Khazryn, perhaps twenty thousand people. Remote, austere, and deeply traditional. The current ruler, **Vāhrām of Ushtra-bēr**, is a hard man of fifty whose court ceremonial is said to preserve forms that have been forgotten elsewhere.
- **House of Vahúrdash** (revived, partially ruling). The Vahúrdash settlement is small (perhaps four thousand) and was reclaimed only in the last generation from desert ruins; the current head, **Khoršád-Vahūr**, holds the title by recent acclamation rather than ancient right and is considered a kind of seventh-among-equals at the Council.

The Council does not, in practice, govern. It coordinates. The three still-ruling oases govern themselves; the four exile houses maintain their formal courts and operate diplomatically and commercially in Amradad and beyond. What the Council _does_ maintain is the unified diplomatic position toward the Ātárzád (no recognition, no formal treaty, perpetual claim of sovereignty over the conquered cities) and the institutional infrastructure of the diaspora — the legal codes by which Khazran communities within the Ātárzád cities govern their internal affairs, the funding of Khazran shrines and schools in Amradad and Haradian ports, and the quiet network of agents and intelligencers that keeps the exile houses informed about conditions in the lost cities.

## The Diaspora and the Subject Population

The Khazran civilization survived the Catastrophe in two main ways: through the small peripheral oases that the Ātárzád did not bother to take, and through the substantial subject population that remained in the conquered cities under Ātárzád rule. The latter is by far the more numerous group, and it is the central fact of the Khazran condition today.

Within the four Ātárzád-held cities, Khazran communities occupy designated quarters — generally the older pre-Catastrophe districts, which the Ātárzád settler-class has not extensively redeveloped. They are not slaves and have not been since two generations after the Conquest, when the Council of the Twelve abolished the formal categories of bondage that had attached to the surviving Khazran population in the immediate aftermath of the war. They are, however, subject in concrete ways: barred from holding land outside their designated quarters, barred from public worship of any of the Āsháian gods other than (oddly) Ātáröš himself, barred from carrying arms outside the militia organizations the Ātárzád permit, and required to pay the **water-tax** — a tribute on the use of city water systems that applies only to Khazran heads of household and that is widely understood to be punitive in intent.

What the Khazran subject population _has_, and what makes them indispensable, is professional expertise. The Khazran are the city's water-engineers, irrigation-masters, and palm-grove cultivators — the technical professions on which oasis civilization depends. They are also the city's principal physicians, scribes (in three languages), commercial bankers, and astronomers. Most Ātárzád courts of any sophistication employ Khazran technical specialists; most Ātárzád merchant houses use Khazran bookkeepers; the Mōbadān-Shōfár himself is widely known to consult Khazran astronomers when the Unconsuming Flame must be observed for omens.

This dependence is the central structural fact of the Ātárzád-Khazran relationship and the principal reason why the Ātárzád rebellion-suppressions, however brutal, have always stopped short of the population-level violence that would actually depopulate the Khazran communities. The Ātárzád need the Khazran to keep the cities running. The Khazran need the Ātárzád to permit them to remain in their ancestral cities. Neither finds the arrangement comfortable; both have learned, painfully and reluctantly, to live with it.

## Religion

The Khazran practice the **full [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian pantheon]]** with all twelve deities honored in their proper places — Āhúrdáén supreme, Ātáröš the Sacred Flame as one of the twelve and the means by which Āhúrdáén is made manifest in the world, the others (Mítharä, Khóršádrä, Zúráthrä, Báhrámiš, Ázráván, Zárványä, Árávēštä, the rest) honored at their seasons and according to their domains. This is the original form of the Āsháian faith as the Khazran Mōbads systematized it over centuries before the Catastrophe, and it is the form still maintained today by the High Mōbad of Āhúrdáén at the Great Fire-Temple in Amradad and by the Khazran-tradition Mōbadate generally.

Particular Khazran devotional emphases:

- **Āhúrdáén** is the supreme object of formal devotion. The Khazran tradition insists that no other god — including Ātáröš — can rightly receive the formal weekly observance that is owed to the Keeper of Harmony. This is the central theological point of dispute with the Ātárzád: where the Ātárzád elevate Ātáröš to exclusive worship, the Khazran insist that doing so violates the cosmic order Āhúrdáén himself established.
- **Zúráthrä** the Mother of the Oasis receives the deepest popular devotion in Khazran life. Every Khazran oasis (and every Khazran quarter within an Ātárzád city) maintains a shrine to Zúráthrä at its principal water-source. Every Khazran wedding involves a Zúráthrä blessing. Every Khazran child is presented at a Zúráthrä shrine within forty days of birth. The Khazran's intimate identification with the oasis itself — water, palm, life — runs through their devotion to Zúráthrä, and the Ātárzád's destruction of the great Zúráthrä shrine at Shirvan is among the most bitter wounds of the Catastrophe.
- **The Mōbadate** is the Khazran institutional religious authority. Mōbads are educated for fifteen to twenty years before ordination, expected to master astronomy, mathematics, theology, law, and at least three languages, and serve in a quasi-judicial capacity within their communities. The High Mōbadate at Amradad, currently held by **Aravdán the Stargazer**, is the senior religious authority for the entire Khazran Confederation and (formally, though the Ātárzád deny it) for all Āsháian peoples without exception.
- **Calendar**: The Khazran maintain the ancient Khazran calendar — twelve thirty-day months plus a five-day intercalary festival at the spring equinox — which differs significantly from the Vylarian Common Calendar and from the Ātárzád ritual calendar. Most Khazran in the Ātárzád cities track both calendars; most of those in the still-Khazran oases use only the Khazran calendar.

## Economy

In the still-Khazran oases — Mehrnāgord, Ushtra-bēr, the small Vahúrdash settlement — the economy is the traditional oasis economy: dates, palm-sugar, garden produce, salt (in Mehrnāgord's case), and trade with passing caravans. These are small economies; the populations are not large, the surpluses are modest, and the wealth of the still-Khazran lands is reckoned in continuity rather than in volume.

The substantial Khazran economic activity takes place in the diaspora and the subject communities. Khazran merchant-houses, primarily based in Amradad but with branches in Vylarian Sea ports, handle a meaningful share of the Sultanate's external trade and a near-monopoly on certain specialty goods — Khazran wines (rare, but prized), Khazran scholarly books, Khazran scientific instruments. The Khazran professional class within the Ātárzád cities is paid for its expertise by Ātárzád employers, and a significant fraction of those wages flow back to the Council of the Princes through informal channels as donations to the perpetuation of the genealogies, the maintenance of the exile courts, and the funding of Khazran schools.

The Khazran do not pay taxes to any state. They pay — by ancient compact — a tithe to the Mōbadate, which funds the temples, the educational institutions, and the legal-administrative apparatus of the Confederation as a whole.

## Defense

The Khazran maintain no army. The three still-ruling princely houses each retain a small household guard — perhaps a hundred men in Mehrnāgord, fifty in Ushtra-bēr, a token force at Vahúrdash — adequate for ordinary security and for ceremonial purposes but not for any sustained military operation. The exile houses maintain small protective retinues in Amradad, theoretically forbidden by Sultanate law from any military activity but in practice tolerated as long as they remain discreet.

The Khazran's effective security depends on (a) the protection of the Sultanate, which has consistently refused to extradite Khazran exiles or to cooperate with Ātárzád requests for action against the Confederation, and (b) the strategic dependence of the Ātárzád cities on Khazran technical labor, which acts as a structural deterrent against Ātárzád campaigns of destruction.

Khávar of Zargandûr's quiet preparations toward an eventual reclamation campaign are the only serious military planning the Confederation conducts. Whether such a campaign will ever materialize, and whether it could possibly succeed, are questions the Council does not openly debate but does privately assess. The current consensus is: not in this generation.

## The Blood-Feud

(See [[doc-tribestrzd#The Blood-Feud|the Ātárzád article]] for the structural account.)

The Khazran view of the blood-feud is that it is not a feud at all — it is a pending case of stolen property, in which the rightful owners are simply waiting for the moment when restitution becomes possible. The Khazran do not seek revenge; they seek **return**. The exile princes of Shirvan, Ashkarad, Zargandûr, and Kethramír would, if they could, simply walk back into their cities, take possession of the palaces of their forebears, restore the dismantled temples of Āhúrdáén and Zúráthrä, and resume the government their families exercised for centuries before the Catastrophe. They have no interest in retribution against ordinary Ātárzád settlers, whom they would (so they say) tolerate as a subject population in their reclaimed cities — much as the Khazran have been tolerated under Ātárzád rule, but with somewhat more dignity.

Whether this magnanimity would survive contact with the actuality of reconquest is, of course, doubtful. The current generation of Khazran exiles has lived with the loss for ninety years. The temptations of revenge would not be small.

## Famous Figures

**Aravdán the Stargazer**, High Mōbad of Āhúrdáén at the Great Fire-Temple in Amradad, presiding convenor of the Council of the Princes, senior religious authority of the entire Khazran Confederation. About sixty years old, an astronomer of continental reputation, and the man whose moral and political weight has held the Khazran exile community together through three decades of small crises. (Note: also exercises significant influence within the Sultanate of Amradad's secular government — see [[doc-sultntmrdd|Sultanate of Amradad]].)

**Mōrá-Shirván**, heir-in-exile of the senior princely house. Theologian, legal historian, and the author of the comprehensive case for Shirvan's restoration that has become the central document of Khazran political claims. Forty years old, unmarried, considered by some exile factions as a possible focal point for a more activist phase in Khazran diplomacy.

**Khávar of Zargandûr**, the young heir-in-exile training in Sultanate military service. Twenty-seven, ambitious, and the figure around whom the more militant exile faction increasingly organizes. The Council has not formally endorsed his preparations but has not forbidden them either.

**Yázdín of Kethramír**, Mōbad and head of the Kethramír exile house. The most theologically rigorous of the exile leaders and the principal author of the formal theological refutation of the Ātárzád "Promise" doctrine. Her commentary, _On the Ungrantable Land_, is required reading in Khazran religious education and is contraband in Ātárzád-held territory.

**Pārván of Mehrnāgord**, ruling prince of the largest still-Khazran oasis. The voice of accommodation in the Council. Has been tentatively explored by Ātárzád merchant interests as a possible interlocutor for limited normalization arrangements; has not yet given a definitive answer in either direction.

**The Hidden Mōbad of Shirvan**, an unnamed (and possibly mythical) figure said to maintain the original ritual office at a secret location near the Shirvan spring, conducting the proper observances for Āhúrdáén in defiance of the Ātárzád prohibition. The Khazran of the Shirvan quarter believe the Hidden Mōbad exists and that the line of succession has been maintained continuously since the Catastrophe; the Ātárzád periodically launch search-operations and have never found anyone. Whether the Hidden Mōbad exists or is a useful myth, the belief itself is a non-trivial fact about Khazran religious life under Ātárzád rule.

## Commerce and Currency

Khazran Confederation's commerce operates primarily through [[doc-bartercnmy|barter]] and [[doc-kinhalcrdt|tribal credit]] customs, mediated through the caravan trade that crosses the steppe. The urbanized trading-cities of the Confederation have begun to develop modest moneylender activity along Vedyaran lines (see [[doc-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]]) but the tribal interior runs almost entirely on barter and on the elaborate inter-clan credit relationships that the [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|Warrior's Circle]] occasionally adjudicates. Foreign coin (Vylarian Argo from western trade, Ta'Kheperan deben pieces from southern trade) circulates by intrinsic-metal value among traders.
