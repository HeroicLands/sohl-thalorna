# Zûravlen Region

**Zûravlen** is a barony of the [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provènzia]], in the hills of the
[[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]] behind the coast — a single long valley, and the source of the
finest peaches in Ankaris. Its fruit reaches tables in
[[place-batarencity|Batáren]] and beyond, its name is a guarantee of quality among fruiterers who have
never been within a hundred leagues of it, and the whole of its public life is arranged around a tree
that bears for eight weeks a year.

The barony is held of the Crown at [[place-lunacorte|Lunacorte]] by **Álegar alrí Zûravel**, who is
also Baron of Kývarel, and who sits at [[place-mercadaure|Mercadaure]]. His clan's prominent support of the Order of the Burning Brand gives
a small agricultural barony rather more weight in Provenzian affairs than its size would suggest.

## The Valley

The Zûravlen river runs the length of the barony from the uplands to the plain, and the valley's
wealth is made by water. South-facing slopes have been cut into **terraces** over some four hundred
years, and a system of channels — the _règa_ — carries river water along the terrace lines to every
orchard in turn. Water-turns are allotted by custom, recorded at Mercadaure, and are the single most
litigated property in the barony: a family's water-turn is worth more than its house.

Above the terraces the ground turns to thin pasture, chestnut and scrub, running up to summer grazing
in the hills. Below, the valley floor is flat, well-watered and given to grain.

## The Peach

The trees are grafted, not grown from stone. Zûravlen stock is worked onto **almond root**, which
tolerates the dry stony ground of the upper terraces where peach root will not thrive, and the
grafting is done in winter by men whose families have done nothing else for generations. A tree comes
into bearing in its fourth year and is finished by its twentieth, so a third of the barony's orchards
are always young, and an orchard's age is the first thing any Zûravlen will tell you about it.

**Fresh fruit does not travel.** A peach picked at Vergéval is worth a fortune in Válaren and is
worthless four days later, so the fresh trade is a prestige trade — fast riders, iced crates, noble
households and nothing else. What actually leaves the valley in quantity is preserved:

- **Dried halves**, stoned and laid on cane trays in the drying yards, which keep a year and travel
  anywhere.
- **Fruit in honey**, sealed in crocks — the luxury export, and the reason Zûravlen honey is never
  sold outside the barony.
- **Kernels**, cracked from the stones and sold to confectioners and apothecaries for their bitter
  almond flavour.
- **Peach spirit**, distilled in small quantities under the apothecaries' eye, expensive and
  medicinal.

## The Rest of the Year

The peach bears for eight weeks. The barony eats for fifty-two, and an outsider who thinks Zûravlen is
an orchard has seen it in August.

- **Grain** — wheat and barley on the valley floor, which is what the barony actually lives on.
- **Wool and cheese** — flocks go up to the hill pastures for the summer and come down in autumn.
  Wool is the barony's steadiest income and its most reliable one, since it does not spoil and does
  not care about frost. Ewe's-milk cheese is pressed in the uplands and sold at the autumn fair.
- **Honey and wax** — bee-boles are kept beside every orchard. Nobody in Zûravlen will explain why
  beyond that it has always been done, and the honey is needed for the preserving in any case. The wax
  goes to the churches.
- **Vines and olives** on the lower slopes, for local use rather than export.
- **Osier** — willow beds along the river, cut and woven into the baskets and trays the harvest is
  impossible without. Basketry is a winter trade and employs half the valley.
- **Chestnut** in the uplands, for flour, fodder and timber.

**The manure question** is the barony's permanent quarrel. Orchard terraces are hungry and must be
dunged; the dung is in the flocks; the flocks belong to men who are not orchardists. Every manor's
custom sets out where and when the sheep must be folded after they come down, and every generation
argues about it.

## The Year

| Season       | The valley                                                                                   |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Winter       | Pruning and grafting; basket-weaving; ploughing the floor; flocks folded on orchard terraces |
| Early spring | **Blossom**, and the frost-vigil; lambing; sowing                                            |
| Late spring  | Thinning the set fruit — brutal, unpopular, and the whole crop depends on it                 |
| Summer       | Flocks up to the hills; the first early varieties come in                                    |
| Late summer  | **Harvest**, and the drying yards run day and night                                          |
| Autumn       | The Mercadaure fair; vintage and olives; flocks come down; the wool clip goes to market      |

## The Frost Vigil

A late frost during blossom destroys the year, and everyone in the valley knows it. On nights when
frost threatens, the bells at Mercadaure are rung and the whole barony turns out to light **smudge
fires** along the terrace rows — damp straw and prunings, banked to smoke rather than burn — and to
keep them going until dawn. From the hills the valley appears to be alight from end to end.

The vigil is a religious occasion as much as an agricultural one. Priests of
[[affiliation-florania|Flórania]] walk the rows through the night, and it is bad luck and worse
manners to refuse a call. Baron Álegar's people note, without ever quite saying it, how well the
practice sits with the Order of the Burning Brand.

## Faith

Provènzia is [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]], and Zûravlen's devotion falls naturally to two of
the Twelve: **[[affiliation-venusia|Vénusia]]**, the Bountiful One, whose symbol is a tree hung with
golden fruit and who is honoured at harvest with the first-picked basket of every orchard; and
**[[affiliation-florania|Flórania]]**, the Nurturer, who owns the blossom and the vigil. A Zûravlen
will tell you the two goddesses divide the year between them, and that Vénusia gets the credit for
what Flórania saved.

## Settlements

- **[[place-mercadaure|Mercadaure]]** — the market town and the baron's seat, where the fruit is
  graded, packed and sold, and where the autumn fair is held.
- **[[place-vergeval|Vergéval]]** — the great orchard manor of the upper terraces, and the richest and
  most anxious place in the barony.
- **[[place-blavera|Blavéra]]** — a mixed manor of the valley floor: grain, fold and a little fruit.
  The ordinary model, and the one most of Zûravlen actually resembles.
- **[[place-estivren|Estivren]]** — the upland sheep manor, oldest of the three, poorest in coin and
  least interested in peaches.

## Adventuring in Zûravlen

The barony is small, rich, and utterly dependent on a crop that can be destroyed in one cold night —
which makes it unusually easy to hurt and unusually worth hurting. Water-turns, folding rights,
drying-yard shares and the grading table at Mercadaure are all worth killing over on a small scale, and
all of them are written down somewhere that could be altered. See
[[scenario-pechszrvln|The Peaches of Zûravlen]].
