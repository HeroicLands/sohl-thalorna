# Valdûn

## Overview

Valdûn lies deep within the verdant valleys of central Élavendre, a town of timber buildings with high-peaked roofs and gardens that seem to overflow with impossible abundance. The town serves as a regional center for the surrounding agricultural lands—farms and vineyards that produce the wines and crops for which Élavendre is renowned—yet it maintains a dreamlike quality that marks all settlements in this realm. Valdûn's markets are less boisterous than those of continental cities; vendors conduct business with a kind of unhurried grace, and there is an almost ceremonial quality to even simple transactions.

The spiritual life of Valdûn is woven into daily existence in ways outsiders find difficult to articulate. The Aurèldían temples conduct their services in the morning, as is traditional across the western realms, but the town also honors the older fae traditions through small shrines in groves and beside springs, and through festivals that follow no calendar that can be rationally explained—they occur when the fae influence seems strongest, marked by portents that only local residents can read. Stories of Valdûn speak of travelers who lost their way in the surrounding forests for weeks, only to emerge into the town square having experienced what felt like a single evening.

The town is governed by a council of elders that includes the local priest of the Aurèldían temple, the headmaster of the local academy, and three "silence-keepers"—individuals whose true function is never explained to outsiders, though their presence at council meetings seems essential. Valdûn maintains peaceful relations with the smaller fae enclaves in the surrounding forests; there is a tacit understanding that the boundaries of human settlement will not expand further, and that in return, the fae will not disrupt human life. This balance has held for generations, and both communities seem to prefer silence and mystery to direct negotiation.
