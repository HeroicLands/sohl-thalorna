The **Dhanurkota Janapada** is the federation of fourteen villages of the upper Sarvada river valley, governed jointly through the great hall of the **Mahájaya temple** at Dhanurkota — the "Bow-Fort" town from which the janapada takes its name. Its population is approximately eighteen thousand; its territory occupies a long defensible curve of the Sarvada where the river emerges from the northern hill country into the inland plain. It is one of perhaps eighty similar janapadas comprising the [[doc-janpdsvdyr|Mahā-Saṅgha of Vedyara]], and it is not large or wealthy by Vedyari standards. What it has, and what it is known for across the region, is the **Bow** — the four ancient archery academies whose graduates have for fifteen centuries staffed the elite missile units of every major Vedyari kingdom.

## The Bow-Fort

The town of Dhanurkota is built on a low fortified hill where the Sarvada bends west around an outcrop of red rock. The fort itself is older than the janapada — older, indeed, than the Mahā-Saṅgha, older than the temples, older perhaps than the Vedyari language itself. Its lowest courses are megalithic, of a construction-style that no living mason knows how to reproduce; the upper walls have been rebuilt many times in successive Vedyari styles. The fort encloses the **Mahájaya temple**, the four academy halls, the sabhā chamber, and the granary; the town proper spreads down the slope below the walls and along the riverbank.

The fort's name has always been Dhanurkota. The original meaning of the word is contested — some scholars derive _dhanur_ from the bow that has been the janapada's emblem since before recorded history, others from a much older root meaning a bend or curve in a river. The latter is probably more accurate to the fort's original naming; the bow-association came later, after the academies were founded, and is now ineradicable.

## The Four Academies

The four ancient archery academies of Dhanurkota are:

- **The Academy of the High Draw** — the oldest, founded in the dim past, which teaches the great longbow tradition. Its graduates serve in heavy infantry-support roles.
- **The Academy of the Swift Hand** — the cavalry-archery tradition, mounted bow from horseback. Its graduates are sought by every Vedyari kingdom that fields cavalry.
- **The Academy of the Patient Eye** — the marksmanship tradition, accuracy at extreme range. Its graduates serve as scouts, hunters, and fortress sharpshooters; many become the personal bowmen of Vedyari nobility.
- **The Academy of the Hidden String** — the irregular-warfare tradition, ambush and concealment archery. Its graduates serve in scout and special-operations capacities and are unusually difficult to recruit, since the Academy's discipline includes a vow of selectivity that allows it to refuse contracts it considers dishonorable.

Each academy maintains its own master, its own hereditary teaching lineages, and its own set of distinctive practices. They cooperate (loosely) within the janapada and compete (intensely) for students, prestige, and the reserved seats they collectively hold on the sabhā. The four masters are by long convention seated together at every formal sabhā session, and their joint pronouncements on military matters carry decisive weight in the janapada's foreign-policy decisions.

## Society

Dhanurkota's social structure is the standard Vedyari caste arrangement, but with the warrior caste (kshatriya-equivalent) unusually well-represented and unusually wealthy by janapada standards. About one in eight Dhanurkoti is of the warrior caste, compared to perhaps one in twenty in a typical Vedyari janapada. The wealth flows from the academy fees paid by aspiring archers from across Vedyara, the long-term retainer fees paid by patron kingdoms whose officer corps draws on Dhanurkota graduates, and the prestige donations made to the Mahájaya temple by those who have benefited from the academies' training.

The other castes are present in normal proportions. The cultivator villages along the Sarvada produce rice, sugarcane, sesame, and the cotton that feeds the local weaver-caste workshops. The merchant caste handles the considerable trade in arms (Dhanurkoti bows and arrows are exported across Vedyara) and in academy-related logistics (provisioning, lodging, equipment maintenance). The priestly caste is concentrated in and around the Mahájaya temple, with the senior priest holding the convening authority of the sabhā.

Dhanurkota maintains an unusual degree of social mobility for a Vedyari polity. The academies accept students from any caste, including the lowest, and a graduate of the academies — regardless of birth — is granted certain warrior-caste prerogatives within the janapada (the right to bear arms, the right to hold land in his own name, the right to participate in sabhā discussions on military matters). This is a long-standing janapada tradition that the Vedyari classical-orthodox occasionally find scandalous and that the sabhā has consistently defended as essential to the academies' continued excellence.

## Religion

The **Mahájaya temple** at Dhanurkota is the religious heart of the janapada and one of the major Mahájaya temples of inland Vedyara. Mahájaya — the preserver-deity of the [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varṇaka pantheon]], lord of order, prosperity, and the maintenance of dharma — is an unusually appropriate patron for a janapada whose identity centers on disciplined martial training in service of legitimate authority. The temple maintains the standard Mahājaya devotional cycle (daily, weekly, seasonal, and annual rites) and adds certain janapada-specific observances: the **Festival of the Drawn Bow** at the spring equinox, when each year's incoming academy students present themselves at the temple for blessing; the **Festival of the Returning** in autumn, when academy graduates returning from service abroad ritually re-enter the janapada and are welcomed; and the long **Watch of the Bow-String** held the night before any major military engagement involving Dhanurkoti graduates anywhere in Vedyara, when the temple's bowmen-priests maintain a vigil for the safety of their alumni.

Subsidiary shrines to other Varṇaka deities exist throughout the constituent villages. The temple of **Vyālendra** the creator at the Sarvada source-spring is a popular pilgrimage site for those seeking children; the **Rásikara** shrine in the cremation-ground outside the town receives the dead.

## Economy

Dhanurkota's economy rests on three pillars: agriculture (the standard Vedyari rice-cotton-sugarcane mix, providing subsistence and modest export surplus), bow and arrow manufacture (a distinctive specialty, with hereditary bowyer-fletcher families whose products are exported across Vedyara), and the academy-and-retainer income system. Of the three, the academies produce the smallest direct revenue but the greatest indirect benefit — through the pilgrim-traffic of academy aspirants and their families, the constant inflow of patron-kingdom payments for graduate services, and the steady stream of donations to the Mahájaya temple.

The janapada is moderately wealthy by janapada standards but not rich by city-state standards. Most of its wealth is held by the temple and the warrior-caste lineages; the cultivator villages live at standards typical of inland Vedyara.

## Defense

Dhanurkota has, as one would expect, an unusually strong defensive capacity for a janapada of its size. The four academies maintain their own training-corps, which collectively can field perhaps eight hundred elite archers on short notice. The villages can muster another two thousand fighting men of the warrior caste. The fortifications of the bow-fort itself, with the megalithic foundations rendering them effectively unbreachable to any siege technique short of sustained heavy magic, provide a refuge for the entire janapada population in extreme need.

In practice, Dhanurkota has not been seriously threatened in three centuries. Its reputation is its primary defense; no neighboring kingdom wants to test what eight hundred elite archers fighting on home ground would cost. The only Dhanurkoti military engagements of recent memory have been Dhanurkoti academy-graduate units serving abroad in the wars of patron kingdoms.

## Relations

Dhanurkota maintains formal patron-client relations with three neighboring kingdoms (the small mountain kingdoms whose names rotate as their dynasties rise and fall) and informal supplier-client relations with most of the major Vedyari city-states, including [[doc-chandrapur|Chandrapur]] and the inland branches of the great coastal merchant houses. Its relationship with [[doc-vyalendra2|Vyālendra]] is particularly close — Vyālendra has historically retained a Dhanurkoti graduate as commander of its city watch for as long as anyone can remember, and the two polities maintain a continuous diplomatic correspondence on military and commercial matters.

Within the [[doc-janpdsvdyr|Mahā-Saṅgha]], Dhanurkota's reputation is high. The senior priest of the Mahájaya temple is one of the seniormost voices at every Mahā-Mela, and the four academy masters together are routinely consulted on inter-janapada military questions.

## Commerce and Currency

Dhanurkota Janapada uses the [[doc-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran banking system]] administered through the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]]. Foreign currencies (Vylarian Argo and Aurion, Ta'Kheperan deben pieces) are exchangeable at frontier moneylenders at Collective-controlled rates, but the Vedyaran system is the dominant medium of internal commerce.
