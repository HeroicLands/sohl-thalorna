## Overview

K'uxi Balam, the "Second Jaguar Land," sits at a threshold—one foot planted firmly in the heights and civilization of the upper southern territories, the other pointing toward the harsh, forsaken wastes that lie beyond. The region is a frontier realm, vast and relatively thinly settled compared to the dense city-states of [[affiliation-balamtzaku|Ba'alam Tza'ku]]. Here, the grasslands stretch in endless waves, broken by isolated highlands and the scattered outpost-cities of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itzáni]] priesthood.

This is a land of transition and warning. The great ceremonial roads that bind [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]] together grow less certain here, their magnificent stones sometimes cracked by forces that priests attribute to the anger of [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Tz'uqil Ix'bal]], goddess of death, or the chaotic wrath of [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|P'uqil Ch'aqun]], master of storms. The Ch'um Ix'lan relay network thins here as well; Ch'umbal way-stations are spaced further apart, runners are harder to recruit for the harsh southern routes, and messages from [[affiliation-balamkul|Balamkul]] that arrive in two days at [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] take five or six to reach K'uxi Balam's outpost cities. Beyond K'uxi Balam, the Speaking Road falls silent entirely — the last tz'ib'al cord to arrive is decoded at [[place-kawilulkik|K'awi'il'ul Ki'ik]], and what lies further south relies on the courage of individual messengers. Those who venture south from K'uxi Balam do so with offerings and prayers heavy on their lips.

## Geography

The dominant feature of K'uxi Balam is an immense high plateau that dominates the landscape—the "altiplano," where cold winds sweep across sparse grasslands dotted with hardy shrubs and low vegetation. The air grows thin here; those from the lowlands gasp for breath on their first ascent. The plateau sits beneath an open sky where the heavens seem closer, and shamans and priests travel here to commune with celestial forces.

Vast herds of llama-analogs and alpaca-analogs roam these grasslands, tended by pastoral peoples who have adapted to the high-altitude existence. Deep in the plateau lie hidden valleys where settlements cluster near freshwater springs and sheltered gorges. The great lake of [[place-ixkulkul|Ixkul K'ul]] dominates the region's eastern reaches—a vast, sacred body of water said to be the eye of the world, where the boundary between the mortal and divine grows thin.

To the south and west, the plateau gradually descends, the grasses thicken into scrubland, and the temperature begins to rise. The transition is slow and uneasy—the land does not offer gentle gradations here, but rather abrupt drops, twisted rock formations, and increasing signs of the cosmic wound that has soured the very earth farther south.

## Maritime Powers

K'uxi Balam holds no maritime territories in the traditional sense, but the great sacred lake of [[place-ixkulkul|Ixkul K'ul]] is its heart and spiritual center. The waters of the lake are controlled entirely by the priesthood, and only those who have been properly blessed may venture onto its surface. Small ceremonial boats and pilgrimage vessels travel the sacred waters, but war canoes and merchant galleons do not traverse these holy depths.

The plateau itself is bordered to the north by [[affiliation-balamtzaku|Ba'alam Tza'ku]] and to the south by the increasingly hostile terrain of [[affiliation-kawiltzakl|K'awiil Tza'kul]]. Control of the major passes and highland routes is contested between the great cities, though the priesthood's authority usually prevails in matters of territorial claim.

## Trade

Trade in K'uxi Balam flows primarily north and south along the sacred roads, though the volume is far less than in the wealthier regions to the north. The pastoralist peoples herd llama and alpaca, producing fine wool that is woven into textiles and exported northward. The high grasslands produce hardy grain analogs, freeze-dried tubers, and medicinal plants said to cure altitude sickness and sharpen the mind.

The main trade goods flowing through K'uxi Balam are provisions for travelers heading to and from the far south—dried meats, cold-weather cloaks, strong cords, and the dried coca-analog that helps climbers and those at altitude breathe more easily. The great fortress-city of [[place-kawilulkik|K'awi'il'ul Ki'ik]] stands as the primary staging point for expeditions into the wastes, and merchants there profit enormously from outfitting southern-bound adventurers, priests, and warriors.

The priesthood maintains strict control over K'uxi Balam's resources. Large portions of the pastoral herds and grain harvests are claimed as tribute or tithe, and little of true wealth leaves the region—most is hoarded in temple treasuries or consumed in ritual sacrifice. This has created a frontier mentality; the cities are garrison-like, their economies oriented toward survival and the provisioning of expeditions rather than the accumulation of luxury goods.
