# Crime Syndicates of Thalorna

Unlike some settings where a single monolithic "thieves' guild" spans the known world, Thalorna's criminal underworld is fractured, competitive, and deeply regional. Four major syndicates operate across the Vylarian Sea basin, with a fifth — the Hëk Lōt Zhāk — dominating the far east in the [[doc-tanvurempr|Empire of Tānvür]]. None share any love for one another. Alliances shift with the tides — the same two organizations might cooperate on a smuggling run in spring and be murdering each other's agents by autumn, depending on whose territory was encroached upon, whose informant was turned, or whose deal fell through.

Most towns also have smaller, purely local organizations — gangs that handle petty crime, prostitution, protection rackets, blackmail, extortion, and the occasional hit job. These local outfits exist in an uneasy relationship with the larger syndicates, who use them as subcontractors and buffers. A local gang boss might take a cut from the Gray Tide to look the other way about shipments at the docks, while simultaneously selling information about those same shipments to the Velvet Hand. This layered structure means that the syndicate bosses rarely get their hands dirty, and if a local gang is broken up by the authorities, the larger organization loses an asset but suffers no real exposure.

The syndicates compete ruthlessly for territory, contacts, and revenue. Border disputes — where one syndicate's territory bleeds into another's — are settled through negotiation, bribery, or violence, roughly in that order. A deal brokered between the Crimson Ledger and the Gray Tide in Kethara's harbor district doesn't bind their agents in Varoshan, where the Ash Road considers itself sovereign. Betrayals are remembered for generations. Truces are transactional, not sentimental. The criminal underworld of Thalorna is a web of pragmatic self-interest, and the only real loyalty is to profit.

## The Major Syndicates

### Vylarian Sea Basin

- [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|The Crimson Ledger]] — Financial crime network based in Harad
- [[affiliation-velvethand|The Velvet Hand]] — Espionage and assassination network across Vylaria, Provènzia, and Heliónis
- [[affiliation-graytide|The Gray Tide]] — Maritime crime network in the northern and western Vylarian Sea
- [[affiliation-theashroad|The Ash Road]] — Overland smuggling network along the desert caravan routes

### Eastern Ankaris

- [[affiliation-heklotzhak|Hëk Lōt Zhāk]] (The Black Lotus Society) — Bureaucratic, commercial, and ritual crime network dominating the [[doc-tanvurempr|Empire of Tānvür]]

## Inter-Syndicate Relations

The four syndicates interact constantly — collaborating and warring, sometimes simultaneously.

The **Crimson Ledger** launders money for all three others, making it indispensable but also deeply resented. The Ledger charges steep commissions, and its agents have a habit of "discovering" damaging financial records about their clients — insurance against betrayal, or leverage for better terms. The Gray Tide in particular chafes at paying the Ledger's rates and periodically tries to set up its own laundering operations, which the Ledger crushes with ruthless efficiency by tipping off customs officials.

The **Gray Tide** and the **Ash Road** cooperate on human trafficking routes — the Tide handles the sea leg, the Road handles the overland. But they clash violently over smuggling through Varoshan, which both consider their territory. A decade-long turf war over Varoshan's eastern docks left dozens dead and was only resolved when the Crimson Ledger brokered a profit-sharing arrangement — which both sides immediately began scheming to undermine.

The **Velvet Hand** sells information to anyone who pays, including the other syndicates, making it both indispensable and universally distrusted. When the Ash Road needs a political obstacle removed in a Vylarian port, it contracts the Velvet Hand. When the Velvet Hand needs a body to disappear by sea, it calls the Gray Tide. But the Velvet Hand has also been known to sell the same intelligence to two opposing parties, profiting from the resulting chaos. The Gray Tide has standing orders to kill any confirmed Velvet Hand agent found operating in their territory without prior arrangement — an order that is enforced about half the time, depending on whether the agent has something useful to trade.

The **Ash Road** considers itself above the petty squabbles of the coastal syndicates, but this is mostly posture. Its leadership in Varoshan watches the Crimson Ledger's growing influence with alarm, and its desert operatives occasionally "lose" shipments that were bound for Gray Tide fences. The Ash Road's greatest advantage is geographic — the desert is vast and largely lawless, and no rival syndicate has the local knowledge or tribal connections to operate there effectively.

Local gangs play all four syndicates against each other, taking money from whichever one offers the best deal this week. A tavern keeper in a port town might fence goods for the Gray Tide, pass messages for the Velvet Hand, and owe money to the Crimson Ledger — all while telling the Ash Road's caravan contacts that he's loyal only to them. This mercenary pragmatism keeps the local operators alive but means they are the first casualties when syndicate relations deteriorate.

The **[[affiliation-heklotzhak|Hëk Lōt Zhāk]]** has minimal contact with the four Vylarian Sea syndicates. The geographic separation is substantial, the Lotus's operations depend on Tānvüri bureaucratic structures that do not exist in the west, and the Lotus's leadership regards western crime as beneath notice. Where the two worlds intersect — in the border cities of Byzaría, along the eastern caravan routes, in the ports where eastern goods transit through Vylarian hands — the relationships are transactional and mutually respectful. The Black Lotus quietly fences restricted eastern goods through Ash Road caravans; the Crimson Ledger has handled occasional money transfers for senior Lotus figures who need wealth moved westward. These arrangements are professional and limited in scope.
