- **Deity:** Vúlcan — _The Forge-Lord_
- **Domain:** Fire (creative aspect)
- **Symbol:** A radiant anvil beneath a sheltering flame.
- **Pantheon:** [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldián Pantheon]]

The Sacred Forge represents Vúlcan's creative aspect: craftsmanship, transformation, illumination, and protection. Its priests are smiths, builders, and knights-errant who defend the faithful. Fire is sacred not as a weapon but as a tool — the forge-fire that shapes raw metal into something useful, the hearth-fire that warms a home, the beacon-fire that guides travelers to safety. The Sacred Forge is the established, mainstream Vúlcani faith in most of western Ankaris and is politically favored in Provènzia.

## Worship

The worship of the Sacred Forge centers on reverence for the creative power of fire and the sacred craft of metalworking. The temples of the Sacred Forge are built around actual forges — great stone hearths where the sacred fires burn continuously, day and night, year after year. Some of these forges have burned for centuries, their flames passed from generation to generation of priests, and to let such a flame die out is considered a catastrophe of cosmic significance.

The central liturgical practice is the Blessing of the Anvil. When a new forge is established—whether a smithy, an armory, a foundry, or a smithcraft workshop—a priest of the Sacred Forge performs an elaborate consecration. The ceremony involves the lighting of the forge fire, the inscribing of protective prayers around the anvil's base, and the offering of the first object to be forged in the new space. This object is always given to the temple and becomes part of the sacred treasury, a tangible record of the forge's sanctification and proof of Vúlcan's blessing upon the craftspeople who will work there.

Major festivals include the Festival of the Hearth, celebrated at the winter solstice when the days are darkest and the need for light and warmth is most acute. During this festival, every hearth in a household is ceremonially blessed, and flames are carried from the temple's central forge to light family fires. Those whose hearths have been newly established in the past year bring gifts to the temple to receive the sacred blessing. The festival emphasizes Vúlcan's protective aspect—the god who shelters the faithful from cold and darkness.

Another significant observance is the Lanterns of the Night, held during the spring equinox. Priests and devotees carry lanterns—small braziers of blessed flame—through the streets at twilight, creating a river of light that moves through the city. The processional ends at a high point, where the collected lanterns are arranged in the shape of an anvil, a hammer, or a great forge. The symbolism is clear: through craft, through illumination, through the transformation of raw materials into useful objects, civilization triumphs over chaos.

Knights and soldiers desiring the Sacred Forge's blessing often perform the Vigil of the Forge the night before battle or investiture. They remain awake in the temple throughout the night, watching the forge fires, meditating on courage and purpose. Some spend the vigil working at a forge themselves, learning the basic motions of hammering metal—the repetitive rhythm is understood as a kind of prayer, aligning the warrior's will with Vúlcan's creative purpose. At dawn, the knight is blessed by a Hammer-Priest with holy oil, and they depart to face their challenge strengthened by the god's favor.

## Organization

The priesthood of the Sacred Forge is organized into a formal hierarchy centered on the Grand Pontifex, the supreme priest or priestess who oversees all Sacred Forge temples and clergy across western Ankaris. The Grand Pontifex resides in a major cathedral-forge, typically located in a significant city—currently in Provènzia, where the faith holds the greatest political influence. The Grand Pontifex's authority is absolute in matters of doctrine and sacred practice, though their power is checked by the collegium of senior Hammer-Priests who govern the faith's major temples.

Below the Grand Pontifex stand the Hammer-Priests, the ordained clergy who lead individual temples and oversee the consecration of forges, the blessing of craftspeople, and the training of acolytes. These priests are themselves typically accomplished smiths or craftspeople, having spent years perfecting a trade before advancing to full ordination. Many Hammer-Priests maintain active smithcraft—the faith teaches that a priest who has abandoned their craft has lost touch with Vúlcan's essential nature. A Hammer-Priest might spend the morning in the temple performing liturgies, then the afternoon at an anvil, creating sacred objects or teaching apprentices.

The third tier consists of the Acolytes—younger initiates in training who are not yet ordained. Many acolytes are apprentices to master smiths, learning the practical craft while simultaneously studying theology and sacred practice. The distinction between a smithcraft apprentice and a Sacred Forge acolyte is often blurred; many begin as one and gradually become the other.

The priesthood maintains deep structural ties with the smiths' guilds of each city. Guild masters in good standing are often members of the Sacred Forge's lay order, and guild halls commonly maintain shrines dedicated to Vúlcan. Some smiths' guilds have effectively merged their organization with the local Sacred Forge temple, so that the distinction between "priest" and "master craftsman" is nearly invisible. In Provènzia, the Grand Guild of Smithers is the single largest patron and supporter of the Sacred Forge's cathedral-forge, and its guild council participates directly in temple governance.

The faith also maintains several knightly orders dedicated to Vúlcan's protection. The Order of the Anvil and the Knights of the Forge are the two largest; their members serve as warrior-priests who defend Sacred Forge temples and advance the faith's interests militarily. These knights wear armor blessed and often forged by Sacred Forge priests, and they consider themselves as much servants of Vúlcan as any ordained cleric. They are bound by vows of craftsmanship and protection—to defend the helpless, to protect the tools and trades of honest craftspeople, and to oppose those who pervert fire as a weapon.

## Relations

The Sacred Forge maintains a close alliance with the [[affiliation-janus|Faith of Janus]]. Both faiths value discipline, craftsmanship, and the defense of civilization. Sacred Forge smiths arm Jánite knights; Jánite courts protect Sacred Forge temples. This partnership is one of the most stable interfaith alliances in the Aurèldían pantheon.

The Sacred Forge wages an unceasing doctrinal war against [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]], which it regards as a dangerous heresy that perverts Vúlcan's divine gifts. Where the Sacred Forge sees fire as a creative force to be respected and wielded with care, the Black Flame glorifies its destructive power. The Sacred Forge and the Faith of Jánus are united in their opposition to the Black Flame, though for different reasons: the Sacred Forge fights a war against apostasy within Vúlcan's own faith, while Jánus fights a cosmic struggle against everything the Black Flame represents.
