Goddess of wealth, abundance, and seduction. Vénusia blesses people with prosperity and physical love.

## Worship

Vénusia is worshipped with a sensuality that distinguishes her cult from nearly all other faiths in the Aurèldían pantheon. Her temples are draped in silks and hung with incense—sandalwood, myrrh, and rose oils perfume the air. Priestesses and priests dress in fine fabrics, adorned with jewelry and cosmetics that celebrate the beauty of the body. This is not considered licentiousness by the faithful but rather a rightful honor to the goddess who blesses abundance and physical joy.

The core worship practice is the Offering of Gain. Merchants, bankers, and traders who have realized a successful transaction will present a portion of their profit at Vénusia's altar—not out of obligation as with some deities, but as a joyful acknowledgment of good fortune shared. A successful merchant who has turned a tidy profit will commission artisans to craft an offering: perfumed oils, fine garments, or golden tokens depicting fruit or coins. The priestess receives these offerings with celebration, understanding them as both gratitude to the goddess and a kind of loving competition—each merchant desires to give Vénusia a more magnificent offering than the last, as a sign of their prosperity and favor.

The major festival honoring Vénusia is the Festival of the Golden Harvest, celebrated in late summer when traders return from their journeys with laden ships and profitable goods. The festival lasts three days and is the most openly hedonistic celebration in the Aurèldían religious calendar. Merchants and craftspeople close their shops to participate. The streets fill with musicians, dancers, and acrobats. Wine flows freely. People commission singers and actors to perform love ballads and comedic plays in the temple squares. Wealthy traders host feasts featuring rare delicacies—spiced meats, fresh fruits, honey cakes—and those who attend are expected to celebrate without restraint. The Priestess of Vénusia leads a ceremonial procession through the city's market districts, blessing newly opened merchant stalls, consecrating new trading companies, and receiving the accumulated tributes of the past season.

Individual worship often takes the form of private devotions before important business dealings. A merchant preparing to negotiate a major contract may spend the night at a shrine to Vénusia, making an offering and praying for charm, eloquence, and—if the merchant is honest with themselves—a favorable outcome. Courtesans and companions who work within the framework of the faith are often consecrated to Vénusia and view their work not as shameful but as sacred service to the goddess's domain of seduction and physical pleasure. These temple-companions are respected figures within the cult, as are the merchants and guild-masters who support them.

## Organization

The priesthood of Vénusia is organized around individual temples rather than a centralized hierarchy. Each major city's temple of Vénusia is governed by a High Priestess (or High Priest, though priestesses are far more common), who serves as the cult's spiritual head and the primary intermediary between the goddess and her followers. These High Priestesses often wield considerable influence in their cities' merchant councils and trading guilds, as the affiliation between Vénusia worship and commercial prosperity is well-established.

Below the High Priestess stand the Priestesses of the Temple, who manage day-to-day worship, maintain the sacred spaces, and counsel those who come seeking Vénusia's favor. These ordained clergy are typically well-educated in the arts of negotiation, rhetoric, and persuasion—qualities essential for mediating disputes between merchants and for blessing commercial ventures. Many study the practical side of commerce (accounting, trade law, contract interpretation) as thoroughly as they study theology.

The temple also maintains a cohort of Acolytes—younger devotees in training who are not yet fully ordained. These acolytes study the theology of prosperity, learn the rituals and festivals, and often serve as scribes and administrators for the temple's records of offerings and blessings. Many acolytes go on to full priesthood, but others use their training to become merchants, accountants, or guild officers in their own right.

A distinctive feature of Vénusia's priesthood is the formal recognition of Temple Companions — practitioners of courtesanship who are consecrated to the goddess and understood as sacred priestesses in their own right. These individuals are not merely tolerated but actively honored within the cult structure. They undergo formal initiation ceremonies, wear identifying marks or tokens showing their dedication to Vénusia, and often take oaths to conduct their work with skill, discretion, and a kind of spiritual integrity. A temple's sponsorship of a Companion — providing training, housing, protection, and a portion of earnings — is understood as a religious duty and a form of worship itself.

The temples also maintain close relationships with merchant guilds, banking houses, and trading companies. Many guilds formally dedicate themselves to Vénusia and maintain a shrine within their guildhouse where merchant-members can make offerings before major transactions. These guild-temple partnerships create a network of mutual support: the priesthood blesses commercial ventures, and the guilds provide tithes and resources to maintain the temples.

## Relations

Vénusia maintains an easy, complementary relationship with [[affiliation-florania|Flórania]], goddess of natural fertility and the harvest. Where Flórania oversees the physical fertility of fields and bodies, Vénusia blesses the resulting abundance and the commerce that springs from it. Agricultural communities give thanks to Flórania for the crop; merchant communities give thanks to Vénusia for the profit they realize from trading that crop. The two faiths work together without tension, as they occupy different domains within the broader theme of prosperity.

With [[affiliation-janus|Faith of Janus]], Vénusia maintains a practical, respectful relationship. Jánus values order and stability, which merchant communities require to conduct business safely. In turn, prosperous, wealthy cities are stable cities, which pleases both Vénusia's merchants and Jánus's need for cosmic order. Vénusian merchants often donate to Jánite temples, and Jánite magistrates protect Vénusian temple-properties through law and justice.

Vénusia's relationship with [[affiliation-menerva|Faith of Menerva]], the goddess of knowledge, is one of mutual enrichment. Knowledge leads to successful business practices; prosperity funds the pursuit of learning. Vénusian merchants often patronize scholars, artists, and philosophers, seeing such patronage as a form of worship—the physical manifestation of abundance made available for creative pursuits. Ménérvian temples and academies receive donations from wealthy Vénusian traders, and Ménérvian scholars often work as advisors to merchant companies on matters of trade and negotiation.
