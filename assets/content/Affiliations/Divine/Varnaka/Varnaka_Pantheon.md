# Varṇaka Pantheon

The **Varṇaka** is a single unified faith with a plural pantheon. Ten deities and spirit-courts are acknowledged and honoured across the tradition, but they do not each correspond to a separate religion. Rather, the Varṇaka faith is organised into dozens — and in some regions hundreds — of **sects and traditions** (_sampradāyas_, _vādas_, _panthās_, _samājas_), each with its own theological emphasis, temple style, and favoured rites. A sect may venerate one god, a small group of gods, or the entire pantheon in a particular interpretive key; likewise, a given deity may be the focus of several distinct sects that read that god differently. A priest is ordained into a specific sect, and most temples are associated with a particular sect, but the lay faithful move between sects and temples with relative ease, attending the shrine most appropriate to each occasion in their lives.

Accordingly, the notes below describe the **deities** — their symbols, iconography, sacred objects, and the veneration practices of their worshippers — alongside a **sample** of the sects that organise the faith's institutional life. Only a handful of sects are documented here; the full ecology of Varṇakan tradition would fill a library.

## Principal Gods

### **Vyālendra** (_The Shaper of Worlds_)

- **Aspect**: **Creation**
- **Role**: God of creation and cosmic order. Vyālendra is the architect of existence, shaping the earth, skies, and all life.
- **Symbol**: A lotus with golden petals.
- **Pronunciation**: _VYAH-len-drah_
- **Spirits**: **Shilpa-Kalas** (crafting spirits) assist mortals in art, architecture, and innovation.
- **Local Worship**: Revered in cities for their founding and prosperity.

---

### **Mahájaya** (_The Eternal Preserver_)

- **Aspect**: **Order and Prosperity**
- **Role**: Goddess of preservation, harmony, and abundance. Mahájaya governs the cycles of growth, trade, and societal well-being.
- **Symbol**: A balanced scale encircled by wheat.
- **Pronunciation**: _MAH-ha-jah-yah_
- **Spirits**: **Nivara-Kshetras** (spirits of the fields) ensure bountiful harvests and economic balance.
- **Local Worship**: Central in rural regions for agricultural blessings.

---

### **Rásikara** (_The Flame of Renewal_)

- **Aspect**: **Fire, Transformation, and Chaos**
- **Role**: God of fire, destruction, and change. Rásikara transforms through both destruction and rebirth, purging corruption and igniting growth.
- **Symbol**: A flame rising from a broken circle.
- **Pronunciation**: _RAH-see-kah-rah_
- **Spirits**: **Agni-Pralayas** (spirits of purging fire) manifest during natural disasters or rituals of renewal.
- **Local Worship**: Honored at temples during fire festivals.

## Lesser Gods

### **Kālavrata** (_The Gatekeeper of Death_)

- **Aspect**: **Death**
- **Role**: A solitary god who oversees the passage of souls and guards the cycle of reincarnation. Kālavrata is impartial and revered for guiding the dead to their next life.
- **Symbol**: A black conch shell.
- **Pronunciation**: _KAH-lah-vrah-tah_
- **Spirits**: **Mārgapālas** (guiding spirits) lead souls through the afterlife.
- **Local Worship**: Villages light lamps to honor their departed and ensure safe passage.

### **Meghanātha** (_Lord of Thunder_)

- **Aspect**: **Storms**
- **Role**: God of storms, rain, and protection. Meghanātha ensures the balance of rain for crops and controls the fury of the monsoons.
- **Symbol**: A drum encircled by lightning bolts.
- **Pronunciation**: _MEG-ha-nah-tha_
- **Local Worship**: Fishermen and farmers pray to him for protection and abundance.

### **Jñānasūra** (_The Radiant Sage_)

- **Aspect**: **Knowledge**
- **Role**: God of wisdom, learning, and enlightenment. Jñānasūra grants insight to scholars, warriors, and artisans.
- **Symbol**: A radiant sun with an open book.
- **Pronunciation**: _GYAH-nah-soo-rah_
- **Spirits**: **Vidyā-Karanas** (scribes of divine knowledge) inspire mortals.
- **Local Worship**: Invoked at schools, libraries, and before battles.

### **Kāmavrata** (_The Celestial Lover_)

- **Aspect**: **Fertility and Lust**
- **Role**: God of fertility, passion, and desire. Kāmavrata encourages the creation of life and the pursuit of love and joy.
- **Symbol**: A blooming lotus entwined with vines.
- **Pronunciation**: _KAH-mah-vrah-tah_
- **Local Worship**: Celebrated during festivals of love and renewal.

### **Vyāhrati** (_The Keeper of Decay_)

- **Aspect**: **Decay**
- **Role**: Goddess of endings and transformation. Vyāhrati oversees the decay of old things to make way for new growth.
- **Symbol**: A wilting flower encased in dew.
- **Pronunciation**: _VYAH-hrah-tee_
- **Local Worship**: Revered during seasonal transitions and harvest cycles.

## Spiritual Courts

### **Svapnadēvas** (_The Dreaming Host_)

- **Aspect**: **Dreams**
- **Role**: Celestial spirits who weave dreams, omens, and visions. They act as intermediaries between mortals and the divine. The Svapnadēvas are divine servants of [[lore-goddreams|The God of Dreams]], though the Varṇaka do not name or directly worship that elder deity — they revere the Host itself.
- **Symbol**: A crescent moon with a lotus stem.
- **Pronunciation**: _SWAP-nah-day-vahs_
- **Local Worship**: Seers and mystics invoke them in rites of prophecy.

### **Pavanajitras** (_The Wandering Wind_)

- **Aspect**: **Voyager/Traveler**
- **Role**: A collective of wind spirits that guide travelers and merchants on their journeys.
- **Symbol**: A wind-filled sail.
- **Pronunciation**: _PAH-vah-nah-jee-trahs_
- **Local Worship**: Honored at crossroads and trade routes.
