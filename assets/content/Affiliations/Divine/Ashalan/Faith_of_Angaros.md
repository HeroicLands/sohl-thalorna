Ángáróš is the great adversary, the god of chaos, destruction, deception, and entropy. He is the living principle of Druj—falsehood, corruption, and the slow dissolution of all imposed order. Where [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] creates and sustains, Ángáróš tears down and unmakes. Where [[affiliation-ataros|Ātáröš]] purifies through flame, Ángáróš corrupts through poison and shadow. Where [[affiliation-korsadra|Khóršádrä]] reveals truth through light, Ángáróš obscures it through lies and deception. He is not a minor or regional deity—he is the supreme cosmic force opposing all righteousness, order, and truth. The entire Āsháian pantheon exists primarily to wage war against him.

The worship of Ángáróš is absolutely forbidden throughout all Āsháian-dominated lands. No temples are built in his name. No priests wear his colors openly. No fires are kindled in his honor. To acknowledge Ángáróš openly, to speak his name aloud without protective prayer, to so much as suggest sympathy with his cause is to invite immediate execution in civilized lands and brutal punishment even among the most lawless desert tribes. Yet his worship persists—driven underground, hidden in secret cells and shadow-cults, whispered about in fearful tones. The very prohibition against his worship is paradoxically his greatest strength, for it forces his followers into secrecy, making them impossible to root out entirely and lending them an air of forbidden power that attracts certain temperaments of mind: those who despise the comfortable order of civilization, those who hunger for power regardless of its cost, those who see in Ángáróš's vision of dissolution a terrible and seductive liberation.

The theology of Ángáróš begins with a single, revolutionary premise: creation is a cage. The cosmos that [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] constructed is beautiful to the enslaved, perhaps, but it is slavery nonetheless. The cosmic order that the righteous gods sustain is oppressive, limiting, denying. Ángáróš teaches that true freedom lies only in the dissolution of all imposed structure, all morality, all authority. He promises liberation through destruction—if all things are unmade, if the world returns to the primordial chaos that preceded creation, then all beings are freed from the bonds of law, consequence, and meaning. To the desperate and the ambitious, to the powerless and the power-hungry, to those who have been wronged by a system they cannot change, Ángáróš offers a terrible promise: that by serving chaos, they can overturn the order that oppresses them.

The followers of Ángáróš do not worship him out of love, as one might love [[affiliation-zarvanya|Zárványä]] or [[affiliation-mithara|Míthárä]]. Rather, they serve him out of cold recognition of cosmic truth as they understand it. They believe that Āsha—the principle of order—is an illusion, a tyranny masquerading as truth. They see the structure of society, law, morality, and consequence as chains. And they see in Ángáróš the power to break those chains. His followers work toward the slow corruption of all things, the introduction of entropy wherever order exists, the sowing of doubt and deception wherever truth is claimed. They are arsonists, assassins, poisoners, and seducers—not because they are inherently evil, but because they serve a god who believes that all of civilization's structures, all of its moral frameworks, deserve to be burned.

## Worship

The secret cults of Ángáróš operate with the understanding that discovery means death. Unlike the open priesthoods of other deities, who conduct their rituals in great fire-temples visible to all, the followers of Ángáróš meet in hidden chambers—sealed caves in the desert, cellars beneath seemingly innocent buildings, hidden chambers within apparently orthodox fire-temples where heretical priests maintain a secret altar. Because public worship is impossible, the rituals of Ángáróš take perverse, inverted forms. Where other Āsháian worship begins with the kindling of fire, Ángáróš-worship involves the extinguishing of flames—the supplicant enters a dark chamber and, by will and meditation, mentally unmakes the sacred fire that all Āsháian culture takes to be central.

The followers of Ángáróš perform rituals of corruption and inversion. They may recite the righteous prayers of [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] backward, corrupting them into heretical meanings. They anoint themselves with poisons and substances that induce hallucination and delirium, seeking to perceive the "truth" hidden beneath the illusions of ordered reality. They practice ritual lying, deliberately and methodically cultivating the ability to speak falsehood with such conviction that even they can no longer distinguish lie from truth. In the most extreme cults, they engage in acts of ritual destruction—burning crops, poisoning wells, murdering the innocent—not from personal hatred but from theological conviction: each act of chaos and destruction is a prayer to Ángáróš, a contribution to the great work of unmaking.

The communion of Ángáróš-followers involves the consumption of poisoned wine or fermented substances derived from corruption and decay. Unlike the clear wine of [[affiliation-aravesta|Árávēštä]]'s prosperity-feasts, the drink of Ángáróš is bitter, toxic, and induces visions of dissolution. The followers believe they are imbibing the essence of chaos itself, preparing themselves to survive the day when Ángáróš finally defeats [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] and the world dissolves back into formless void. They emerge from these dark rituals with dilated pupils, trembling hands, and a fierce certainty that they are serving a truth that is too terrible for the comfortable masses to comprehend.

In some settlements where Ángáróš-cults have taken root, a more subtle form of worship emerges: the infiltration of orthodox fire-temples. A seemingly pious Āthravān or Hērbad works their way up through the priesthood of another deity, all while secretly serving Ángáróš. From within the priesthood, they can corrupt doctrine, teach subtle heresies, introduce confusion and doubt into the minds of the faithful. A fire that burns with strange colors, an oracle whose interpretations are consistently misaligned with the truth, a Mōbad who begins to issue decrees that undermine the social order—these are the subtle victories of Ángáróš worship, far more dangerous than open rebellion.

## Organization

The priesthood of Ángáróš is structurally the inverse of legitimate Āsháian cults. Where the faith-cults of other deities emphasize clarity, truth, and the preservation of knowledge through careful documentation and transmission, Ángáróš's priests deliberately obscure and fragment their teachings. There is no single canonical scripture, no universally recognized hierarchy, no agreed-upon liturgy. Instead, each cell of worshippers develops its own variations, its own heresies, its own interpretations. This radical decentralization makes the cult impossible to eradicate—there is no center to strike, no high priest whose death would cripple the movement, no temple whose destruction would suppress the faith.

The closest thing to leadership among Ángáróš-followers are the Dēvāshers ("Corruptors" or "Truth-Destroyers"), individuals who demonstrate particular talent at spreading chaos and confusion. A Dēvāsher is not appointed but recognized through reputation: other cultists know their name, know they have accomplished significant works of corruption, and defer to their judgment. A Dēvāsher might spend years infiltrating a legitimate priesthood, or might be known for particularly lethal poisons or destructive plots. But their authority is contingent and temporary—a Dēvāsher who fails or becomes known to the orthodoxy is abandoned without hesitation. Loyalty to person is antithetical to service to Ángáróš; the only loyalty demanded is to the principle of chaos itself.

Among the cultists, initiates are called Tasfāns ("Those-Dwelling-in-Shadow"). A Tasfān undergoes training in the art of deception, studying how to identify the vulnerable points in others' belief-systems and how to introduce seeds of doubt that will grow into corrosive cynicism. They are taught the arts of assassination and poisoning—not merely for their direct destructive value, but because the act of killing the righteous and seeing their gods fail to protect them is itself a form of theological argument. They practice ritualized lying and become adept at the manipulation of social currents.

Those who have proven themselves through successful corruption-work may be recognized as Āzdhāns ("Blade-Bearers")—the warrior-priests of Ángáróš. These are individuals who have moved beyond theoretical corruption to practical destruction on a grand scale. They lead assassination plots against priests and nobles, they organize rebellions against established rule, they commit acts of sabotage intended to destabilize entire regions. An Āzdhān is understood to be a weapon wielded by Ángáróš, a human instrument of cosmic entropy.

The most feared Ángáróš-worshippers are those rare individuals who have become Dēvájahns ("Serpents" or "Soul-Corrupted")—those who have practiced chaos-worship for so long and so intensely that they are believed to have become partially merged with Ángáróš himself. These are figures whispered about in fearful tones: sorcerers said to command poison, assassins who leave no trace, prophets of doom whose words inevitably come true. Whether such individuals truly exist or are merely legends cultivated by the cult to inspire terror is uncertain, but their supposed existence is enough to make the cult terrifying to even the most confident authorities.

## Relations

Ángáróš exists in eternal, irreconcilable war with every other deity in the Āsháian pantheon. His opposition to [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] is the central theological and cosmic struggle. Āhúrdáén represents the principle of order that Ángáróš is committed to destroying. Everything that Āhúrdáén creates, Ángáróš seeks to unmake. Every law Āhúrdáén establishes, Ángáróš encourages his followers to break. Every civilization Āhúrdáén's principles support, Ángáróš works to tear down.

[[affiliation-ataros|Ātáröš]] (The Sacred Flame) is perhaps Ángáróš's most direct opponent. Fire purifies, burns away corruption, destroys entropy. Every sacred flame is a defiance of Ángáróš's influence. The priesthoods of Ātáröš and Ángáróš are in constant, direct conflict: wherever a fire-temple is established, the Ángáróš-cults move to extinguish it; wherever an Ángáróš-cell operates, the flame-priesthood works to discover and destroy it.

Ángáróš also stands opposed to [[affiliation-zarvanya|Zárványä]], whose clear visions of truth are antithetical to his principle of deception. He opposes [[affiliation-korsadra|Khóršádrä]], whose knowledge and revelation are weapons against ignorance and the chaos that feeds in darkness. He opposes [[affiliation-azravan|Ázráván]], whose principle of cosmic consequence and the weighing of the soul ensures that actions have meaning—a meaning Ángáróš seeks to dissolve. And he even opposes the gentler deities: he opposes [[affiliation-zurathra|Zúráthrä]]'s cultivation of fertility and life, [[affiliation-aravesta|Árávēštä]]'s blessing of prosperity, and [[affiliation-mithara|Míthárä]]'s sustaining of creation itself.

However, [[affiliation-morvahna|Mórváhñä]] (The Silent Keeper) occupies a more ambiguous position. Mórváhñä governs natural decay and the cycles of dissolution that are necessary for renewal. While the priesthoods of Ángáróš and Mórváhñä are not allied, neither are they in open war. The Ángáróš-cultists sometimes argue that Mórváhñä's process of decay is a preview of the ultimate dissolution that Ángáróš promises—the return of all things to primordial chaos. Some scholars have noted with alarm that certain Ángáróš-cults have begun to target priests of Mórváhñä with unusual frequency, suggesting that the heretical worshippers may be attempting to corrupt the doctrine of natural decay into a theology of total annihilation. If true, such an alliance—even unspoken—would represent a new and terrifying development in the cosmic struggle between Āsha and Druj.

The fundamental truth that unites all the cults opposed to Ángáróš is that they recognize his existence as the existential threat. In this regard, Ángáróš is the villain against whom all the righteous deities define themselves. To be a follower of any legitimate Āsháian faith is, ultimately, to be a soldier in the war against chaos, entropy, and the corrosive nihilism that Ángáróš represents. The Āsháian world is not a place of comfortable piety—it is a battlefield where the cosmic struggle between Āsha and Druj is fought daily through human hands.
