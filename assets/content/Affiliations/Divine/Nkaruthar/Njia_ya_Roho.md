# Njia ya Roho

The **Njia ya Roho** — the Spirit Path — is the shamanic tradition of the Okháric tribes: the
practice of standing in relationship with the [[lore-zohira|zohira]], the intermediary spirits
that govern rivers, storms, the hunt, fertility and death.

It is not a branch of [[affiliation-nkaruthar|Nkaru'thar]] and it is not a rival to it. The two
are held at once, by the same people, without contradiction. The Eternal Flame is a religion —
doctrine, temples, an ordained priesthood, and a hierarchy that runs from the Mwanafunzi to the
High Pyremant. The Spirit Path is not a religion at all in that sense. It has no doctrine to
assent to, no temple, no ordination, and no rank; what it has is a set of particular relationships
with particular spirits, and the people who can conduct them.

An Okháric villager goes to the pyramid at the turning of the year and to the cairn when the river
is wrong.

To outsiders this is incomprehensible. Ta'Kheperu priests and Vylarian factors have written the
Okhárics down as muddled, as syncretists, as people who have not finished deciding — and the
question they keep asking, which of the two is really believed, is one no Okháric can find a use
for. To insiders it is not a compromise between two systems. It is simply the natural order of
things: the Flame is what the world is made of and the zohira are who you deal with about the
river, and a person who confused the two would be the eccentric one.

## The Spirit Hunt

There is one gate, and it is not an examination. A candidate must track and defeat a powerful
jungle predator — one named by the Master of the Hunt and understood to be a guardian of its
ground — and consume its heart in a rite that binds their soul more closely to the spirit realm
and to the zohira.

Candidates die. The tradition is direct about this and does not soften it, and the Elder's
judgement about who may attempt the Hunt is the most consequential thing the tradition asks anyone
to do. Those who return are **mwalimu wa roho**, and are so for life.

The Hunt confers no rank in any temple. A High Pyremant outranks every priest in Okháris and does
not outrank a shaman, because the two are not on the same ladder — a fact both sides state plainly
and neither much enjoys.

## Practice

A shaman's work is conducted at cairns rather than in buildings: a stone pile with a flame-mark
cut into the capstone, sited where the zohira concerned is closest — a ford, a birthing-house, a
hunting ground, a burial field. What is done there is petition, offering, interpretation and
intercession, and each zohira's requirements are its own.

Because zohira are addressed by domain before place, a shaman who travels is not left mute. The
bond weakens with distance from the cairn where it was made, but it does not sever, which is why
Okháric shamans are found far from Okháris and Áelendan shamen almost never are.

## Relations

With [[affiliation-nkaruthar|Nkaru'thar]] the tradition is aligned and occasionally irritable. The
city priesthoods acknowledge the zohira as part of the Flame's design; the shamans acknowledge the
Flame as the ultimate source. What they argue about is precedence, tribute and whose word settles
a matter in a frontier village — never about whether the other is true.

The two make plainly incompatible claims about the dead, the harvest and the blade (see
[[lore-zohira|Zohira]]), and nobody on either side treats this as a problem requiring a solution.

The **Shókuma** are the tribe most associated with the Hunt and with spirit communion; the
**Magara** with the Spirit Blades, weapons in which a zohira of the blade is seated. Both practise
the same tradition, and both regard the other's emphasis as slightly eccentric.
