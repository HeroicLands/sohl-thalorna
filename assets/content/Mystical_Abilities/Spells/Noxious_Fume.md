The caster gestures toward a metallic surface — a coin, a weapon, a piece of ore — and draws forth the poisonous vapors that metals naturally produce when heated or chemically agitated, concentrating and amplifying them into a visible cloud of acrid, yellowish-green gas. The fumes sting the eyes, burn the throat, and induce violent coughing, nausea, and disorientation in anyone who breathes them. Prolonged exposure causes dizziness, headaches, and eventually unconsciousness.

The cloud clings to low ground and enclosed spaces, making it particularly effective in cellars, tunnels, and rooms with poor ventilation. In open air, the fumes disperse more quickly but remain effective for several minutes within the initial area. The caster is not immune to the effects and must exercise care with wind direction and their own positioning. The spell requires the presence of metallic material as a source, though even a handful of coins or a single iron nail provides sufficient basis for a disabling cloud.
