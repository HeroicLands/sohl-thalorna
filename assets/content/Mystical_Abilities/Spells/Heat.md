The caster focuses their attention on an object or area and wills its temperature to rise. Metal grows warm, then hot, then too hot to hold. Water steams. Wood begins to smoke and may eventually ignite if the caster sustains their effort. The effect is precise — the caster can heat a sword's blade while leaving its hilt cool, or warm a stone floor beneath their feet while the walls remain cold.

The temperature increase is gradual and controllable, making Heat useful for a remarkable range of tasks: forging metal without a furnace, cooking food without a fire, warming a cold room, melting ice blocking a passage, or softening wax seals for examination. Against opponents, a heated weapon or piece of armor forces them to drop it or suffer burns, while heated stone beneath an enemy's feet can make a position untenable without any visible flame.
