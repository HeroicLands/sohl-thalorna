The caster draws moisture directly from a target — a creature, a length of timber, a sack of grain — leaving it parched and desiccated. Living targets feel an unpleasant draining sensation as water is pulled from their skin and tissues, experiencing sudden thirst, cracked lips, and dried mucous membranes. Organic materials become brittle, shrunken, and lighter.

The extracted moisture collects visibly in the air around the caster as a fine mist or gathers as droplets on their hands and arms. The spell is used for preserving food and hides, drying out waterlogged materials, and as a weapon — dehydrating an opponent even briefly causes disorientation, muscle cramps, and difficulty concentrating. Sustained application can cause serious harm, and the Hydälis school considers its use against living targets a last resort in most traditions.
