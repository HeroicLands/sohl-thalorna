# Appearance {#appearance}

Pallàvi stands 5'9" tall with a light build. She has tawny brown skin, dark black hair, and hazel eyes. Her features include a heart-shaped face, a broad nose, a soft jawline, tawny brows.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Early Life and Calling**

Pallàvi was born in the city-state of Dharmapur to a family of modest means. Her father was a merchant of spices, her mother a seamstress of considerable but unrenowned skill. Unlike her siblings, Pallàvi showed no interest in commerce or practical crafts; instead, she became obsessed with the written word and its visual presentation. Her first mentor was **Keśava the Illuminator**, an aging master scribe who recognized in her something rare: not merely technical skill, but genuine artistic vision combined with the discipline to master the technical foundations of her craft. Under Keśava's tutelage from age fourteen, Pallàvi learned not just calligraphy and the formation of letters, but the philosophy of text as art—the belief that how a word is written is inseparable from what it means.

**Guild Training and Breakthrough**

At eighteen, Pallàvi formally joined the Scribes' Guild and began her professional training in earnest. She quickly distinguished herself not through conservative mastery of traditional styles, but through innovative fusion: taking classical Vedyaran script forms and subtly reimagining them, creating decorative borders that enhanced rather than overshadowed text, and developing a distinctive technique of integrating colored inks to create visual harmony without sacrificing readability. Her breakthrough came when a wealthy merchant commissioned a copy of a philosophical text, requesting that the quality of transcription match the depth of the ideas. Pallàvi produced a work so visually and spiritually stunning that the merchant displayed it in his home as a masterpiece rather than a reference document. Word spread, and by age twenty-four, she had a waiting list of commissions.

**Current Situation**

Pallàvi now stands at a fascinating inflection point in her career. She is recognized as one of the most talented scribes in Dharmapur, with commissions from the temples of Vyālendra, wealthy collectors, and even minor nobility. However, success has brought constraints. The guild's masters increasingly press her to teach the traditional techniques to younger scribes, time she resents because it takes her away from her own creative work. Additionally, her reputation for innovation has attracted the attention of scholars and philosophers who wish to commission ambitious projects: an illustrated encyclopedia of natural philosophy, a comprehensive genealogy of the noble houses rendered in elaborate illumination, even a proposed translation of an ancient religious text from a neighboring realm. These projects call to her artistic ambition, but completing any one of them would require abandoning her current commissions and potentially breaching her contracts with the guild.

## Psyche

### Personality

Pallàvi is possessed of a restless, creative energy that makes sitting still for extended periods a form of meditation rather than constraint. Her mind moves rapidly from thought to thought, idea to idea, and she struggles with the linear, repetitive work that her craft also demands. She is intensely passionate about aesthetics and meaning, capable of passionate discussion about the philosophical implications of a letter's form or the emotional resonance of a color choice. To those who share her interests, she is engaging and warm. To those who find her preoccupations frivolous, she can seem scattered and dismissive.

Pallàvi has a complicated relationship with authority. She respects genuine skill and artistic vision but chafes at arbitrary rules and tradition for its own sake. She is capable of meticulous technical discipline when working on a project that engages her intellectually, but she becomes almost rebellious when asked to work in styles she considers outdated or aesthetically inferior. This combination of passion and resistance to authority has earned her both devoted admirers and critics within the guild. She is loyal to those she cares about and has a gift for making others feel that their commissions—and by extension, their stories and ideas—matter deeply to her.

### Motivation

Pallàvi's primary drive is the integration of beauty and meaning. She believes that every text deserves to be rendered with the care and artistry that its ideas demand, and she sees her role as a scribe not as merely transcribing but as interpreting and honoring the ideas she transcribes. She seeks creative recognition—to be remembered not as a competent technician but as an innovative artist who expanded the possibilities of her craft. She is also motivated by a desire to help others communicate their most important thoughts clearly and beautifully. There is a spiritual dimension to her motivation: she worships Vyālendra, the creator god, and she sees her work as a form of prayer, a way of honoring the creative impulse that she believes all sentient beings possess.

### Strengths

- **Visionary Artistic Technique**: Pallàvi's extraordinary creativity (18) and drawing skill (17 across 5 levels) allow her to create calligraphic work that is simultaneously technically perfect and artistically innovative. Her work transcends mere transcription to become interpretation and art.

- **Versatility in Scripts and Styles**: Her mastery of multiple languages and scripts (5 levels each in Language Two, Script Two, Script Three, and 5 levels in Native Language Script) means she can work in diverse traditions and can create seamless fusions of styles.

- **Understanding of Color and Harmony**: Her background in multiple crafts—ceramics, drawing, singing—combined with her high dexterity (16) and perception (15) gives her an intuitive sense of visual and aesthetic harmony that transcends mere technical skill.

- **Meticulous Precision**: When focused, her dexterity and mathematical reasoning (16) allow her to create work of extraordinary technical precision. Her mathematical skill also helps her understand proportion and spacing at a deep level.

- **Intellectual Depth**: Her ability to understand and interpret the philosophical and literary content of texts she transcribes means her work goes beyond copying to genuine artistic interpretation.

### Weaknesses

- **Distraction and Inconsistent Focus**: Pallàvi's high creativity and quick-moving mind mean she struggles with monotonous work. Projects that require extensive repetition of the same task—copying multiple pages in a simple style, transcribing dry reference materials—bore her to the point of distraction, and her work quality can suffer.

- **Low Empathy and Social Difficulty**: Her relatively low empathy (10) means she sometimes fails to understand how others perceive her or her work. She can be insensitive to patrons' actual needs, focused instead on her own artistic vision. She also struggles to navigate guild politics and interpersonal conflict.

- **Resistance to Authority and Tradition**: Pallàvi's willfulness and tendency to resist rules make her difficult to work with when others demand adherence to traditional or conservative approaches. This has created tension with the guild's more traditional masters.

- **Limited Practical Capabilities**: Her low strength (10) means she cannot perform demanding physical labor. She would struggle with any work requiring significant physical exertion or combat capability.

- **Vulnerability to Impulsive Decision-Making**: Her low will (11) suggests she can be swayed by enthusiasm or passion, potentially leading her to accept projects she cannot complete or to act without adequate forethought.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Temple of Vyālendra**: The priesthood of her patron deity regularly commissions her to create sacred texts and illuminated prayers. They trust her spiritual understanding and have made her something of an unofficial artist-in-residence.

- **The Merchants' Cooperative**: Wealthy merchants regularly commission elaborate documents from her—contracts rendered beautifully, ledgers transformed into works of art, gifts to business partners that demonstrate their refinement and taste.

- **Master Keśava**: Though now very elderly and no longer accepting commissions, her former teacher remains her most trusted advisor and occasional patron. He occasionally requests that she copy texts of personal spiritual significance.

- **The Guild of Scribes**: The guild leadership, despite their sometimes fraught relationship with her, regularly commissions official documents, certificates of apprenticeship, and formal records that require her distinctive touch.

### Enemies

- **Master Yajña, Conservative Guild Elder**: A powerful senior figure in the Scribes' Guild, Yajña represents the traditionalist faction. He views Pallàvi's innovations as disrespectful to the classical traditions and has begun to publicly criticize her work as "frivolous decoration" that obscures rather than serves the text. He has subtle support from other conservatives, and there is quiet conflict over whether the guild should encourage innovation or preserve traditional forms.

### Affiliations

- **The Scribes' Guild of Dharmapur**: Pallàvi is a full member of the guild and holds certain official responsibilities, though her relationship with the organization is complex due to her innovative approaches and the conservative faction's criticism.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Forbidden Text**: A mysterious scholar approaches Pallàvi with an extraordinary commission: to create an illuminated copy of a text that has been suppressed by religious authorities. The text is a philosophical work that challenges certain orthodox interpretations of Vyālendra's nature and teachings. The scholar claims that only Pallàvi's combination of artistic vision and spiritual understanding can create a version worthy of the ideas. The commission is illegal, dangerous—possession of the text could result in censure by the priesthood—and lucrative beyond measure. Pallàvi becomes involved in a complex moral and political situation involving religious authority, freedom of thought, and the ethics of her craft. The party might help her acquire the original text, protect her from religious authorities who learn of the commission, negotiate with the temple to find a compromise that allows philosophical discussion, or help her decide whether artistic principle justifies the legal and spiritual risk.

2. **The Guild's Choice**: Internal conflict within the Scribes' Guild intensifies when the leadership is forced to choose between supporting the conservative faction and the innovators. They approach Pallàvi with an ultimatum: she can demonstrate that her innovations honor rather than mock the classical traditions by creating a masterwork that synthesizes ancient and modern styles, or she will be expelled from the guild. The project is extraordinarily challenging and requires her to research ancient texts, consult with historians and philosophers, and undertake a spiritual journey to understand the deeper principles underlying the traditions she seeks to innovate upon. The party might help her research, face opposition from Master Yajña and his allies who deliberately attempt to sabotage her project, or join her in presenting the finished work to the guild leadership in a formal proceeding.

3. **The Collector's Obsession**: An eccentric nobleman from a distant land, a collector of rare manuscripts and artistic works, becomes obsessed with acquiring original works by Pallàvi. He begins purchasing her commissioned pieces from their original patrons, often at premium prices, and eventually approaches Pallàvi directly to negotiate an exclusive contract that would make her his personal scribe. The offer is flattering and financially tempting, but it would isolate her from the broader community and from the meaningful connections she forms with diverse patrons. Furthermore, there are hints that the collector's motivations may not be purely aesthetic—that he may be interested in her work for magical, political, or esoteric reasons. The party might help her investigate the collector's true motivations, negotiate terms that preserve her independence, or alternatively, decide whether accepting his patronage would represent artistic betrayal or a legitimate pursuit of greater resources.

4. **The Lost Library's Tragedy**: Pallàvi is commissioned by a group of scholars to help preserve texts from an ancient library that is threatened with destruction due to coastal erosion or political instability. She becomes deeply invested in the project, working alongside the scholars to understand the texts and render the most important ones in new copies created with her finest technique. However, during the preservation effort, the party discovers evidence of a cover-up: someone intentionally caused or accelerated the library's destruction to suppress certain texts or hide secrets contained within them. The party becomes involved in uncovering the conspiracy, protecting the remaining texts, and helping Pallàvi decide whether to publicize her findings at the risk of repercussions from those who wished the library destroyed.

5. **The Living Text**: While working on an unusually complex illuminated manuscript, Pallàvi begins to suspect that the text contains something more than mere words and decoration—that her artistic rendering might somehow be activating hidden meanings, esoteric knowledge, or magical properties within the work. The original text was created by an ancient scholar whose reputation included rumored studies of the intersection between language and magic. Pallàvi becomes obsessed with understanding the work's true nature, consulting with philosophers, mages, and historians. The party might help her research the original author, test whether her theories about the text's nature are correct, navigate encounters with those who want the text's knowledge suppressed, or grapple with the philosophical implications if a work of art can literally alter reality through the beauty of its presentation.

6. **The Scribe's Apprentice**: A talented young apprentice in the guild shows unusual promise and gravitates toward Pallàvi as a mentor. However, the apprentice begins to obsessively copy Pallàvi's style and techniques to the point of producing forgeries of her work. When the forgeries are discovered and sold to unsuspecting patrons, Pallàvi's reputation and the guild's credibility are damaged. The situation is complicated by her genuine affection for the apprentice and her understanding of the creative hunger that drives the young person. The party becomes involved in exposing the forgery ring, protecting Pallàvi's reputation, and ultimately deciding whether the apprentice deserves redemption or harsh punishment.
