# Appearance {#appearance}

Padmàvali stands 5'2" tall with a light build. She has warm caramel skin, dark black hair, and deep brown eyes. Her features include an oval face, a delicate nose, a soft jawline, well-shaped lips, deep brows, full cheeks.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Early Life and Artistic Awakening**

Padmàvali was born into the merchant family Dhanurvedakīrtirāja in the city-state of Saṁgha, one of the largest trading hubs of Vedyara. Her father, a successful spice merchant, had little time for his bookish, art-obsessed daughter, but her mother recognized her exceptional talent early. By age seven, Padmàvali could sketch the architectural features of buildings with stunning precision, and by twelve, she had begun studying the mathematical principles underlying perspective and proportion. Her formal training came under **Master Ananda**, a legendary cartographer whose maps were treasured by merchants, military commanders, and noble houses throughout the city-states. Ananda recognized in her young student a rare combination: the precision and mathematical rigor required for accurate mapmaking, paired with an intuitive artistic sense that transformed functional maps into works of sublime beauty.

**Journey to Mastery**

Padmàvali spent her twenties undertaking commissions for increasingly important patrons: merchants seeking detailed maps of trade routes, explorers venturing into uncharted territories, and nobles desiring illuminated maps of their domains. She developed her distinctive style—cartographic precision rendered with watercolor washes that captured the essence of a place's landscape and character. Her maps were not merely accurate; they told stories. A desert region would be rendered in warm ochres and sandy tones, with illustrations of the rare water sources marked not just functionally but artistically. Coastal territories were depicted with wave patterns that suggested both the mathematical precision of currents and the living movement of the sea. Her reputation grew steadily, and by age thirty, she had been commissioned for a prestigious project: the creation of a comprehensive map of all five city-states, a work intended to celebrate the centennial of their foundational trade alliance.

**Current Situation**

Padmàvali stands at a crossroads of opportunity and vulnerability. Her commission to create the Great Map has consumed her for two years, and its impending completion promises to establish her as one of the most accomplished cartographers of the age. However, the work has made her nervous. The project's patronage comes from a coalition of merchant lords, each with political interests in how their city-state is represented. She has begun to sense subtle pressure from her patrons to "adjust" boundaries, emphasize certain trade routes, or downplay the territories of rival city-states. Her innocence regarding political power—her belief that cartography should reflect truth rather than serve propaganda—has begun to collide with the realities of patronage. Additionally, she has been approached by scholars and explorers seeking her to undertake dangerous expeditions to uncharted lands, offers that terrify and tempt her in equal measure.

## Psyche

### Personality

Padmàvali is an intensely imaginative soul, prone to losing herself in her work for days at a time, forgetting to eat or sleep. She speaks quietly and deliberately, choosing her words with an artist's care, and she has little patience for small talk or social convention. Her mind moves constantly, analyzing spatial relationships, color harmonies, and the mathematical patterns underlying natural phenomena. She is genuinely curious about people, places, and systems, though her curiosity is often abstract rather than personal—she is more interested in understanding how a city functions as a whole than in the gossip of its inhabitants.

Despite her talent, Padmàvali harbors deep self-doubt. She questions whether her artistic choices are merely indulgent decoration or genuine contributions to her craft. She worries constantly about making mistakes that could mislead travelers or explorers. This anxiety, combined with her naïveté regarding human nature and power, makes her vulnerable to manipulation by those with stronger personalities and clearer ambitions. She can be stubborn when defending her artistic vision, but she lacks confidence in other domains of life.

### Motivation

Padmàvali is driven by the pursuit of truth and beauty working in harmony. She believes that maps are sacred documents—that they represent humanity's relationship with the world and should reflect reality as faithfully as possible. Her deepest motivation is to create works that will endure, that will help people understand their world, and that might prevent suffering caused by ignorance or misdirection. She also secretly harbors a romantic notion of exploration and discovery; part of her dreams of abandoning her workshop to travel to unmapped lands, to see extraordinary places firsthand, and to capture them in her art. She seeks validation—not wealth or fame, but recognition that her work matters and that her artistic choices were right.

### Strengths

- **Extraordinary Cartographic Precision**: Padmàvali can render maps of stunning accuracy, capturing spatial relationships, distances, and geographical features with mathematical precision. Her knowledge of projection, scale, and the translation of three-dimensional terrain into two-dimensional representation is nearly comprehensive.

- **Masterful Artistic Technique**: Her high scores in drawing, theatrics, and related arts mean that her maps transcend functionality to become visual art. She can render landscapes, architecture, and decorative elements with beautiful stylization that enhances without distorting information.

- **Mathematical and Analytical Mind**: Her reasoning (13) and mathematics (15) combine to allow her to solve complex problems of representation and to understand systems and structures intuitively.

- **Meticulous Attention to Detail**: Her perception (14) and the sheer volume of her skill training in drawing (5 levels!) mean she notices subtleties others miss and can translate these into her work.

- **Physical Capability and Resilience**: Her dexterity (15), climbing (14 across 5 levels), and related physical skills suggest she is more capable of fieldwork than her delicate appearance suggests. She can navigate difficult terrain and gather firsthand data.

### Weaknesses

- **Naive Regarding Power and Politics**: Padmàvali genuinely believes that truth and objectivity matter more than political interests. She is shockingly unprepared for patrons who demand that she falsify or manipulate her maps for political gain, and she struggles to navigate situations where her integrity is in conflict with her patrons' demands.

- **Anxiety and Self-Doubt**: Her relatively low will (11) and the intense pressure of her current commission have left her emotionally fragile. She is prone to spiraling self-doubt and becomes paralyzed when uncertain about the rightness of her choices.

- **Physical Frailty**: Her low strength (8) and moderate endurance (12) mean she cannot perform demanding physical labor, carry heavy equipment, or defend herself effectively in combat or survival situations.

- **Socially Awkward and Withdrawn**: Her low charm (14, not exceptional) and tendency to disappear into her work make her poor at maintaining relationships or navigating social situations. She can be perceived as distant or even cold by those who don't understand her preoccupation.

- **Limited Practical Experience Outside Her Craft**: While talented in her field, Padmàvali has little experience with the practical necessities of survival, commerce, or conflict. She would be entirely dependent on companions for protection and basic survival needs.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Five City-States Merchant Coalition**: Collectively, the merchant lords of the five city-states have commissioned her to create the Great Map, a comprehensive and politically significant project that has both elevated her status and exposed her to their conflicting interests.

- **Master Ananda**: Her former teacher, now retired to a house in the countryside, remains her most trusted advisor and occasional patron. He commissions small personal maps and serves as a source of wisdom about the ethics of cartography.

- **House of Saṁgha**: The leading merchant family of her native city continues to patronize her work and has offered her a permanent workshop in exchange for priority commissions.

### Enemies

- **None Named, But Tensions Exist**: Various political factions within the city-states view her with suspicion, each believing she might be secretly aligned with their rivals. She has not yet made true enemies, but her refusal to manipulate her maps for political advantage is beginning to create tensions.

### Affiliations

- **Independent Cartographers' Fellowship**: Padmàvali is a member of an informal but respected network of master cartographers who maintain high standards for accuracy and integrity. The Fellowship occasionally commissions collaborative works and provides moral support.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Great Map's Secret Demand**: As Padmàvali nears completion of the Great Map, the political pressure on her intensifies. A faction of merchant lords—those whose city-states have diminished economically in recent years—secretly approaches her and demands that she adjust the map to make their territories appear larger, more strategic, or more wealthy. They offer enormous payment and threaten financial ruin if she refuses. Padmàvali, caught between her artistic integrity and her economic dependence on these patrons, turns to the party for help. The characters might investigate the political situation, work to broker a compromise that preserves the map's integrity, gather evidence of the attempted bribery to expose the lords publicly, or help Padmàvali secure alternative patronage that would free her from this coercion. The situation escalates when she discovers that one of the merchant lords has already secretly attempted to alter the nearly-finished map themselves.

2. **The Expedition to Uncharted Lands**: An explorer and scholar named **Captain Vihara**, whose reputation is both legendary and somewhat disreputable, approaches Padmàvali with an audacious proposal. She is organizing an expedition to a rumored lost kingdom in the distant jungles beyond the southern border, a place that, if it exists, has never been mapped by anyone from the city-states. Vihara wants Padmàvali to accompany the expedition as the official cartographer and historian. The pay is exceptional, and the opportunity to create the first accurate maps of a completely new region is every cartographer's dream—but the expedition is dangerous, involving travel through disease-ridden lands, encounters with hostile peoples, and the high likelihood of getting hopelessly lost. Padmàvali is terrified but tempted. The party might join the expedition as protection, help her prepare physically and mentally, debate with her about the risks and rewards, or alternatively, help her research the legend of the lost kingdom to determine whether the expedition is worthwhile or merely a dangerous waste of resources.

3. **The Map That Predicts Disaster**: While working on a commission to map the agricultural lands surrounding the city of Saṁgha, Padmàvali notices a pattern in the geology, water systems, and seasonal flood records that suggests a catastrophic flood is likely within the next year or two. The patterns are subtle enough that she alone, with her extraordinary perception and mathematical mind, has noticed them. Sharing her findings with the city authorities would warn the population but would also publicly acknowledge a risk that could destabilize the economy and cause panic. The merchant lords, hearing whispers of her discovery, try to suppress her warning to protect their interests. The party becomes involved in helping Padmàvali decide whether to sound the alarm despite political pressure, gather more evidence to make the warning undeniable, or help evacuate vulnerable populations in secret if the authorities refuse to act on her warnings.

4. **The Cartography of the Soul**: Padmàvali is contacted by a mysterious scholar and priest of Mahájaya, the god of order, who claims that her maps are not merely representations of physical geography but are somehow spiritually significant. The priest believes that Padmàvali possesses an innate connection to the god's order-bringing power, and he invites her to use her cartographic skills in service of a spiritual mission: to map the invisible boundaries of sacred sites, magical ley lines, and places of spiritual power across the land. The priest offers her resources, protection, and partnership—but also hints that the knowledge she might gain could be dangerous or transformative. The party must help Padmàvali explore whether this invitation is genuine spiritual calling, manipulation, or something stranger still. The investigation might involve traveling to purported sacred sites to verify the priest's claims, uncovering the priest's true motivations and connections, or discovering that the "spiritual maps" she is being asked to create have hidden practical applications in warfare, politics, or the control of magical resources.

5. **The Stolen Masterwork**: Padmàvali's nearly-completed Great Map is stolen from her workshop in a sophisticated heist. Initially, she fears the worst—that a rival cartographer or political faction has stolen it to alter or destroy it. However, the truth is stranger: a collector of rare artworks and maps, an eccentric nobleman from a distant realm, has acquired the map through thieves because he considers it the finest work of its kind and wishes to add it to his private collection. He has no intention of returning it. The party must track the thieves to recover the map, potentially infiltrate the nobleman's heavily-guarded estate, and negotiate with him—all while protecting the map from further damage. In the process, they discover that the nobleman actually has access to information about even older, lost maps from ancient civilizations, and he might be convinced to share this knowledge if the party approaches him with the right appeal.

6. **The Anomaly**: While researching previous maps in an archive, Padmàvali discovers something impossible: two maps created five hundred years apart, by different cartographers working with different tools and in different cities, both show a specific geographical feature—a landmark, river bend, or mountain pass—in slightly different locations, as if the geography itself has shifted. She brings this discovery to Master Ananda, who confirms that ancient texts reference similar anomalies in other locations. Together, they hypothesize that either ancient cartographers were occasionally inaccurate (threatening her faith in her craft) or something far stranger has occurred: the land itself has shifted, moved, or been magically altered over the centuries. The party becomes involved in investigating this mystery, potentially traveling to the sites of the anomalies to gather data, consulting with historians and mages, and uncovering evidence of ancient magical cataclysms or ongoing subtle transformations of the world. The investigation might reveal that the anomalies are expanding or accelerating, suggesting danger to inhabited lands.
